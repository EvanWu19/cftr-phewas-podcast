Ep4 (Track C — registry QC, 2 of 2): The hidden kurtosis assumption under every LMS reference curve

Rigby and Stasinopoulos (2004, Statistics in Medicine) introduce the Box-Cox power exponential (BCPE) distribution, generalizing the standard LMS method that underlies CDC and WHO growth-chart centile curves: where LMS fits three parameters (skewness, median, coefficient of variation) and assumes the residual distribution is exactly normal-shaped, BCPE adds a fourth parameter, tau, that explicitly models kurtosis, letting the reference curve be leptokurtic or platykurtic rather than forced into a normal shape. The generalization is named LMSP. Note: this paper is closed-access with no PMCID — only the abstract was available, flagged on-air; no specific numeric results from the Dutch-BMI demonstration could be verified. The transfer is direct: any z-score-based flagging threshold inherits whatever tail-shape assumption its underlying reference curve makes, and a mismatched kurtosis assumption miscalibrates exactly the tail probabilities flagging decisions depend on. Buildable artifact: a GAMLSS Kurtosis Diagnostic.

Plain-English glossary

  • BCPE (Box-Cox power exponential) distribution — a four-parameter distribution (location, scale, skewness, kurtosis) generalizing the normal distribution to model both skewed and kurtotic data.（中文：一种四参数分布（位置、尺度、偏斜度、峰度），将正态分布推广以建模偏斜和尖峰/低峰态数据。）
  • CDC growth charts / WHO growth standards — two reference-curve systems for paediatric anthropometry, both built using LMS-family methods but differing in source population and intent (CDC: US reference sample; WHO: international healthy-breastfed-infant sample describing how children should grow).（中文：两套儿科人体测量参考曲线体系，底层均采用LMS系方法构建，但来源人群和目的不同——CDC基于美国参考样本，WHO基于国际健康母乳喂养婴儿样本，旨在描述儿童"应该"如何生长。）
  • Kurtosis — a distribution-shape property describing tail heaviness; leptokurtic (尖峰态) means heavier tails and more extreme values than normal, platykurtic (低峰态) means lighter tails and fewer extreme values.（中文：描述分布尾部厚重程度的性质；尖峰态指尾部更重、极端值更多；低峰态指尾部更轻、极端值更少。）
  • LMS method — the standard method for building growth-chart centile curves: three smoothly age-varying parameters (L: Box-Cox skewness power, M: median, S: coefficient of variation) converting a measurement to a z-score on a transformed, assumed-normal scale.（中文：构建生长曲线百分位曲线的标准方法：三个随年龄平滑变化的参数（L：Box-Cox偏斜幂次；M：中位数；S：变异系数），将测量值转换为假定正态尺度上的z分数。）
  • LMSP method — this paper's generalization of LMS, adding a fourth power-exponential parameter to also model kurtosis.（中文：本文对LMS方法的推广，增加第四个幂指数参数以同时建模峰度。）

  • BCPE / LMSP — the four-parameter distribution and fitting method this paper introduces, implemented today in the gamlss R package's BCPE family.
  • Fisher scoring algorithm — the penalized-likelihood maximization approach used to fit all four smoothly age-varying parameters; the paper supplies the needed derivatives.

ALEX And that assumption matters enormously the moment you're using a z-score to decide whether a value is implausible. 而这个假设恰恰在你用z分数来判断某个数值是否不合理的那一刻变得至关重要。 If the real population's tails are heavier than normal — leptokurtic — but your LMS-based reference curve assumes normal tails, then a measured value that LMS calls "extremely rare, three standard deviations out" might actually be a fairly ordinary occurrence in that real, heavier-tailed population, and vice versa for a platykurtic population where LMS would understate how rare an extreme value really is. 如果真实人群的尾部比正态分布更重——也就是尖峰态——但你基于LMS的参考曲线却假定尾部是正态的，那么一个被LMS判定为"极为罕见，偏离三个标准差"的测量值，在那个真实的、尾部更重的人群中可能其实相当常见；反过来，对于一个低峰态的人群，LMS则会低估一个极端值实际有多罕见。 Any flagging rule built on top of a z-score threshold inherits whatever tail-shape error is baked into the reference curve underneath it, and that's exactly the gap this paper is built to close. 任何建立在z分数阈值之上的标记规则，都会继承其底层参考曲线所带有的尾部形状误差，而这正是这篇论文想要弥合的那个缺口。
