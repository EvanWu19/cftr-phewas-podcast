Blurb

Karen Raraigh and colleagues (senior author Garry Cutting; J Cyst Fibros 2022) performed complete CFTR gene sequencing — whole-genome-based, introns and all — on 5,058 individuals with cystic fibrosis, essentially the JHU/UNC cohort that anchors the CF Foundation's WGS data. Working through three phases (known + structural variants → novel loss-of-function → remaining variants) and interpreting with CFTR2 and ACMG criteria, they identified both causative variants in 98.1% (n=4,960), up 2.3 points from the 95.8% solved by registry/chart genotypes. They formally interpreted 371 variants (16 [4.3%] previously unreported), characterized 18 structural variants (2 novel) invisible to coding-only tests, and concluded that 99.6% of the cohort carries ≥1 variant targetable by current or emerging gene-based therapies. The proposed direction for Runyu: use this complete-CFTR-genotype framework — structural and intronic variants included — as the CFTR-status backbone for any modifier/PheWAS analysis. Plain-English glossary

  • ACMG criteria — the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics' standardized rulebook for classifying any variant (pathogenic / likely pathogenic / uncertain / benign); makes calls reproducible.
  • CFTR2 — expert CF-specific variant database used for interpretation.
  • Coding region (exons) — the DNA stretches translated into protein, where most CF-causing variants live; the usual sequencing shortcut.
  • Complete gene sequencing — reading the whole gene (exons + introns), not just coding regions; here via WGS.
  • Compound heterozygote — a person with two different CF-causing variants, one per copy.
  • F508del homozygote — both gene copies carry the same most-common CF mutation; the most "solved" genotype on paper.
  • Gene-based therapy (emerging) — treatments that repair/replace the faulty DNA or its message directly (e.g. gene editing, RNA therapeutics); generally need the exact defect known.
  • Intron — non-coding DNA between exons, spliced out of the final message but where variants can still disrupt the gene.
  • Loss-of-function (LoF) variant — a mutation that clearly breaks the protein (premature stop, frameshift); high-confidence damaging.
  • Recessive — disease requires two broken copies; an "unsolved" genotype means the second hit is hidden.
  • Structural variant — a large-scale DNA change (big deletion/insertion), easily missed by short coding-region tests.
  • Variant-specific treatment — therapy matched to a patient's exact CFTR variant; requires a fully defined genotype.
  • VCC (varying clinical consequences) — a variant whose CF impact depends on context/co-variant.
  • WGS (whole-genome sequencing) — reading essentially the entire genome base by base.
