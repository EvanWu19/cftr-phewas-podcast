Ep3 (Track C, paper 1) — 2026-08-28

Multi-source harmonization added 44% coverage for kids — but nobody validated the cleaning rules

Childs and colleagues (BMJ Open, 2025) built a 23-year, four-source BMI harmonization pipeline out of Wales's SAIL Databank — linking primary care, hospital, maternity, and child community-health records into a single research-ready data asset covering 5.1 million people. The five-stage pipeline extracts and bounds-filters BMI values (removing values below 12 or above 100), attaches demographics, calculates age-matched BMI (height-weight pairing within 180 days for children; most-recent-height carry-forward for adults), flags same-day inconsistencies (category jumps or >5% value disagreement), and picks one entry per person per day via a source-reliability hierarchy. Adding non-primary-care sources boosted unique BMI coverage by 44% for children (driven mostly by child community-health data) but under 2% for adults. The paper is candid that it never validated its own cleaning rules against ground truth — it measures coverage, not accuracy — which is the central gap the next episode's paper actually addresses.

Plain-English glossary

  • TRE (trusted research environment) — a secure system, like Wales's SAIL Databank, where researchers run code against linked population-level health data without ever downloading or seeing identifiable records.（中文：可信研究环境，例如威尔士的SAIL数据库，研究者在其中对关联的人群级健康数据运行代码，而无需下载或接触可识别的记录。）
  • RRDA (research-ready data asset) — a pre-cleaned, harmonized longitudinal dataset built once and reused across multiple research projects.（中文：预先清洗、协调好的纵向数据集，构建一次即可供多个研究项目复用。）
  • Source-reliability hierarchy — a ranking of which data source to trust when multiple sources report conflicting values for the same day; this paper ranked primary-care BMI values highest.（中文：当多个数据源在同一天报告相互冲突的数值时，用于决定信任哪个来源的排序规则；本文将初级保健的BMI数值排在最高优先级。）
  • Carry-forward (measurement) — reusing the most recent recorded value (e.g., adult height) across a long time window rather than re-measuring; a known source of error since adults' height decreases with age.（中文：在较长的时间窗口内沿用最近一次记录的数值（例如成人身高），而不是重新测量；由于成人身高会随年龄增长而降低，这是一个已知的误差来源。）
  • CDC vs WHO growth references — two different age/sex-specific growth-curve standards; WHO standards (built from breastfed infants across countries) are generally preferred under age 2, CDC references (built from observed US national samples) are more common for older children.（中文：两套不同的按年龄/性别划分的生长曲线标准；WHO标准（基于多国母乳喂养婴儿数据构建）通常更适用于两岁以下儿童，CDC标准（基于美国全国样本实际观察数据构建）则更常用于年龄较大的儿童。）
  • Informative missingness — when data is more likely to be missing for certain kinds of people (e.g., healthier, male, lower healthcare utilization) rather than missing at random, biasing any analysis that ignores it.（中文：数据缺失并非随机发生，而是更可能发生在某些特定人群身上（例如更健康、男性、医疗资源使用较少的人），若分析中忽略这一点，会导致结果产生偏倚。）
