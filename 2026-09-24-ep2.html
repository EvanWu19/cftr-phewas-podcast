Ep 10 — When the outcome is not there: NRI, odds-ratio tilts, and reference-based imputation

Arc day 3, episode 10 of 16. CorEvitas PV/PASS interview prep, for 2026-09-25.

Blurb

Two sources, one idea. The CorEvitas item is ISPE 2025 poster A-177 (McLean, Singleton, Pugach, Chen, Middaugh, Litman, Harrold), which takes 407 psoriasis-registry patients who initiated a biologic, of whom 341 (84%) have an observed six-month PASI outcome, and shows what happens when you stop treating non-responder imputation as a neutral default. The observed response rate is 49.3%; NRI reports 41.3%; tilting the odds of response among the 66 missing patients by 1.5 and 3.0 gives 50.9% and 53.4%. The foundational paper is Cro, Roger and Carpenter (Pharm Stat 2024), retrieved in full text, which builds the trial-side version of the same move: a reparameterisation that writes a retrieved-dropout compliance model as a core reference-based model (J2R, CIR, CR) plus zero-centred deviation parameters, so the analysis degrades gracefully to reference-based imputation when off-treatment data run out. What this episode arms Runyu with is the ability to say, precisely and with the arithmetic in hand, that a missing-outcome convention is an assumption, that assumptions can be made tunable and arguable, and that the direction of the bias reverses when the endpoint is safety rather than effectiveness.

Plain-English glossary

Biologic (psoriasis) — An injected or infused therapy targeting a specific immune pathway (TNF, IL-17A, IL-23) rather than broad immunosuppression; the exposure class in the CorEvitas poster.（中文：生物制剂，针对特定免疫通路的注射类银屑病治疗药物。）

Delta adjustment — A sensitivity analysis in which the assumed distribution of the unobserved values is shifted from the observed by a stated amount (the delta), and the effect on the conclusion is reported; the poster's odds-ratio tilt is a delta adjustment on the log-odds scale.（中文：delta 调整，把缺失值的假设分布相对观测值平移一个明确设定的量，再报告结论如何变化。）

Deviation — Cro, Roger and Carpenter's term throughout for the intercurrent event of withdrawal from randomised treatment, following Carpenter, Roger and Kenward.（中文：偏离，该文全篇用来指"停用随机分配治疗"这一伴发事件。）

Estimand — The precise quantity being estimated, specified by population, variable, summary measure, and the strategy for each intercurrent event.（中文：估计目标，由人群、变量、汇总指标以及伴发事件处理策略共同精确定义的量。）

ICH E9(R1) Addendum — The 2019 regulatory addendum that introduced the estimand framework and the named strategies (treatment policy, hypothetical, composite, while-on-treatment, principal stratum) for handling intercurrent events.（中文：ICH E9(R1) 附录，2019 年引入估计目标框架及伴发事件处理策略的监管文件。）

Information-anchored inference — The criterion that the information lost because of missing data, under your chosen missingness assumption, should be about the same as the information that would be lost under a missing-at-random analysis.（中文：信息锚定推断，要求在所选缺失假设下因缺失损失的信息量，与 MAR 分析下的损失量大致相当。）

Intercurrent event — An event after baseline that changes how the outcome should be interpreted, or whether it exists at all: treatment withdrawal, rescue medication, death.（中文：伴发事件，基线后发生并改变结局解释或结局是否存在的事件。）

Non-responder imputation (NRI) — Assigning non-response to every patient with a missing binary outcome. The convention for binary endpoints in registration work; equivalent to a response probability of zero among the unobserved.（中文：无应答填补，把所有结局缺失的患者一律记为无应答。）

PASI — Psoriasis Area and Severity Index, 0 to 72, higher is worse. The poster's response definition is PASI at or below 2 at six months, which is near-clear skin.（中文：银屑病面积与严重程度指数，0–72 分，分数越高病情越重。）

Perforated data — Cro's term for a data structure in which some arm-by-deviation-visit stratum has no observed off-treatment data at all, so an imputation-model parameter is not estimable.（中文：穿孔型数据，某个"治疗组×偏离访视"层完全没有停药后观测数据，导致填补模型参数不可估。）

Plaque psoriasis — The most common form of psoriasis, presenting as demarcated scaly plaques; the poster's indication.（中文：斑块型银屑病，最常见的银屑病类型，表现为边界清楚的鳞屑性斑块。）

Retrieved dropout — A patient who deviated from randomised treatment but whose outcome data were still collected afterwards; the observed off-treatment data that make a compliance model estimable.（中文：回收失访者，已停用随机治疗但仍继续被随访并采集到结局数据的患者。）

Single-arm effectiveness study — A study with no comparator arm, assessing what fraction of patients responded within a defined follow-up window.（中文：单臂有效性研究，无对照组，只评估在规定随访窗内达到应答的比例。）

Treatment-policy strategy — The ICH E9(R1) strategy that keeps the outcome regardless of whether treatment continued, the estimand-language counterpart of intention to treat; it obliges the study to collect outcome data after people stop.（中文：治疗策略法，无论治疗是否继续都保留结局，相当于估计目标语言里的 ITT，要求停药后仍继续采集结局。）

Question for her

"In the psoriasis registry missing-outcome work the tilt on unobserved responses ran upward only, which makes sense for an effectiveness endpoint. For a safety endpoint in a PASS, where imputing 'no event' to everyone lost to follow-up understates harm, does the team have a standing convention for which direction the sensitivity analysis runs — and is that pre-specified in the protocol, or negotiated with the regulator?"

What to verify first

  • Whether the odds ratios of 1.5 and 3.0 were pre-specified, or chosen after the NRI result was seen. A sensitivity range selected after the fact is a different object from a pre-specified one.
  • Whether a downward tilt (odds ratio below 1, the "sicker patients stop attending" story) was run and not reported. NRI is effectively an odds ratio of zero and the observed rate an odds ratio of one, so the interval between them is unexplored in what is shown.
  • How imputation uncertainty propagated into the reported confidence intervals. Checkable from the poster's own numbers: every interval on the full N of 407 reproduces almost exactly as an ordinary binomial interval, and the three of them are about 9.5 to 9.6 percentage points wide, while the observed-only interval on 341 is 10.6 wide. The intervals narrowed after imputation, which is what would be seen if imputed responders were counted as observed with no between-imputation variance added. The abstract does not state the variance method, so this is a question, not a finding.
  • What the team does about missing follow-up for safety outcomes — malignancy excluding NMSC, NMSC, MACE, VTE, serious infection, opportunistic infection, herpes zoster — where imputing "no event" understates harm. The poster is explicitly an effectiveness study and does not address this; the transfer is ours.
  • What outcome completeness looks like at the horizons a PASS actually runs to. 84% at six months in a psoriasis registry is good; the corresponding figure at five years in a malignancy study will not be 84%, and Cro's own analytic results say the assumption only drives the answer when both the deviation rate and the missing fraction among deviators are large.
  • Following Lee et al. 2023: what the assumed structure of non-attendance is — in particular whether the outcome itself drives the missed visit — and what that implies for whether the response rate is recoverable from the observed data at all, or whether a delta term is unavoidable.

ALEX Two sources. The CorEvitas item is poster A-177 from the ISPE 41st Annual Meeting, Washington, August 2025, by McLean, Singleton, Pugach and colleagues, on psoriasis registry patients without a six-month follow-up visit. The foundational paper is Cro, Roger and Carpenter in Pharmaceutical Statistics 2024, on retrieved dropout reference-base centred multiple imputation. We have that in full text, so the method beat comes from the paper, not memory.

JORDAN They pair because both are about one move. When an outcome is not there you are asserting something about it whether you write that assertion down or not. Both insist on writing it down. So start me on vocabulary, because some of this is language I have never used in a regulatory sentence.

ALEX A single-arm drug effectiveness study has no comparator: you enrol people starting a drug and ask what fraction responded in a defined window. The class here is biologics for plaque psoriasis. The measure is PASI, the Psoriasis Area and Severity Index, 0 to 72, higher is worse, and response is PASI at or below 2 at six months, near-clear skin. The registry collects at six-month intervals and the qualifying visit is a 5-to-9-month window. Non-responder imputation, NRI, assigns non-response to every patient with a missing outcome. It is the convention for binary endpoints in registration work, and it looks conservative, which is why it survives.

JORDAN Now the trial-side terms Cro uses.

ALEX An estimand is the quantity you are estimating, defined in the ICH E9 R1 addendum by population, variable, summary measure, and handling of intercurrent events. An intercurrent event happens after baseline and changes how the outcome should be read, or whether it exists: withdrawal, rescue medication, death. A treatment-policy strategy keeps the outcome regardless of whether treatment continued, the estimand-language version of intention to treat, and obliges you to collect data after people stop. Cro calls treatment withdrawal deviation. A retrieved dropout is a patient who deviated but whose outcome was still collected. The poster's odds-ratio tilt belongs to the delta adjustment family: shift the assumed distribution of the unobserved by a stated amount.

JORDAN Two terms from the paper I will not recognise.

ALEX Perforated data is Cro's word for a structure where some arm-by-deviation-visit stratum has no observed off-treatment data at all, so a parameter you need for imputation is not estimable. Information-anchored inference is the criterion that the information lost to missing data under your assumption should be about the same as under a missing-at-random analysis.

JORDAN The poster, with real numbers.

ALEX Patients with plaque psoriasis who initiated a biologic between 15 April 2015 and 31 March 2017. 407 included. 341, or 84%, had an observed outcome, so 66 patients, just over 16%, have no qualifying six-month visit. Among the 341, 168 reached PASI at or below 2. That is 49.3%, interval 44.0 to 54.6.

JORDAN And NRI does what.

ALEX Keeps the numerator at 168 and moves the denominator to 407. That is 41.3%, interval 36.6 to 46.1. An 8-point drop, and every bit of it is one assumption: that none of the 66 responded.

JORDAN Which nobody believes.

ALEX Nobody believes it, and that is the poster. NRI is not neutral. It is the strongest available downward assumption, invisible because it is the default. The alternative turns it into a dial. Odds among the observed are 168 over 173, about 0.97. Multiply by an odds ratio of 1.5 and the implied probability among the missing is 59.3%, roughly 39 of the 66, so the total moves to 50.9%, interval 46.1 to 55.7. At 3.0 it is 74.4%, roughly 49 of the 66, total 53.4%, interval 48.5 to 58.1.

JORDAN So the headline is not any single number.

ALEX The headline is the span. 41.3 to 53.4 is 12 percentage points, the honest width of what this study can say when 16% of it is unobserved. Their conclusion is modest, close to verbatim: the approach allows investigators to incorporate subject matter expertise and clinical experience into the specification of assumptions about the responses of patients with missing outcomes in sensitivity analyses. One detail worth carrying: Michael Singleton is listed here as Lead Biostatistician at CorEvitas, the title of the seat. Moore-Clingenpeel is not an author.

JORDAN Now Cro. What problem does it solve.

ALEX In order. First, why a missing outcome after withdrawal is not an ordinary missing covariate. Under a treatment-policy strategy the outcome after someone stops is part of the estimand by construction. Cro says that if all post-deviation data were collected, estimation would be straightforward, ANCOVA on observed data. The difficulty is that it is common for as many as half of those who deviate to have incomplete data afterwards, so the assumption has to be about off-treatment experience specifically.

JORDAN Second, what a plain missing-at-random imputation assumes.

ALEX That conditional on observed data, the people who left behave like the people who stayed in the same arm. Cro's objection is structural. Ignore compliance status and you build an off-treatment prediction out of a mixture of on- and off-treatment data, which is incoherent with a treatment-policy estimand. Their word for it is generally unacceptable.

JORDAN Third, the reference-based family.

ALEX These deliberately break missing at random and write the replacement down. Jump to reference, J2R: the mean profile matches the randomised arm up to deviation, then jumps to the reference arm's profile. Copy increments in reference, CIR: matches up to deviation, then runs parallel to the reference profile, so benefit already banked is kept but no further benefit accrues. Copy reference, CR: the reference profile throughout. Each is a clinical story about what happens after a patient stops, written as a missing-not-at-random assumption a regulator can argue with.

JORDAN Fourth, what these three add.

ALEX Note the title: reference-base centred, not reference-based. Retrieved dropout imputation already existed, as compliance models: current, where an on-off indicator changes visit to visit; historic, conditioning on compliance history so far; full-pattern, stratified by the subject's whole pattern. Those work when off-treatment data are plentiful and fail when the data are perforated, because a stratum with nothing observed leaves a needed parameter unestimable. The contribution is a reparameterisation. Write the compliance model as a core reference-based model plus deviation parameters that are the difference between that model and the core. Fit it as a Bayesian repeated-measures multivariate normal, uninformative priors on the core, mildly informative zero-centred priors on the deviations, then impute and combine with Rubin's rules.

JORDAN The behaviour at the two ends.

ALEX With almost no retrieved off-treatment data the deviation parameters sit near their prior mean of zero and the imputation reduces to the core reference-based model, with a small extra variance for uncertainty about that assumption. With plenty, the likelihood swamps the mild prior and it behaves like the compliance model. It transitions smoothly instead of switching, so one non-adaptive plan can be pre-specified before you know how much off-treatment data you will get, rather than a set of adaptive rules. The core must nest inside the compliance model, and CIR nests only in historic.

JORDAN What do they compare against, and find.

ALEX Two public SWGEMD antidepressant datasets, HAMD17 change at week 6, called covered and perforated, identical where observed. On covered, J2R alone gives 2.18 with standard error 1.13, J2R plus observed off-treatment data gives 2.28 with 1.05, the historic compliance model gives 2.32 with 1.10, and their method at prior variance 40 reproduces that, 2.32 with 1.08. On perforated the existing routes disagree: dropping patient-visits that cannot be imputed gives 2.45 with a standard error of 1.08, a biased mean with a smaller standard error, the wrong direction; zeroing the non-estimable parameter gives 2.44; a broad prior gives 2.75. Theirs gives 2.41 and 2.51.

JORDAN Bias.

ALEX Analytic, not simulation: exact results integrated over deviation and missingness patterns. Unbiased across prior choices in three of four data-generating scenarios. Where the core model is wrong, a more precise prior induces bias, which is the expected and honest result. It only bites when the deviation rate and the missing fraction among deviators are both high.

JORDAN The brief said to expect an information-anchored versus efficiency-anchored contrast.

ALEX The paper does not set it up that way, and I want to be precise. The phrase efficiency anchored does not appear in the text. It cites prior work that reference-based imputation with Rubin's rules gives approximately information-anchored inference, and that delta-based imputation with a fixed delta is information anchored, while a prior on delta adds that prior's variance through the between-imputation term. For their own method it infers, rather than demonstrates, that with little off-treatment data the variance estimator is approximately information anchored plus an increment set by the prior variance, and with plenty it behaves like retrieved dropout imputation. Then it concedes what the efficiency side presses: Rubin's variance estimate in reference-based imputation overestimates the empirical repeated-sampling variance, so type one error sits below 5% under the null. They say a larger simulation study is required, and leave it open.

JORDAN Where do they meet, and where does it strain.

ALEX They are the same move: vary the assumption about the unobserved and report how the conclusion moves. The poster tilts a probability; Cro substitutes a distributional model and softens it with a prior. They meet in intent and separate in formality. Three things I would push on. First, the tilt directions. 1.5 and 3.0 both say the missing did better, a defensible registry story since a patient whose skin has cleared has less reason to come back. But look at the reported set: NRI is effectively an odds ratio of zero, the observed rate an odds ratio of one, then 1.5 and 3.0. Nothing between zero and one. No 0.5, no 0.67, which is the clinically live story that people whose disease is worse stop attending. A tipping-point analysis locates the assumption at which the conclusion flips, and that region is not shown.

JORDAN Second.

ALEX The direction of the trap reverses in a safety study, and this transfer is ours, not theirs. The poster is explicitly effectiveness, where imputing non-response is conservative because it understates benefit. Take the same reflex to malignancy or MACE and imputing no event to everyone lost to follow-up understates harm. Conservative for effectiveness is anti-conservative for safety, the same structure as the intention-to-treat argument in episode 4.

JORDAN Third.

ALEX 84% outcome completeness at six months is good for a registry. The figure at five years in a malignancy PASS will not be 84%. Cro's own result says the assumption only drives the answer when both the deviation rate and the missing fraction are large, exactly the regime the poster does not demonstrate in. The method matters most where it has not been shown to work. Note also the level mismatch: the poster tilts one scalar, while reference-based imputation substitutes a whole longitudinal mean structure and carries the uncertainty through Rubin's rules. Cro's development is for continuous longitudinal outcomes; they say binary and time-to-event could be handled the same way but did not do it. So the poster is not an application of Cro, and the gap is where a registry PASS lives.
