What a mature registry's own gold-standard audit trail looks like

Leosdottir et al. (American Heart Journal, 2025) report on SWEDEHEART, Sweden's national cardiac quality registry (three subregistries: acute coronary syndromes/ACS, percutaneous coronary interventions/PCI, cardiac rehabilitation/CR), which ran source-document audits at four time points from 2011 to 2018 — expanding from partial site sampling (18% of ACS hospitals, 29% of cath labs in 2011) to full coverage of every reporting site by 2017–2018 (72 hospitals, 30 cath labs, 75 CR centres; >200,000 data points controlled). In the final round, completeness was 99.1% (ACS), 99.2% (PCI), and 94.5% (CR); accuracy against source records exceeded 95% for all three subregistries at all four time points, with a statistically significant improving trend (97.5%/98.4%/95.8% in the final round). Time-point fields, self-reported data, and data governed by complex definitions were consistently the least reliable. This is a real, large-scale gold-standard-comparison example — the method Lewis et al. (ep3) found increasingly rare in the wider field. Not OA; abstract-only, flagged throughout. Pipeline stage: (d) evaluation — a template for the shape a rigorous accuracy audit and its reporting can take, not a claim about CFFPR's own state.

  • Source-document audit — comparing registry-recorded values against the original medical record at the reporting site, the classic gold-standard comparison method for data-quality assessment.（中文：将注册库中记录的数值与上报站点的原始病历进行比对，是数据质量评估中经典的金标准比较方法。）

  • Accuracy (audit sense) — the proportion of recorded values that match the source medical record on direct comparison.（中文：经直接比对后，记录数值与源病历相符的比例。）
