Ep3 — Track C: informative observation times and the sicker-patients-visit-more trap

Weaver C, Xiao L, Lu W. (2022/2023). Functional data analysis for longitudinal data with informative observation times. Biometrics.

A statistics paper, not a registry paper, but one that sits directly under any pooled reference curve built from registry encounters. When visit timing and outcome level share a common underlying cause — the sicker-patients-visit-more scenario — the standard approach of pooling every observation with equal weight (OBS weighting) produces an inconsistent estimate of the population mean function: it doesn't converge to the right answer no matter how much data you collect. A per-subject weighting scheme (SUBJ weighting) that down-weights frequently observed subjects restores consistency, estimable via penalized splines for the mean and penalized tensor-product splines for the covariance. The real-data validation — 371 Parkinson's disease patients self-reporting symptom severity on a voluntary follow-up schedule — shows patients who report more often trend toward less severe symptoms, exactly the visit-frequency/outcome-level correlation the theory warns about.

Plain-English glossary

  • Informative observation times — when the timing or frequency of a subject's visits is correlated with their outcome level, violating the usual assumption that visit timing is independent of the outcome.（中文：当受试者就诊的时间或频率与其结果水平相关时，就违反了通常假设的"就诊时间与结果独立"这一前提。）
  • Inconsistent estimation — an estimator that does not converge to the true value even as sample size grows to infinity, as opposed to merely being biased in finite samples.（中文：一种即使样本量趋于无穷也不会收敛到真值的估计量，区别于仅在有限样本中存在偏差的情况。）
  • OBS weighting vs. SUBJ weighting — OBS pools every observation with equal weight regardless of source subject; SUBJ down-weights each subject's observations so every subject contributes equally in aggregate, regardless of visit count.（中文：OBS 加权对每一次观测都一视同仁地等权重汇总，不论来自哪个受试者；SUBJ 加权则对每个受试者的观测值按其次数降权，使每个受试者在汇总中贡献相等，无论其就诊次数多少。）
  • Penalized splines / penalized tensor-product splines — smooth curve-fitting methods (for a mean function and, respectively, a two-dimensional covariance surface) that include a penalty term to prevent overfitting, with tuning parameters chosen to guarantee specific statistical properties here.（中文：一种带惩罚项、用于防止过拟合的平滑曲线拟合方法，分别用于均值函数和二维协方差曲面，此处的调参选择是为了保证特定的统计性质。）
  • Shared random effect model — a model in which an unobserved subject-level factor simultaneously drives both the visit-timing process and the outcome trajectory.（中文：一种模型，其中一个未被观测到的、受试者层面的因素同时驱动就诊时间过程和结果轨迹。）

Full citation

Methods / tools to lift

  • OBS-vs-SUBJ weighting divergence check — a lightweight diagnostic comparing observation-level and subject-level weighted mean/percentile curves to detect encounter-frequency confounding before building a full correction.
  • Penalized spline mean estimator / penalized tensor-product spline covariance estimator — the paper's proposed consistent estimators under informative observation times.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Pipeline stage: (a) candidate generation — primarily affects whatever "expected" reference curve a downstream flagging rule compares against; secondarily touches (d) evaluation. Buildable artifact: given a long-format table (patient_id, time, value), compute both OBS-weighted and SUBJ-weighted smoothed mean/percentile curves and report their divergence as a first-pass confounding check. Difficulty: Small-to-Medium.

What to verify first

  • Whether CFFPR encounter frequency actually correlates with height/weight-relevant severity — an empirical question to check against the data, not to assume from the Parkinson's example.
  • Whether the paper's specific shared-random-effect model is a reasonable approximation of how CFFPR visits are actually scheduled (routine calendar-driven vs. symptom-triggered visits behave very differently).
  • Whether this diagnostic should be a one-time upstream sanity check or a standing part of an existing mixed-model/spline candidate-generation approach.
  • A small OBS/SUBJ divergence is merely reassuring, not proof the shared-random-effect assumption holds — avoid over-interpreting a negative result.

Error types covered / not covered

Covered: bias in a pooled reference curve arising from correlation between visit frequency and outcome level (a confound in the reference, not the individual record). Not covered: unit conversion errors, digit transposition, decimal-shift errors, carry-forward errors, or discriminating a genuine clinical event from a data-entry error at the individual-encounter level — this paper operates entirely upstream of individual-record adjudication.
