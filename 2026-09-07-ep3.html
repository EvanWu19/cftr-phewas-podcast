Blurb

This is a governance and infrastructure case study, not a flagging-algorithm paper. CODI (Childhood Obesity Data Initiative) is a distributed health data network (DHDN) framework that lets public health, clinical, health-services-research, community-intervention, and IT stakeholders jointly build cross-sector data linkage infrastructure — specifically connecting clinical EHR data to community-program data (WIC, schools, food access) for childhood obesity research. The pilot ran 2018–2020 across three health systems and two community partners in the greater Denver metro area, with an independent third-party evaluation built on the GEP-HI (Good Evaluation Practice in Health Informatics) methodology, gathering lessons learned via stakeholder interviews. Key qualitative finding: integrating community-partner data with clinical data may require a trusted third party's infrastructure. The paper index's note that CODI wires in automated anthropometric cleaning is plausible but not confirmed by the abstract in hand — flagged as an open item to verify against full text. The episode reads this as supplying organizational vocabulary and a participatory-governance template, not a portable technical method.
