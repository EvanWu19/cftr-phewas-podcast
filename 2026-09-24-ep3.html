Plain-English glossary

Deviation from strategy — A participant departing from the treatment strategy assigned to them at baseline (e.g., stopping or interrupting the drug), as distinct from being reassigned to a different strategy.（中文：受试者偏离基线时分配的治疗策略（如停药或中断）,不同于转换至另一策略。）

Inverse probability of censoring weighting (IPCW) — Weighting each observed person-time record by the inverse of the probability of remaining uncensored given past covariates and treatment, to recover the population that would have been observed absent informative dropout.（中文：按"根据既往协变量与治疗仍未被删失的概率"的倒数加权,以还原若无信息性失访时本应观察到的人群。）

Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) — Weighting each person-time record by the inverse of the probability of their own observed treatment given past treatment and covariate history, creating a pseudo-population in which treatment is independent of the measured past.（中文：按"个体在既往治疗与协变量史下实际接受该治疗的概率"的倒数加权,构建一个治疗与已测量既往信息独立的伪人群。）

Marginal structural model (MSM) — A model for the marginal (population-level) distribution of the counterfactual outcome under a specified treatment history or regime, with no conditioning on time-varying covariates.（中文：描述在特定治疗史/方案下,反事实结局边际（人群层面）分布的模型,不对时变协变量作条件化。）

Per-protocol estimand — The causal effect of actually adhering to an assigned treatment strategy, as opposed to the effect of being assigned to it regardless of adherence (the intention-to-treat-aligned estimand).（中文：估计"实际依从所分配治疗策略"的因果效应,区别于不论依从与否的意向治疗估计目标。）

Positivity — The assumption that every combination of covariate history compatible with the design has a nonzero probability of receiving each treatment level being compared, required for IPW-based estimators to be well defined.（中文：假设在设计允许的每种协变量史下,每种治疗水平都有非零的发生概率,是IPW类估计量成立的前提。）

Pseudo-population — The reweighted population created by inverse probability weighting, in which treatment (or censoring) at each time is statistically independent of the measured past.（中文：经逆概率加权后构建出的重加权人群,其中各时点的治疗（或删失）与已测量的既往信息统计独立。）

Re-assignment (to a different strategy) — A post-baseline treatment change that constitutes crossing over into a distinct treatment strategy with its own confounding structure, as opposed to a deviation from the original strategy.（中文：基线后治疗变化实际上转入另一具有独立混杂结构的治疗策略,不同于偏离原策略。）

Stabilized weight — An inverse probability weight whose numerator is replaced with the probability of the observed treatment given a coarser set of information (e.g., baseline covariates alone) rather than the full time-varying history, reducing variance while preserving the target of estimation.（中文：将逆概率权重的分子替换为基于更粗信息（如仅基线协变量）而非完整时变病史的治疗概率,在保持估计目标不变的同时降低方差。）

Strategy-based target trial emulation — A target trial emulation in which a treatment strategy, not just a drug, is assigned at baseline, and the analysis plan specifies how post-baseline changes (deviation vs. reassignment) are to be handled.（中文：目标试验模拟中,基线时分配的是"治疗策略"而非单纯药物,分析计划须明确基线后变化（偏离或转换）应如何处理。）

Time-varying confounding affected by prior treatment — A covariate that both predicts subsequent treatment (a confounder) and is itself a consequence of earlier treatment, which standard time-fixed regression cannot correctly adjust for.（中文：某协变量既预测后续治疗（是混杂因素）,又是既往治疗的结果,标准的时间固定回归无法正确校正此类混杂。）

JORDAN So let's set the vocabulary first. This episode lives inside target trial emulation, which Runyu already knows the shape of, but there's a specific flavor here, strategy-based emulation. What does that mean.

ALEX A target trial specifies a treatment strategy at baseline, not just a drug. "Initiate secukinumab and continue it" is a strategy. The estimand you attach to that strategy determines what a deviation means. If your estimand is the treatment-strategy or intention-to-treat-aligned effect, you keep following everyone according to their original assignment regardless of what they actually do afterward, deviation is just noise you analyze through. If your estimand is per-protocol, you're asking what happens if people actually adhere to the strategy they were assigned, and the moment someone stops adhering, they stop representing that counterfactual world, so you censor them at that point.

JORDAN And a deviation from strategy versus what her poster calls re-assignment, why does that distinction matter enough to build a poster around.

ALEX Because they're different events with different implications for the analysis. A deviation is someone falling off the strategy they were assigned to, stopping the drug, interrupting it, going off protocol. A re-assignment is someone actually crossing over into a different strategy arm, switching from drug A to drug B in a way that puts them under a new, distinct treatment regime with its own confounding structure. Those sound similar in a registry, someone stops one biologic and starts another, but for the estimand they're not the same event at all, and that's exactly the seam her poster is standing on.

JORDAN Okay, now tell me what the CorEvitas team actually did. And remember this is a poster, so keep it to what's actually claimed.

ALEX It's a methods and simulation poster, she's the presenting author, three other CorEvitas or PPD statisticians as co-authors. The setup is a strategy-based target trial emulation where everyone gets a treatment strategy assigned at baseline, and any deviation from that assigned strategy is handled by censoring the person at the point they deviate, the per-protocol setup I just described. Their result is narrow, not a general theorem. Under that specific setup, the probability of a person remaining adherent through time t and the probability of that same person remaining uncensored through time t are numerically identical, because adherence-failure and censoring are defined as the same event. And if those two probabilities are identical at every time point, the cumulative time-varying inverse-probability-of-treatment weight is proportional to the inverse-probability-of-censoring weight. Same object, different name.

JORDAN What do they say the practical implication is.

ALEX That time-varying IPTW is only doing new work when post-baseline changes constitute a genuine re-assignment to a different strategy, something with its own confounders to model. When changes are deviations from a single assigned strategy, you don't need a separate time-varying treatment weight on top of the censoring weight, because you'd be weighting by the same probability twice under two names. That's the whole poster, a bookkeeping result stated plainly, presented as a methods point for a specific emulation design, not a claim about causal inference generally.

JORDAN Which is where Robins comes in, because right now that result is an assertion. I don't actually see why it's true.

ALEX Right, to see why, you have to go back to the problem MSMs were built to solve, time-varying confounding affected by prior treatment. Picture a covariate, call it disease activity, measured repeatedly. It predicts what treatment you get next, so it's a confounder you have to adjust for. But it's also a consequence of the treatment you already received, because the drug changed your disease activity. That's the trap. Condition on it in ordinary regression, the way you'd condition on any confounder, and you're also conditioning on something sitting on the causal pathway between earlier treatment and the outcome, so you block part of the effect you're trying to estimate. And when that same covariate shares an unmeasured cause with the outcome, conditioning on it can open a biasing path that wasn't there before, a collider problem. So you're stuck, you must adjust for it to handle confounding of the next treatment decision, and you must not adjust for it in the ordinary sense, because it's also on the causal path. Standard time-fixed regression has no way to do both at once.

JORDAN And the marginal structural model gets around that how.

ALEX By changing what's being modeled. Instead of modeling the outcome conditional on the full time-varying covariate history, an MSM models the marginal distribution, typically the mean, of the counterfactual outcome under a specified treatment history or regime, with no conditioning on intermediate covariates at all. It's a model for "if everyone had followed this history, what would the outcome distribution look like," stated at the population level. You can't just estimate that by comparing people who happened to follow different histories, because they differ on exactly the time-varying confounders you're not allowed to condition on. IPW gets you there anyway. At each time point, you weight each person's record by the inverse of the probability that they'd receive exactly the treatment they did receive, given everything known about them up to that point. Multiply those weights across time and you get a cumulative person-time weight. In the pseudo-population that creates, treatment at each time is statistically independent of the measured past, because you've up-weighted people who did something improbable given their history and down-weighted people who did the expected thing. Fit an ordinary model in that pseudo-population and its coefficients estimate the marginal structural model's parameters, under no unmeasured confounding, positivity, and a correctly specified weight model.

JORDAN And stabilized weights are a fix for what, exactly.

ALEX Variance. The plain weight has 1 in the numerator, so its distribution can get extremely long-tailed, especially for anyone who repeatedly does something improbable given their history, a few enormous weights end up dominating the pseudo-population and blowing up the variance of the estimate. A stabilized weight replaces that numerator with the probability of the person's observed treatment given a coarser set of information, usually baseline covariates alone, instead of the full time-varying history. The ratio still identifies the marginal effect, but it's much better behaved, weights cluster near one instead of spreading into the tail. It doesn't fix a true positivity violation, if some history makes a treatment essentially impossible no reweighting saves you, but it makes near-violations far less punishing in practice.

JORDAN And IPCW is the same idea pointed at dropout instead of treatment.

ALEX Exactly the same logic, different exposure. If people leave the study for reasons related to their own prognosis, analyzing only those who stay produces a biased picture of what would have happened to everyone. IPCW weights each person, at each time they're still observed, by the inverse of the probability that someone with their history would still be uncensored at that point. That reweighting recovers the population you would have had if nobody had dropped out informatively. Structurally it's the identical estimator as IPTW, a cumulative product of inverse probabilities built from a model fit on past covariates and treatment, just aimed at a censoring indicator instead of a treatment indicator.

JORDAN So now go back to her poster. Why does that make the collapse make sense instead of just being asserted.

ALEX Because once you see both weights as "inverse probability of a person's observed status given their past," the collapse is almost mechanical. In her setup, the treatment-adherence indicator and the censoring indicator are the same variable wearing two names. Someone deviates from the assigned strategy, and that is simultaneously the event that ends their adherence and the event that gets them censored, by construction, because deviation is defined as censoring in this design. If the underlying event is identical, the probability model for "still adherent" and the probability model for "still uncensored" are fit on the same data-generating process, so the weights built from them are proportional. You're not discovering two independent sources of bias and correcting each one, you're computing the same correction twice.

ALEX If a protocol specifies both a time-varying IPTW for adherence and a separate IPCW for the same deviation event, and multiplies them together into a combined weight, it's squaring information that should only enter once, which distorts the effective sample and the variance, and it usually signals that the estimand wasn't pinned down cleanly before the weighting was designed.
