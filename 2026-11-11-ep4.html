How did this episode land for you?

Ep4 (Track C — registry QC, slot 2/2): A scalable rules-based data-quality framework, with height/weight value-out-of-range rules among its top findings

Wang and colleagues (Studies in Health Technology and Informatics, 2019) compile over 6,000 healthcare data-quality rules from existing sources (OMOP/OHDSI, Sentinel, HCSRN), abstract them into 22 reusable rule templates mapped onto the Kahn 2016 data-quality framework's conformance/completeness/plausibility language, and run 11 implemented templates over 1.46 million patients across nine facilities and four EHR systems. Two of the ten most frequent flagged problems are literally anthropometric value-out-of-range rules — height greater than 3 meters (2,472 instances) and weight greater than 300 kg (1,581 instances) — alongside 22,028 physiologically impossible lab results and other temporal/coding-incompatibility problems. There's no sensitivity/specificity or gold-standard validation reported anywhere, and the authors themselves flag two rules (Aspirin-in-ischemic-heart-disease, missing HbA1c in older records) where a flagged discrepancy might be clinically valid rather than an error. This episode maps to candidate generation (pipeline stage (a), primary) with a secondary hit on review budget (stage (c)), and the buildable artifact is the simplest possible layer in the whole pipeline: a hard-bound absolute-value pre-filter for height, weight, and age.

Plain-English glossary

  • Kahn framework (2016) — A widely cited harmonized terminology for EHR data-quality dimensions, including conformance (does a value match an expected format or relationship), completeness, and plausibility (is a value biologically/contextually believable).（中文：一套被广泛引用的、用于描述EHR数据质量维度的统一术语体系，包括一致性（数值是否符合预期格式或关系）、完整性，以及合理性（数值在生物学或情境上是否可信）。）
  • OMOP / OHDSI — A common data model (Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership, now under the OHDSI — Observational Health Data Sciences and Informatics — initiative) that standardizes how observational health data is structured across institutions, so analysis code and data-quality rules can be written once and reused anywhere data is mapped into it.（中文：一种通用数据模型（现属于OHDSI——观察性健康数据科学与信息学计划），用于规范观察性健康数据在各机构间的结构方式，使分析代码和数据质量规则能够一次编写、在任何已映射为该格式的数据上重复使用。）
  • Rule template / knowledge table separation — Abstracting many rules sharing the same logic structure into one reusable template, with the specific clinical content (e.g. which gender-diagnosis pairs are incompatible) pulled into a separate, editable knowledge table — the key move that makes thousands of rules manageable.（中文：把许多具有相同逻辑结构的规则抽象为一个可复用的模板，并把具体临床内容（例如哪些性别-诊断组合不兼容）抽取到一个独立、可编辑的知识表中——这是让成千上万条规则变得可管理的关键做法。）
  • Value-out-of-range rule — A rule flagging a value outside limits compatible with life or grossly incompatible with labeling (e.g. height >3m, weight >300kg, drug dose, date of birth before 1880) — the simplest and cheapest type of data-quality check.（中文：标记超出与生命相容限度或严重偏离标签说明数值的规则（例如身高超过3米、体重超过300公斤、药物剂量异常、出生日期早于1880年）——数据质量检查中最简单、成本最低的一类。）
  • Clinically-valid exception (conditional validity) — A rule-flagged discrepancy that may actually reflect a legitimate clinical scenario (e.g. a documented drug contraindication) rather than a true data error, requiring case-by-case judgment before trusting a raw hit count.（中文：某条规则标记出的差异，实际上可能反映的是合理的临床情形（例如有记录的用药禁忌），而不是真正的数据错误，在信任原始触发计数之前需要逐案判断。）

Full citation

Wang Z, Dagtas S, Talburt J, Baghal A, Zozus M. "Rule-Based Data Quality Assessment and Monitoring System in Healthcare Facilities." Studies in Health Technology and Informatics. 2019. PMID: 30741240 | PMCID: PMC6692115 | DOI: 10.3233/978-1-61499-951-5-460

Methods / tools to lift

  • 22 rule templates / 5 higher-level types (incompatibility, value-out-of-range, temporal-sequence-error, incompleteness, duplication) — a reusable taxonomy for organizing data-quality rules at scale, mapped to the Kahn framework's conformance/completeness/plausibility categories.
  • Knowledge-table-driven rule templates — separating rule logic (code) from domain knowledge (an editable table), so new clinical content can be added without touching the rule engine.
  • Manual post-hoc problem grouping — collapsing multiple rule-firing results into clinically meaningful problem categories for actionability (acknowledged by the authors as manual, not systematic).

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Pipeline stage: (a) candidate generation, primary (height>3m and weight>300kg are the simplest possible absolute-bound floor beneath any statistical model); (c) review budget, secondary (the manual problem-grouping step is a human-curated triage pattern relevant to bundling flagged records for the ~0.05% needing adjudication). Buildable artifact — CFFPR Hard-Bound Pre-Filter: a tiny layer of physiologically-impossible absolute-bound rules for height, weight, and age, modeled directly on this paper's rules, run as a zero-cost pre-filter ahead of any statistical flagging model, kept explicitly separate from it, with its own hit-rate tracked over time as a sentinel for upstream pipeline regressions (e.g. a new EHR integration introducing unit-conversion errors). Difficulty: S (small) — pure threshold checks with no statistical machinery; the work is picking bound values and wiring hit-rate tracking.

What to verify first

  • Whether equivalent absolute bounds already exist somewhere upstream in however height/weight currently get entered — a redundant post-hoc rule layer might catch nothing new.
  • Whether this paper's manual, non-systematic problem-grouping step is a real scalability concern worth addressing with an explicit, documented grouping logic from day one, rather than inherited ad hoc.
  • Whether even the simplest hard-bound rule can still fire on clinically valid cases for reasons specific to this project's own data and era, the way the Aspirin/HbA1c examples did here — a reason not to trust any rule's raw hit count as a true error count without review.

Pipeline stage

(a) candidate generation, primary; (c) review budget, secondary.

Error types covered / not covered

Covered: absolute physiologically-impossible values (unit-scale-of-magnitude errors likely to produce height>3m or weight>300kg), temporal-sequence errors, demographic/diagnosis-code incompatibilities. Not covered: no record-level sensitivity/specificity or gold-standard validation; does not address digit transposition, decimal-shift errors, carry-forward errors, or discrimination between a data-entry error and a real-but-unusual clinical event — and some flagged "errors" (Aspirin/HbA1c) may themselves be clinically valid.

ALEX The Kahn framework, from a 2016 paper by Kahn and colleagues, is a widely cited harmonized terminology for describing electronic-health-record data-quality dimensions, breaking quality down into categories like conformance — whether a value matches an expected format or an expected relationship to another value — completeness, and plausibility, whether a value is biologically or contextually believable. Kahn框架来自Kahn及同事们2016年的一篇论文，是一套被广泛引用的、用于描述电子健康记录数据质量维度的统一术语体系，将质量拆解为诸如一致性（conformance，指一个值是否符合预期格式，或是否符合与另一个值之间预期的关系）、完整性，以及合理性（plausibility，指一个值在生物学或情境上是否可信）等类别。 This paper explicitly maps its own five rule-template categories — incompatibility, value-out-of-range, temporal-sequence-error, incompleteness, and duplication — onto Kahn's conformance, completeness, and plausibility language, which is useful vocabulary for talking about any data-quality rule precisely rather than just calling everything "an error." 这篇论文明确把自己的五类规则模板——不兼容（incompatibility）、数值越界（value-out-of-range）、时间顺序错误（temporal-sequence-error）、不完整（incompleteness）和重复（duplication）——对应到Kahn框架中一致性、完整性和合理性这套语言体系中，这为精确谈论任何数据质量规则提供了有用的词汇，而不是笼统地把所有问题都叫作"错误"。 OMOP, or the Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership, now folded into the OHDSI — Observational Health Data Sciences and Informatics — initiative, is a common data model that standardizes how observational health data gets structured across institutions, specifically so that analysis code and, relevant here, data-quality rules, can be written once and reused anywhere that data has been mapped into the OMOP format. OMOP（Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership，观察性医学结局合作项目），现已并入OHDSI（Observational Health Data Sciences and Informatics，观察性健康数据科学与信息学）计划，是一种通用数据模型，用来规范观察性健康数据在各机构之间的结构方式，目的正是让分析代码、以及这里所说的数据质量规则，能够一次编写、并在任何已映射为OMOP格式的数据上重复使用。 This paper used OMOP/OHDSI's published rule set, alongside similar rule sets from the Sentinel initiative and the Healthcare Systems Research Network, as seed material before compiling its own six-thousand-plus rules. 这篇论文正是把OMOP/OHDSI已发布的规则集，连同来自Sentinel计划和医疗系统研究网络（Healthcare Systems Research Network）的类似规则集，作为种子材料，在此基础上才汇编出了自己那六千多条规则。

ALEX The background problem is specific to healthcare, and it's a sharp contrast with the clinical-trials world: in a clinical trial, if a data value looks wrong you can resolve the discrepancy against the medical record, the authoritative source — that's standard practice, and ten to thirty percent of a trial's budget can go to exactly that kind of data cleaning. 这里的背景问题是医疗健康领域特有的，和临床试验的世界形成了鲜明对比：在临床试验中，如果某个数据值看起来不对，可以拿医疗记录这个权威来源去核对、解决这个差异——这是标准做法，一项试验预算的百分之十到三十，可能就花在这类数据清理上。 In routine healthcare secondary-use data, there usually is no equivalent authoritative source to resolve against, clinicians already suffer alert fatigue from decision-support systems, and systematic data-quality assessment has historically received little institutional investment or research attention despite the recent, large-scale push toward secondary use of EHR data for research — driven by national programs like the NIH's Healthcare Systems Research Collaboratory and PCORI. 在常规医疗健康数据的二次使用中，通常没有这样一个可以核对的权威来源，临床医生本身已经因为决策支持系统而深受警报疲劳之苦，而系统性的数据质量评估，尽管近年来在推动EHR数据二次用于研究方面有大规模的政策推动——由NIH的医疗系统研究协作网（Healthcare Systems Research Collaboratory）和PCORI等国家级项目驱动——历来却很少获得机构层面的投入或研究关注。 What was genuinely unresolved before this paper was whether a rules-based approach could scale past the small, single-institution pilots that had shown promise before — Hart and Kuo's few-hundred-rule home-health system being one, with a reported fifty-percent-plus drop in rejected records — up to a multi-institution, multi-EHR scope without the rule set becoming unmanageable. 在这篇论文之前，真正尚未解决的问题是：一种基于规则的方法，能否从此前已显示出一定成效的小规模、单一机构试点——比如Hart和Kuo那个只有几百条规则的居家医疗系统，据报告拒收记录下降超过百分之五十——扩展到跨多机构、多EHR系统的规模，而不让规则集本身变得无法管理。
