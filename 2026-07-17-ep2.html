Show notes — Track B — RAG in biomedicine: systematic review, meta-analysis, GUIDE-RAG

Blurb. Liu, McCoy & Wright (JAMIA, 2025) is a PRISMA systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) versus baseline large language models on biomedical tasks — 20 studies (all published in 2024), pooled odds ratio 1.35 (95% CI 1.19–1.53), with Egger's test (p=.001) flagging likely publication bias, so the true effect is smaller. Two findings travel: the measured benefit depends heavily on the grader (human eval OR 1.65 vs automatic 1.20), and "complex" retrieval did not beat "simple" (both OR 1.30). The paper ships no code — it delivers GUIDE-RAG, a build/report checklist — so it is flagged on-air as a Track B miss and read for its method-map. Its most actionable gap: only ~25% of studies evaluated the retrieval stage separately from the answer, so almost no one can say whether a wrong answer was a retrieval miss or a generation miss. That motivates a rag-stage-attributor: a harness that logs retrieval-recall@k independently from answer correctness and labels every failure as "evidence not retrieved" vs "retrieved but misused."

Plain-English glossary

  • RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) — the model retrieves relevant documents and inserts them into the prompt before answering, rather than relying on memory.（中文：检索增强生成，模型先检索相关文档并并入提示，再作答，而非只凭记忆。）
  • GraphRAG — a RAG variant that retrieves over a knowledge graph of entities and relationships instead of loose text chunks.（中文：基于知识图谱的检索增强，在实体-关系图谱上检索，而非零散文本块。）
  • MMR (maximal marginal relevance) — a retrieval-selection rule that rewards both relevance and diversity, avoiding near-duplicate chunks.（中文：最大边际相关性，一种同时奖励相关性与多样性、避免选到重复片段的检索选择规则。）
  • SMART on FHIR — the open standard for securely plugging third-party apps into electronic health record data.（中文：让第三方应用安全接入电子病历数据的开放标准。）
  • POMR (problem-oriented medical record) — the structure by which modern EHRs organize information around a problem list.（中文：问题导向病历，现代电子病历围绕"问题列表"结构化组织信息的方式。）
  • Hallucination — an LLM confidently generating incorrect or fabricated information.（中文：幻觉，大语言模型自信地生成错误或虚构的信息。）
  • Fine-tuning — adapting a model's weights to a domain; effective but costly and not real-time updatable (contrast: RAG).（中文：微调，调整模型权重以适配领域；有效但代价高、无法实时更新，与 RAG 相对。）
  • SPOKE — a biomedical knowledge graph integrating 40+ public sources (genes, drugs, diseases and their relationships), used as one study's retrieval source.（中文：整合 40 多个公开生物医学来源的知识图谱，被一项研究用作检索来源。）
  • Algorithmovigilance — scientific monitoring of the adverse effects of deployed clinical algorithms over time.（中文：算法警戒，对已部署临床算法随时间产生的不良效应进行的科学监测。）

Methods / tools to lift

  • Three-stage RAG decomposition (pre-retrieval / retrieval / post-retrieval) — a checklist skeleton for building and, crucially, for evaluating each stage separately.
  • Effect-size meta-analysis of an AI intervention — odds ratio (dichotomous) / SMD (continuous), random-effects pooling, prediction interval, Egger's test for publication bias — a template for quantifying "does this AI thing actually help."
  • Grader-sensitivity subgroup — reporting human-eval vs automatic-eval effects separately, exposing how much a benefit depends on the evaluation method.
  • GUIDE-RAG — an expert-consensus build/report checklist; its most useful rule is "evaluate all three stages, not just the final answer."

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Serves AI-tooling / career (RWE × LLM-tooling, evaluation-discipline lane).

rag-stage-attributor (S/M) — wrap any biomedical RAG pipeline; for each question, log retrieval recall@k (did the gold evidence span appear in the top-k retrieved chunks?) independently from answer correctness, then label every failure "evidence not retrieved" vs "retrieved but misused." Input: question→gold-evidence-span pairs (no gold answer required). Dogfood on the project's own literature base or a CFTR2-guideline RAG where retrieval targets are known. Align the recall metric to the evidence a clinician would actually need, not to surface string overlap.

What to verify first

  • Publication bias is load-bearing — Egger p=.001 means the true effect is below 1.35; report it as "modest and probably inflated," never as a flat 1.35.
  • Apples-and-oranges pool — an OR from a multiple-choice benchmark and one from a triage tool are not the same currency; the single number hides real task/metric variety.
  • No code — every included pipeline is a black box; the retrieval settings behind these effects are not reproducible.
  • Grader dependence — human-vs-automatic (1.65 vs 1.20) means your reported benefit is partly a choice of evaluator; pin and pre-register it.
  • GUIDE-RAG is consensus, not validated — treat as a checklist, not proven evidence.
