Ep3 (Track C — registry QC, 1 of 2): The historical template for a two-part candidate flag

Thurber, Banks, and Banwell (2014, Public Health Research and Practice) review WHO-based approaches for evaluating the accuracy of children's height/weight data and, informed by that review and expert consultation, develop a method for processing and verifying longitudinal anthropometric measurements, applied to the Australian Longitudinal Study of Indigenous Children. The method checks for (a) deviation from the WHO reference population via age- and sex-adjusted height/weight/BMI z-scores, and (b) implausible within-individual height/weight trajectories over time — explicitly framed as a probabilistic approach to flagging likely-implausible measurements, rather than a hard binary rule, and explicitly designed to be reproducible in other settings, including for validating large databases. Only the abstract was retrievable for this paper (no PMCID, full text not recoverable); this episode is abstract-level detail throughout, with no specific numeric cutoffs or performance figures available to report. The paper lands on pipeline stage (a), candidate generation: its dual-check design, where overlap between the cross-sectional and longitudinal flags is itself diagnostic, is a direct historical template for modern anthropometric QC candidate generators. Buildable artifact: a Two-Part Z-Score + Trajectory Candidate Flagger.

Plain-English glossary

  • CDC vs. WHO growth references — two different reference growth charts, built from different source populations and (for some ages) different statistical choices, so a z-score computed against one is not numerically identical to one computed against the other for the same measurement.（中文：两套不同的参照生长图表，分别基于不同的源人群构建，在某些年龄段的统计方法也不同，因此同一测量值按两套参照计算出的z分数在数值上并不相同。）
  • LMS method (Lambda-Mu-Sigma) — the standard statistical method behind most pediatric growth-reference z-scores, using three smoothly age-varying curves to explicitly model the skewness of growth measurement distributions rather than assuming symmetric normality.（中文：大多数儿科生长参照z分数背后的标准统计方法，用三条随年龄平滑变化的曲线明确建模生长测量分布的偏度，而非假设对称的正态分布。）
  • Longitudinal trajectory check — examining an individual's sequential height/weight measurements over time to catch implausible within-person changes (e.g., an apparent height decrease) that a single cross-sectional value can't reveal.（中文：检查个体随时间推移的连续身高体重测量值，以捕捉单次横断面数值无法揭示的不合理个体内部变化，例如表观身高下降。）
  • WHO growth reference z-score — a measurement expressed as the number of standard deviations above or below the age- and sex-specific median of a defined WHO reference population.（中文：将测量值表示为相对于特定WHO参照人群按年龄和性别划分的中位数、高出或低于多少个标准差。）

Full citation

Thurber KA, Banks E, Banwell C. "Approaches to maximising the accuracy of anthropometric data on children: review and empirical evaluation using the Australian Longitudinal Study of Indigenous Children." Public Health Research and Practice. 2014. PMID: 25828446 | DOI: 10.17061/phrp2511407

Methods / tools to lift

  • Cross-sectional WHO z-score deviation check — flagging height/weight/BMI values that sit implausibly far from the age- and sex-adjusted WHO reference distribution.
  • Longitudinal within-individual trajectory check — flagging sequential measurement changes (drops, implausible jumps) inconsistent with real growth patterns.
  • Combined dual-flag reporting — treating agreement between the cross-sectional and longitudinal checks as a stronger review signal than either check alone.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Pipeline stage: (a) candidate generation, primary — the dual cross-sectional-plus-longitudinal check is itself a two-part candidate-flagging rule, with agreement between the two signals serving as a built-in confidence indicator. Buildable artifact: a Two-Part Z-Score + Trajectory Candidate Flagger — compute age/sex-adjusted WHO z-scores per visit and flag extremes, separately flag implausible within-individual sequential changes, and report both flag sets plus their overlap as a transparent baseline.

Error types covered / not covered: Covers cross-sectional extreme-value errors and longitudinal implausible-change errors (including apparent height "shrinkage" and improbable jumps) in principle; does not specify (in the retrievable abstract) which concrete error mechanisms — unit conversion, digit transposition, decimal shift, carry-forward, equipment miscalibration, or real clinical events — the flagged records in this cohort were ultimately attributed to.

What to verify first

  • The full paper's actual z-score and trajectory-change cutoff values, and any flag-rate or sensitivity figures for the Indigenous Australian children cohort — none of this is in the retrievable abstract.
  • Whether this 2014 dual-check framing was later absorbed into, or superseded by, more recent tools (e.g., growthcleanr) that formalize the longitudinal half with an explicit statistical model — worth a direct comparison rather than treating this as purely historical.
  • Whether any thresholds calibrated on this specific cohort would need re-calibration for a different population before reuse.
  • Whether a simple two-part flagger like the one sketched here is best run as a standalone QC pass or as a transparent sanity baseline alongside a more sophisticated longitudinal model — an open design choice, not something this paper settles.
