Blurb

This episode covers "Allostery is a widespread cause of loss-of-function variant pathogenicity" (Liao & Lehner, Nature Communications, 2026), a methods paper that separates two different reasons a protein loses function: destabilization (the protein misfolds and is degraded) versus a distant allosteric defect (the protein stays stable but a distal site stops communicating with the active site). The authors build an activity-abundance residual framework and validate it across PTEN, GCK, three DNA-repair genes profiled by saturation genome editing (BRCA1, RAD51C, BAP1), two experimental allosteric maps (PSD-95-PDZ3, GRB2-SH3), and six more enzymes, showing that allostery explains roughly half of pathogenic loss-of-function variants, with effect size decaying exponentially with distance from the active site. For Track A and the Karen Raraigh collaboration on CFTR variant interpretation, this maps directly onto the existing Class II (trafficking) versus Class III (gating) split, and suggests a buildable "CFTR allosteric-residual flag" pipeline using ESM-1v or AlphaMissense minus ThermoMPNN.

Plain-English glossary

  • Allostery / allosteric site — Regulation of a protein's activity through changes at a site distant from its active (orthosteric) site, rather than at the site itself.（中文：变构——通过远离活性位点的部位发生变化来调控蛋白质活性的机制。）
  • AlphaMissense — A deep-learning model that predicts missense-variant pathogenicity directly from sequence and structure.（中文：一种直接从序列和结构预测错义变异致病性的深度学习模型。）
  • CFTR Class II vs Class III variants — CF-specific variant classes: Class II (e.g., F508del) are trafficking defects where the protein misfolds and never reaches the membrane; Class III (e.g., G551D) are gating defects where the protein reaches the membrane but the channel fails to open properly.（中文：CF特有的变异分类——II类（如F508del）为转运缺陷，蛋白质错误折叠无法到达细胞膜；III类（如G551D）为门控缺陷，蛋白质到达细胞膜但通道无法正常开放。）
  • ESM-1v — A protein language model (pLM) that scores a variant's "total evolutionary fitness" directly from protein sequence.（中文：一种直接从蛋白质序列预测变异"总体进化适应度"的蛋白质语言模型。）
  • Gain-of-function (GOF) variant — A variant that makes a protein hyperactive or active when it shouldn't be; the classic mechanism behind oncogene drivers like KRAS, JAK2, EGFR.（中文：功能获得型变异——使蛋白质过度活跃或在不应活跃时活跃，是KRAS、JAK2、EGFR等癌基因驱动突变的经典机制。）
  • Loss-of-function (LOF) variant — A variant that breaks a protein's function; almost all CFTR disease variants are LOF.（中文：功能丧失型变异——使蛋白质功能受损；几乎所有CFTR致病变异都属于此类。）
  • Orthosteric site (active site) — The physical pocket on a protein where substrate binding or catalysis actually occurs, as distinct from an allosteric site.（中文：正位位点/活性位点——蛋白质上底物结合或催化真正发生的物理口袋，与变构位点相区别。）
  • Protein language model (pLM) — A model (e.g., ESM-1v) that learns patterns of evolutionary fitness directly from protein sequence data, without explicit structural input.（中文：蛋白质语言模型——直接从蛋白质序列数据中学习进化适应度模式的模型，无需显式结构输入。）
  • Saturation genome editing (SGE) — An experimental method that introduces nearly every possible variant at a genomic locus and reads out a direct fitness/function score for each.（中文：饱和基因组编辑——在基因组某位点引入几乎所有可能变异，并为每个变异读出直接的适应度/功能评分的实验方法。）
  • ThermoMPNN — A model built on the ProteinMPNN inverse-folding network that predicts a mutation's effect on folding free energy (ΔΔG).（中文：一种建立在ProteinMPNN反向折叠网络之上、用于预测突变对折叠自由能影响的模型。）
  • VAMP-seq (Variant Abundance by Massively Parallel sequencing) — An experimental method that measures the cellular abundance of thousands of protein variants at once.（中文：一种同时测量数千个蛋白质变异在细胞内丰度的实验方法。）
  • ΔΔG (folding free energy change) — The predicted or measured change in a protein's folding stability caused by a mutation; used here as the stability axis separated from the functional/allosteric axis.（中文：折叠自由能变化——突变导致蛋白质折叠稳定性发生的预测或实测变化，本文中作为与功能/变构轴相分离的稳定性轴。）

Full citation

Methods / tools to lift

  • ThermoMPNN — ProteinMPNN-based inverse-folding model predicting ΔΔGf (folding free energy change) per missense variant.
  • ESM-1v — protein language model giving a sequence-only "total evolutionary fitness" score, used as a pathogenicity/fitness proxy.
  • AlphaMissense — deep-learning missense pathogenicity predictor, used as a cross-check against ESM-1v (same R² pattern).
  • Activity-abundance LOESS-residual framework — LOESS (span=0.7, family="symmetric", R stats package) fit of stability vs. activity/fitness; vertical residual isolates the abundance-independent ("allosteric") functional defect.
  • Nonlinear least-squares exponential decay fit (R nls()) — models decay of residual magnitude with 3D distance from the orthosteric site.
  • AlphaFold Protein Structure Database — source structures for distance and solvent-accessibility calculations.
  • DSSP / BioPython — relative solvent-accessible surface area (rSASA) calculation to define buried core residues.
  • PIONEER interactome — binding/active-site annotations.
  • ChimeraX — structure visualization and van-der-Waals-overlap-based contact definition.
  • EVcouplings — evolutionary coupling scores (comparison method).
  • Code: https://github.com/lehner-lab/allostery_pathogenicity. Data: Zenodo 18386427 (ESM-1v/stability predictions), 18381534 (ThermoMPNN predictions).

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Karen Raraigh / CFTR variant interpretation & harmonization. Buildable artifact — "CFTR allosteric-residual flag": for every CFTR missense variant, compute a residual equal to an ESM-1v or AlphaMissense pathogenicity score minus a comparably rescaled ThermoMPNN-predicted ΔΔG, using CFTR's AlphaFold structure plus precomputed/runnable scores for both models. Flag large-residual variants (high predicted pathogenicity despite low predicted destabilization) as candidate allosteric/gating-mechanism (Class III-like) variants, routed to a different curation lane than stability-explained (candidate Class II/trafficking-defect) calls. Difficulty: S-M — scores and structure are free/public; the work is building the LOESS-residual pipeline and picking a threshold, directly templated by the paper's own code (github.com/lehner-lab/allostery_pathogenicity).

What to verify first

  • Whether ESM-1v or AlphaMissense residuals computed on CFTR actually correlate with existing clinical Class II vs. Class III calls.
  • Whether a single-pocket, single-domain residual framework validated on PTEN/GCK/BRCA1-type proteins generalizes to CFTR's multi-domain ABC-transporter architecture, including its intrinsically disordered R-domain.
  • Comprehensive experimental allosteric maps exist for only a handful of proteins; proteome-wide extrapolation (including to CFTR) is consistent with, but not directly validated by, those maps.
  • ESM-1v is a fitness/evolutionary proxy, not a direct clinical-pathogenicity measure — a large residual on an unannotated CFTR variant could reflect a true allosteric effect, a subclinical variant, or a model artifact, and these would need to be distinguished before using the flag for curation decisions.
