How did this episode land for you?

Ep1 (Track A — variant interpretation): Splice-site variants in neurology, from molecular mechanisms to clinical interpretation

Gowda and Harish's 2026 Neurogenetics review doesn't introduce a new tool or dataset — it synthesizes how a splice-altering variant found on a diagnostic report actually gets validated and turned into a clinical decision. Splice-disrupting variants are estimated to account for 15–30% of pathogenic alleles across Mendelian neurological disorders (epilepsy, hereditary neuropathy, ataxia, dystonia, mitochondrial disease, neuromuscular disorders, and the newly recognized spliceosomal snRNA neurodevelopmental disorders), yet clinical pipelines built around protein-coding regions routinely deprioritize the intronic and synonymous positions where many of these variants sit. The review walks through deep learning splice predictors (SpliceAI, Pangolin), RNA sequencing, long-read transcriptomics, and minigene functional assays as the toolkit for closing that gap, while being explicit about where each piece is weakest: applying the ACMG/AMP PVS1 criterion to a splice variant needs gene- and variant-specific adaptation rather than a universal rule; the prediction tools disagree with each other most exactly outside the core splice-site consensus sequence, where disagreement matters most; and RNA-level validation is constrained by splicing's tissue- and developmental-stage-specific nature, which is precisely why minigene assays (reconstructing a variant's genomic context in a plasmid to test splicing directly in a cell line) exist. The episode maps this onto CFTR's own well-documented deep-intronic splice burden — using the classic c.3849+10kbC>T pseudoexon-activating variant as a worked example — and lands on a buildable triage tool for the Karen Raraigh variant-interpretation collaboration. This paper is abstract-only (closed access, no PMCID), so every specific claim here is sourced to the abstract and flagged as such on-air.

Plain-English glossary

ACMG/AMP framework — The standard rule system (American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics / Association for Molecular Pathology) clinical labs use to classify a variant as pathogenic, likely pathogenic, uncertain, likely benign, or benign, by combining weighted evidence codes.（中文：美国医学遗传学与基因组学学会/分子病理学会制定的变异分类标准体系，通过加权证据代码判断变异的致病性。）

Antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) — A short synthetic nucleic acid drug that binds RNA directly and can be designed to block a pseudoexon or force exon inclusion; the splice-modulation mechanism behind drugs like nusinersen.（中文：一种能直接结合RNA的短链合成核酸药物，可用于阻断假外显子或强制外显子保留，是诺西那生等药物的作用机制。）

Canonical splice site — The short, nearly invariant 5' donor and 3' acceptor sequence motifs at each intron boundary that the spliceosome normally recognizes to remove the intron.（中文：内含子两端几乎恒定不变的短序列motif，剪接体依靠它识别并切除内含子。）

Cryptic / pseudoexon-activating variant — A deep-intronic variant that creates a new splice site strong enough to be used instead of the canonical one, causing a stretch of intron to be mis-spliced into the transcript as if it were a real exon (a "pseudoexon").（中文：位于内含子深处的变异，会产生一个强度足以被误用的新剪接位点，使一段内含子被错误地当作"假外显子"拼接进转录本。）

Leaky splicing — A splice variant that partially disrupts the normal site but still allows some correctly-spliced transcript to be produced alongside the aberrant product; the residual normal transcript can soften the phenotype.（中文：变异只部分破坏正常剪接位点，仍有部分转录本被正常剪接，这部分残留正常产物可能使表型变轻。）

Minigene assay — A functional test that clones a variant's genomic exon-intron context into a plasmid, transfects it into cells, and reads out the resulting splicing pattern directly — used when the patient's accessible tissue (e.g., blood) doesn't naturally express the relevant isoform.（中文：将变异的基因组外显子-内含子上下文克隆进质粒并转染细胞，直接检测剪接结果的功能实验，用于患者可取材组织不表达相关转录本的情况。）

PVS1 — The ACMG/AMP evidence code for "predicted loss of function, very strong" — the strongest evidence tier, which for splice variants requires careful gene- and variant-specific adaptation rather than a one-size-fits-all rule.（中文：ACMG/AMP框架中"预测功能丧失，极强证据"代码，是最高等级的证据，对剪接变异的应用需要按基因和变异具体调整，而非通用规则。）

SpliceAI / Pangolin — Two deep-learning models that predict, from DNA sequence alone, how likely a position is to create or disrupt a splice site; they are known to disagree with each other most outside the core canonical splice-site consensus.（中文：两种深度学习模型，仅凭DNA序列即可预测某位置产生或破坏剪接位点的可能性，二者在偏离核心剪接位点共识序列时最容易产生分歧。）

Full citation

Gowda N, Harish S. Splice-site variants in neurology: from molecular mechanisms to clinical interpretation - a focused review. Neurogenetics. 2026. PMID: 42799960. DOI: 10.1007/s10048-026-00947-7.

Methods / tools to lift

  • SpliceAI — deep-learning splice-effect predictor from raw sequence; first-pass computational triage.
  • Pangolin — a second deep-learning splice-effect predictor, useful specifically as a disagreement check against SpliceAI.
  • Minigene functional assay — the RNA-level tie-breaker when accessible-tissue RNA won't express the relevant isoform.
  • RNA sequencing / long-read transcriptomics — direct observation of splicing outcome when the relevant tissue is accessible.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Serves the Karen Raraigh collaboration (CFTR variant interpretation and notation harmonization). Buildable artifact: CFTR Splice-Evidence Triage Sheet — run SpliceAI and Pangolin on a list of CFTR intronic/splice-region variants, flag disagreement between the tools or variants far from the canonical consensus sequence, cross-reference ClinVar and CFTR2 for any variant with a published minigene result, and output a per-variant note on whether a PVS1 call looks defensible, needs adaptation, or needs RNA follow-up first. Rough difficulty: M (mostly API calls to existing predictors plus a lookup layer; no new model training).

What to verify first

  • Read the full review once access allows, to see the specific PVS1 adaptation rules recommended and whether they're gene-family-general or something a CFTR-specific ClinGen expert panel would need to independently ratify.
  • Check published benchmark comparisons of SpliceAI vs. Pangolin specifically in the deep-intronic regime before treating "the two tools disagree" as inherently meaningful — their calibration there may differ.
  • For any CFTR variant flagged this way, check whether a minigene result already exists in the literature before assuming RNA follow-up is actually needed.
  • Keep the diagnostic/therapeutic distinction clear: flagging a candidate pseudoexon variant is not the same claim as identifying a treatable target for an ASO strategy.
