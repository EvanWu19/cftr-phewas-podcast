Ep2 — Track B — Seamless EMR data access: Integrated governance, digital health and the OMOP-CDM (Hallinan 2024)

A descriptive governance and architecture overview, not an empirical study, walking through how the OMOP Common Data Model (OMOP-CDM) converts heterogeneous electronic medical record data from any institution into a shared, standardized 16-table schema — without altering or moving the underlying source data. The paper frames OMOP-CDM's real value as governance-by-design: because standardized vocabularies (SNOMED-CT, LOINC, RxNorm) and a firewalled query tool (ATLAS) let collaborating institutions share aggregated, de-identified analysis results without ever handing over raw patient-level data, multi-site research becomes both easier to govern and easier to audit. Global adoption is offered as scale evidence — roughly 12% of EMRs worldwide, 453 databases, 928 million patient records, 41 countries, as of 2022 — alongside an honest admission of gaps: no seamless hospital-to-primary-care linkage in Australia yet, and specialist domains like oncology and pregnancy still need custom CDM extensions because the default schema doesn't standardize them cleanly. It's not a CFTR or CF paper at all, but it's a portable piece of registry/RWE infrastructure knowledge, and it lands on the AI-tooling-for-career priority with a small buildable scaffold-mapping exercise.

Plain-English glossary

OMOP-CDM (Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership Common Data Model) — A standardized schema, roughly 16 core tables, that raw EMR data from any institution is mapped into without changing the source data.（中文:一个标准化的数据模式,大约由16张核心表组成,任何机构的原始电子病历数据都会被映射进这个模式,而不会改变源数据本身。）

OHDSI (Observational Health Data Sciences and Informatics) — The open-source international consortium that develops and maintains the OMOP-CDM.（中文:开发和维护OMOP-CDM的开源国际协作组织。）

SNOMED-CT / LOINC / RxNorm — Standardized vocabularies used to code diagnoses, lab tests, and medications respectively, so the same clinical concept resolves to one standard code regardless of source system.（中文:分别用于诊断、检验项目和药物编码的标准化术语库,使同一临床概念无论来自哪个源系统都能归结为同一个标准编码。）

FAIR principles — Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable — a data-stewardship framework.（中文:可查找、可访问、可互操作、可重用——一套数据管理原则。）

CARE principles — Collective benefit, Authority, Researcher responsibility, and Ethics — a framework centering Indigenous data rights, used alongside FAIR.（中文:集体利益、权威性、研究者责任与伦理——一套聚焦原住民数据权益的框架,与FAIR原则并行使用。）

Five Safes framework — Safe people, safe projects, safe settings, safe data, safe outputs — a structured approach to data-access risk management.（中文:安全人员、安全项目、安全环境、安全数据、安全产出——一套结构化的数据访问风险管理方法。）

Waiver of consent — An ethics-committee-approved exception to requiring individual patient consent for secondary data use, typically applied when research is judged minimal risk.（中文:经伦理委员会批准的例外情形,允许在二次数据使用中免除个人患者同意,通常适用于被判定风险极小的研究。）

Data lake — An unstructured or loosely structured repository of native, disparate data with no standardized schema, contrasted in this paper with the OMOP-CDM's standardized approach.（中文:保存原生、彼此不一致数据、没有标准化模式的松散数据仓库,本文中与OMOP-CDM的标准化方法形成对比。）

Full citation

Hallinan CM, et al. (2024). Seamless EMR data access: Integrated governance, digital health and the OMOP-CDM. BMJ Health Care Informatics. PMID: 38387992 · PMCID: PMC10882353 · DOI: 10.1136/bmjhci-2023-100953. Open access, full text retrieved.

Methods / tools to lift

  • OMOP-CDM's extract-transform-load pattern for converting heterogeneous source data into one standardized schema without touching source data.
  • Standardized vocabulary mapping (SNOMED-CT / LOINC / RxNorm) as the mechanism that makes cross-institution concepts comparable.
  • ATLAS as a firewalled analysis-without-data-release query tool.
  • Achilles / the OHDSI Data Quality Dashboard — automated completeness, concordance, plausibility, and currency checks run against any OMOP-converted dataset.
  • CDM extensions as the sanctioned mechanism for specialist domains (oncology, pregnancy) that don't fit the generic schema.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Serves AI-tooling / career (portable RWE/registry infrastructure knowledge). Buildable: omop-cdm-scaffold-mapper (M) — map a handful of representative synthetic/de-identified registry-shaped fields (encounter dates, diagnosis codes, a lab-style measurement, a medication record) into their corresponding OMOP-CDM core tables and target vocabularies; optionally extend with a small Achilles-style completeness/plausibility check on the same fields.

What to verify first

  • The specific governance and consent mechanisms described are Australian/NHMRC-specific — the operational-use-vs-research-use distinction generalizes as a mental model, but the rules themselves don't transfer directly to other jurisdictions.
  • Adoption scale (12% of EMRs, 453 databases) measures conversion volume, not conversion quality — being "in OMOP" doesn't by itself mean the mapping or data-quality checks were clean.
  • Whether the default OMOP schema handles longitudinal anthropometric/growth trajectories cleanly, or would need a custom extension the way oncology and pregnancy data do — not addressed in this paper.
  • This is an external governance/tooling model — no claim here about any specific institution's own data infrastructure or consent practices.

Pipeline stage: Not applicable (Track B paper).
