Track C — Registry Data Quality & Anthropometric Outlier Detection

Blurb

Any residual-based height/weight flag rests on a fitted trajectory, and someone had to pick the software and its settings for that fit. This episode reads a 2019 methodology review from STRATOS Topic Group 2 (Perperoglou, Sauerbrei, Abrahamowicz, Schmid). It surveys the R packages used to fit splines in regression. The authors list the package landscape by downloads and reverse dependencies, with rms as the most depended-on package. They also name the main basis families. Then they fit the same simple data with different packages: 892 West African women, triceps skinfold thickness against age. Out of the box, the packages produce visibly different curves. The authors trace most of that gap to default hyperparameters (knot count and placement, penalty strength, degrees of freedom) and much less to the choice of basis. The comparison is qualitative, with no formal benchmark. For the CFFPR outlier work, it mainly offers caution and vocabulary, plus an indirect link to candidate generation. We propose a small stress test that checks how sensitive a spline-based trajectory model is to its hyperparameters.

Glossary

  • B-spline basis — The most widely used spline basis, built recursively; each basis function has local support, which gives good numerical stability.（中文：B样条基，局部支撑、数值稳定，实践中最常用）
  • Basis function — One of the set of functions whose weighted sum forms the fitted spline curve.（中文：基函数，加权组合后构成样条曲线的函数）
  • Hyperparameter — A setting chosen before fitting (knot count/placement, smoothing parameter, degrees of freedom) rather than estimated as a model coefficient.（中文：超参数，拟合前设定、非由模型估计的参数）
  • Knot — A point on the predictor axis where spline pieces join.（中文：节点，样条分段连接的位置）
  • Natural (cardinal) cubic spline — A cubic spline constrained to be linear beyond the boundary knots, which prevents erratic behavior at the edges of the data.（中文：自然/基数三次样条，边界节点外约束为线性，避免边缘剧烈摆动）
  • Penalized spline — The spline family that allows many knots and controls overfitting with a roughness penalty tuned by a smoothing parameter.（中文：惩罚样条，多节点加粗糙度惩罚）
  • Regression spline — The spline family in which the analyst fixes the number and placement of knots.（中文：回归样条，由分析者固定节点数量与位置）
  • Reverse dependencies (RD) — The number of other R packages that depend on a given package; a rough proxy for how central it is to the ecosystem.（中文：反向依赖，依赖该软件包的其他软件包数量）
  • Smoothing parameter (λ) — The tuning value that sets the strength of the roughness penalty in a penalized spline.（中文：平滑参数，控制惩罚强度）
  • STRATOS — STRengthening Analytical Thinking for Observational Studies, a multi-institution consortium of methodologists that develops guidance on better default practices for observational research; Topic Group 2 wrote this review.（中文：加强观察性研究分析思维联盟，提供统计方法学指导）
  • Truncated power series basis — An easily interpreted spline basis (polynomial plus a "kink" term after each knot) whose global support can cause numerical instability.（中文：截断幂级数基，易解读但全局支撑、数值不稳定）

Methods/tools to lift

  • rms — Frank Harrell's regression modeling strategies package; 598,185 downloads (2012–2018) and 63 reverse dependencies, the most depended-on package in the review's table.
  • splines2 — dedicated spline-basis construction package (31,031 downloads, RD=4).
  • pspline — Brian Ripley's penalized smoothing splines (146,939 downloads, RD=11).
  • mgcv / gam — generalized additive model route for penalized smooth terms.
  • cobs — constrained B-splines (58,533 downloads, RD=6).
  • B-spline basis — the default choice for numerical stability because of its local support.
  • Regression vs. penalized splines — the two named families: fixed knots chosen by the analyst vs. many knots plus a tuned roughness penalty.
  • STRATOS guidance initiative — a source of consensus-oriented methodological guidance to watch for later, more specific recommendations.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Lands as primarily (e) framework/vocabulary + methodological caution, with a secondary indirect touch on (a) candidate generation, for Liz/CFFPR data quality.

MVP: A hyperparameter-sensitivity stress test for any spline or penalized-spline trajectory model in use or under consideration. It takes a longitudinal height or weight series (or a representative sample) plus a candidate spline specification. It refits the data over a small grid of equally defensible knot counts, knot placements, or smoothing-parameter values using mature packages (rms, splines2, mgcv). For each point, it reports the range of its residual and whether the point crosses a flag threshold under some settings but not others. That shows whether the model's flagging behavior is fragile or robust before anyone trusts it for candidate generation. Difficulty: S–M (a refit loop over existing packages, no new modeling).

What to verify first

  • Has any spline or mixed-effects trajectory model in use or under consideration for registry height/weight had its hyperparameter sensitivity checked, or does it rely on package defaults?
  • Does the paper's finding (differences mostly hyperparameter-driven, not basis-driven) hold beyond its simple univariate cross-sectional examples, in longitudinal, per-patient, repeated-measures growth trajectories?
  • Which of the named packages (rms, splines2, pspline, mgcv/gam, cobs, etc.) is best suited to a longitudinal mixed-model context? The paper's comparisons mostly cover plain regression-spline fitting, not multilevel models.
  • Has STRATOS published more specific guidance on longitudinal or growth-trajectory modeling since this 2019 general review?

Pipeline stage

Primary: (e) nothing directly — methodological caution/vocabulary.

Secondary: (a) candidate generation — indirectly, since an under-validated spline substrate's hyperparameter sensitivity could propagate into noisy or unstable downstream residual-based flags.

Error types covered / not covered

Covered: none directly — this is not an error-detection method, it's a methodological caution about substrate-model hyperparameter sensitivity.

Not covered: unit conversion, digit transposition, decimal-shift, carry-forward/stale values, real-clinical-event-vs-error discrimination. This paper addresses none of these.
