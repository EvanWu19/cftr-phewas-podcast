Ep1 (Track A) — Ancestry-Stratified Variant Classification in Monogenic Diabetes Genes

Dario P, et al. 2026. Annals of Human Genetics. Abstract-only (no PMCID indexed).

Blurb

This paper cross-references gnomAD v4.0 (807,162 individuals) against ClinVar for sixteen monogenic-diabetes (MODY) genes, splitting variants into mutually exclusive European-private and non-European-private groups. The headline finding is two-layered: 86.7% of all variants have no ClinVar classification at all, and that annotation gap is nearly identical across ancestry groups (89.4% vs. 87.5%) — it's a universal problem, not an ancestry-specific one. But among the minority of variants that do get classified, non-European-private variants are called pathogenic/likely-pathogenic less than half as often as European-private ones (8.6% vs. 19.8%), and land in VUS limbo more often instead. The authors frame this as an inequity of clinical actionability, not raw uncertainty. It's not a CF paper, but its design is a near-exact template for auditing the same question in CFTR, feeding the Karen Raraigh collaboration.

Plain-English glossary

  • ACMG classification tiers — the five-level scale (pathogenic / likely pathogenic / VUS / likely benign / benign) used to describe how confident clinicians are that a variant causes disease.（中文:美国医学遗传学与基因组学学会制定的五级变异分类标准,用于表示临床医生对某个变异是否致病的信心程度。）
  • ClinVar — a public, expert- and lab-curated database of what's known about the clinical significance of specific genetic variants.（中文:一个公开的、由专家和实验室共同整理的数据库,记录特定基因变异的临床意义。）
  • gnomAD — the Genome Aggregation Database; a large public reference of how often variants appear in population samples, broken out by ancestry, with no clinical-significance calls of its own.（中文:基因组聚合数据库,一个大型公开参考库,记录变异在不同族群样本中出现的频率,本身不包含临床意义判断。）
  • MODY (maturity-onset diabetes of the young) — a family of monogenic (single-gene) diabetes subtypes, distinct from typical polygenic type 2 diabetes.（中文:青年发病的成人型糖尿病,一类由单个基因突变引起的糖尿病亚型,不同于典型的多基因2型糖尿病。）
  • Population-private variant — a variant that, in gnomAD, appears in only one ancestry group and not others.（中文:在gnomAD中只出现在某一个祖先族群、不出现在其他族群中的变异。）
  • VUS (variant of uncertain significance) — the ACMG middle tier: a variant nobody can yet confidently classify as pathogenic or benign.（中文:意义不明变异,ACMG分类中的中间档,目前无法确定其是否致病。）

Full citation

Dario P, et al. (2026). Ancestry-Stratified Variant Classification in Monogenic Diabetes Genes: Annotation Coverage and Differential Curation Burden. Annals of Human Genetics. PMID: 42634466 | DOI: 10.1111/ahg.70054

Methods / tools to lift

  • Mutually exclusive population-private variant grouping (European-private vs. non-European-private) using gnomAD's own ancestry-specific allele counts.
  • Two-step design that separates "annotation coverage" from "classification outcome among the annotated" — avoids conflating two different kinds of ancestry gap.
  • gnomAD v4.0 + ClinVar GRCh38 cross-reference on a fixed snapshot date, reported explicitly.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Serves the Karen Raraigh / CFTR variant interpretation track. Buildable artifact: cftr-ancestry-annotation-gap-quantifier — port this exact design onto CFTR: pull all CFTR variants from gnomAD v4, split into European-private/non-European-private, cross-reference against both ClinVar and CFTR2, and report the annotation-coverage gap plus the pathogenic/likely-pathogenic rate among classified variants, by ancestry group and by CFTR domain (TMD1/TMD2/NBD1/NBD2/R domain). Difficulty: S-M — all data sources are public and scriptable.

What to verify first

  • Whether the ancestry gap holds up when CFTR variants are stratified by domain, since some domains are far better functionally characterized than others.
  • How gnomAD's ancestry labels are constructed (self-reported vs. genetically inferred vs. mixed).
  • The ClinVar/CFTR2 snapshot date, since both databases update continuously.
  • Whether "population-private" is the right frame for CFTR specifically, given that CF variant frequency already differs by ancestry for demographic reasons unrelated to classification bias.
