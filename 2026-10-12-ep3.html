How did this episode land for you?

**Wei R, Ogden CL, Parsons VL, Freedman DS, Hales CM. "A method for calculating BMI z-scores and percentiles above the 95th percentile of the CDC growth charts." Annals of Human Biology, 2020.**

The 2000 CDC BMI-for-age growth charts fit their LMS smoothing curves only between the 3rd and 97th percentile — there wasn't enough tail data to go further — yet pediatric obesity is clinically defined right at the 95th percentile, just past that boundary. Extrapolating the LMS curve beyond the fitted range severely compresses z-scores in the upper tail: two 10-year-old boys with BMI 50 and 55 end up 0.03 SD apart, because the distribution's shape parameter pins both near a mathematical ceiling. This paper fixes that by refitting a half-normal tail model on a pooled 1963–2016 NHANES sample (n=8,777) restricted to children at/above the 95th percentile, smoothed by age and sex, and splicing it onto the untouched standard LMS curve below the 95th percentile. For Liz's CFFPR data-quality assignment, this is an evaluation / substrate-calibration fix — it corrects how extreme BMI values get scored, not a new flagging or adjudication rule — and the natural artifact is a drop-in post-processing function that recomputes extended BMIz only for records already at/above the 95th percentile.

Glossary

  • BMI-for-age percentile — a child's BMI ranked against a sex-and-age-specific reference population, used instead of raw BMI because healthy BMI varies by age and sex in children.（中文:儿童BMI相对于同性别同年龄参照人群的百分位排名,因为儿童健康BMI会随年龄性别变化,所以不能直接用原始BMI。）
  • CDC growth chart — the US standard pediatric reference charts (ages 2+) that convert a child's measurement into a percentile/z-score against a national reference population.（中文:美国2岁以上儿童的标准生长参照曲线图,把孩子的测量值换算成相对于全国参照人群的百分位数/z分数。）
  • 95th-percentile obesity threshold — the hard clinical cutoff: BMI-for-age at or above the 95th percentile defines pediatric obesity in the US.（中文:美国儿科"肥胖"的临床硬性分界线,即BMI-for-age达到或超过第95百分位。）
  • LMS method — the statistical technique (L = skewness/shape parameter, M = median, S = coefficient-of-variation-like spread) used to fit smooth age-specific curves and convert any BMI into a z-score or percentile.（中文:用L(偏度/形状参数)、M(中位数)、S(类似变异系数的离散度)三条曲线拟合出按年龄平滑的参照曲线,并把任意BMI换算成z分数或百分位数的统计方法。）
  • NHANES — the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, a federal US health survey with a complex, weighted sampling design; its design weights must be used in any analysis or estimates are biased.（中文:美国全国健康与营养调查,一项联邦健康调查,采用复杂的加权抽样设计,任何分析都必须使用其设计权重,否则结果会有偏差。）
  • WHO growth reference — the World Health Organization's own international growth reference, used mainly for children under 2; CDC's is the US standard for ages 2 and up, so which reference is used matters.（中文:世界卫生组织自己的国际生长参照标准,主要用于2岁以下儿童;CDC标准是美国2岁以上的通用标准,所以参照标准的选择会有影响。）

Methods/tools to lift

  • Half-normal tail model fit per 6-month age×sex cell, for values above a fixed percentile threshold.
  • Standard CDC LMS method, retained unchanged below the 95th percentile (splice-in design rather than wholesale refit).
  • NHANES survey-weighted estimation with effective-sample-size correction for clustering, plus weighted quadratic smoothing across age (R² 0.90 boys / 0.93 girls).

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Lands as an evaluation / substrate-calibration fix for Liz/CFFPR data quality — it corrects the measurement scale for extreme BMI values rather than adding a new outlier rule.

MVP: a post-processing function — input: child age, sex, raw BMI; checks if the record is at/above the sex-and-age 95th percentile and, if so, recomputes extended BMIz from the published half-normal sigma curves instead of the extrapolated LMS value; callable from any existing z-score pipeline, leaves sub-95th-percentile records untouched. Difficulty: medium.

What to verify first

  • Whether the secular-trend reference-population pooling (1963–2016) still holds, or needs refreshing with more recent NHANES cycles for a 2026 population.
  • Whether the NHANES-derived general-population reference distribution generalizes to a CF-specific population with a potentially different BMI distribution shape.
  • Whether the single-sigma half-normal is adequate across all 72 age×sex cells, or whether exception cells (e.g., boys 42–47 months) need a richer tail model.

Pipeline stage

Primary: (d) evaluation / substrate calibration — fixes how extreme BMI values are scored so downstream z-score logic doesn't saturate for the most severe patients.

Secondary: (a) candidate generation — a naive fixed-threshold z-score flagger would otherwise fail to distinguish real change from noise in this compressed region, so the fix helps indirectly.

Error types covered / not covered

Covered: z-score compression / measurement-scale artifact in the extreme upper tail of BMI-for-age.

Not covered: unit conversion errors, digit transposition, decimal-shift errors, carry-forward/stale values, or discriminating a real clinical event from a data error. This paper does not address any of these.
