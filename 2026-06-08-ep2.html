Blurb (~150w)

Carrying one pathogenic CFTR variant — being a cystic fibrosis "carrier" — has long been assumed harmless. Shugg and colleagues test that. Querying the Indiana University Adult Precision Genomics clinical database (2,141 cancer patients), they counted germline CFTR pathogenic-variant carriers and compared the observed number to the number expected by chance, using population allele frequencies from the ALFA project scaled to the cohort's ancestry. They expected ~33 carriers (1.5%) but observed 71 (3.3%) — a 2.1-fold enrichment, p < 0.001 — with the strongest overrepresentation in skin and gastrointestinal cancers. The conclusion: CFTR carriers may warrant enhanced cancer screening. The proposed direction for the CFTR project is the mirror-image question — within the full-disease CF cohort, does CFTR genotype track incident solid-tumor (esp. GI/skin) risk? — conditional on the registry actually capturing cancer diagnoses, which has not been confirmed.

Plain-English glossary

  • ALFA (Allele Frequency Aggregator): A reference catalog of how common each variant is across populations; supplies the "expected" carrier count.
  • Carrier / heterozygote: One pathogenic copy + one normal copy; does not cause recessive disease like CF, but may not be fully silent.
  • CFTR: The cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator gene; its protein is a membrane chloride channel in epithelial cells.
  • Enrichment test (observed vs. expected): Compare the carriers you actually find to the number predicted by chance from population frequencies; an excess = enrichment.
  • Epithelial cells: The lining cells of organs (skin, gut, lung) where CFTR is expressed and where most solid tumors arise.
  • Germline variant: Inherited, present in every cell from birth — as opposed to somatic.
  • Pathogenic variant (PV): A DNA change judged genuinely disease-causing (not a benign typo).
  • Solid tumor: A cancer forming a lump in an organ, vs. a blood cancer.
  • Somatic oncogenic driver mutation: A mutation the tumor acquires during life that actively pushes a cell toward cancer.
  • CFTR modulators: Drugs that help the faulty CFTR channel fold/function; by extending CF lifespan they make later-life endpoints like cancer newly relevant.
