Blurb

"Correlating CFTR Function with Clinical Features to Inform Precision Treatment of Cystic Fibrosis" (McCague et al., Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2019; senior author Garry Cutting; CF Foundation co-author) builds the quantitative bridge between how much CFTR channel function a genotype confers and how patients actually do. Assigning CFTR function to 226 unique genotypes and correlating with clinical data from 54,671 individuals in the CFTR2 project, the authors find a logarithmic relationship between genotype function and each of sweat chloride, pancreatic exocrine status, and FEV1 percent predicted (across ages 6–82). Because the curve is steep at the low-function end and flat at the high end, modest function gains help severe patients proportionally more than mild ones — and observed CFTR-modulator trial responses matched the function-phenotype predictions, giving the field reference benchmarks. For this project the paper offers something valuable: a principled way to collapse a CFTR genotype into a single clinically calibrated number that can be regressed out as a covariate, isolating residual variation where modifiers and pleiotropy would live.

Plain-English glossary

  • CF (cystic fibrosis): the recessive disease caused by damaging variants in both copies of CFTR.
  • CFTR: the gene, and the chloride-channel protein it encodes, that keeps surface fluids in lungs/pancreas/sweat glands/gut thin and flowing.
  • CFTR function: a number (roughly, percentage of normal channel activity) assignable to a genotype.
  • CFTR modulators: small-molecule drugs that improve the function of the existing faulty CFTR protein (rather than fixing the gene).
  • CFTR2 (Clinical and Functional Translation of CFTR): a large international, CF-Foundation–supported database linking CF-causing variants to clinical data.
  • Covariate: a variable accounted for in a model.
  • Cross-sectional: measurements compared across different people at one time, rather than following individuals over time.
  • FEV1 percent predicted: forced expiratory volume in one second, expressed relative to a healthy person of the same age/sex/height (100 = normal).
  • Genotype: the specific pair of variants a person carries.
  • Logarithmic relationship: one where adding a fixed amount of the input matters much more at the low end than the high end.
  • Pancreatic exocrine status / pancreatic insufficiency: whether the pancreas can still release digestive enzymes; failure is common in CF.
  • Pleiotropy: one gene influencing several traits.
  • Recessive: requires a damaging variant on both gene copies to cause disease.
  • Sweat chloride concentration: the classic CF test; broken CFTR leaves more salt in sweat.
