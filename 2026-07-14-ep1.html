Blurb

LocalVar is a variant-collection manager built at the University of Utah / Huntsman Cancer Institute for a problem that has nothing to do with sequencing and everything to do with bookkeeping: a locally curated list of genetic variants rots. It rots in three independent ways — two rows turn out to be synonyms for the same physical change (F508del, p.Phe508del, c.1521_1523delCTT, ΔF508 are one variant and four strings); the correct HGVS expression for a variant changes over time as transcripts, specs and normalization rules move underneath it; and the interpretation in ClinVar gets revised while your local copy does not. LocalVar attacks all three: autocomplete to pre-empt duplicate entry, auto-detection of duplicate and synonymous records, asynchronous (human-in-the-loop, never silently overwriting) suggestions of HGVS-expression and interpretation updates from public ClinVar files, full edit history, and export of the collection (.csv), the edit history (.json), and — the best idea in the paper — the HGVS synonym bins (.json). It was informed by a real Huntsman variant dictionary watched over two years, which is the load-bearing detail: drift is a time-series phenomenon and cannot be seen in a snapshot. The abstract reports no evaluation, and that gap is the opening. Where it lands: Karen / variant interpretation. Buildable artifact: cftr-variant-drift-monitor — measure how much drift actually exists in CFTR notation, which nobody has done.

Plain-English glossary

  • ClinVar — NIH's public archive where labs submit variants together with their assessed clinical significance (pathogenic → benign), which is revised over time as evidence accumulates.（中文：NIH 的公共变异数据库，收录各实验室提交的变异及其临床意义评估，随证据积累会被修订。）
  • CFTR2 — the CF-specific variant curation resource, with its own classifications and functional data; the interpretation authority the CF field actually uses, and not the same thing as ClinVar.（中文：CF 领域专用的变异策展资源，有自己的分类和功能数据，是 CF 界真正使用的解读权威，与 ClinVar 不是一回事。）
  • Equivalence class / synonym bin — modeling a variant as a set of strings that all denote the same physical change, rather than as one string; the bin is the identifier, the strings are members.（中文：把一个变异建模为"一组都指向同一物理变化的字符串"的集合，而不是单个字符串；"箱"才是标识符，字符串只是成员。）
  • HGVS — the standardized convention for writing a variant as a string; always has a reference sequence (the accession before the colon), a coordinate system (c. coding DNA, g. genomic, p. protein, r. RNA), and a description of the change.（中文：把遗传变异写成字符串的标准化命名规范；总是包含参考序列、坐标系统和变化描述三部分。）
  • Length polymorphism — a variant that changes the number of repeated units (e.g. the CFTR poly-T and TG tracts) rather than substituting one base for another; notoriously awkward to express unambiguously in HGVS, and a CF-specific trap.（中文：改变重复单元数量的变异（如 CFTR 的 poly-T 和 TG 序列），而非碱基替换；用 HGVS 无歧义地表达它出了名地困难，是 CF 特有的陷阱。）
  • Reclassification — the movement of a variant's clinical significance call over time, most often a VUS becoming likely pathogenic as evidence accrues; a local copy of a classification is a snapshot, not a fact.（中文：变异临床意义判定随时间的变动，最常见的是 VUS 随证据积累变为"可能致病"；本地保存的分类是一张快照，不是事实。）
  • Reference sequence / transcript — the specific accessioned version of the gene's sequence you are counting coordinates on; change it and the same physical variant gets a different string.（中文：你用来数坐标的、带版本号的基因序列；换一个转录本，同一个物理变异就会得到不同的字符串。）
  • VUS (variant of uncertain significance) — a variant with insufficient evidence to call pathogenic or benign; not a permanent state, which is exactly why local copies go stale.（中文：证据不足以判定为致病或良性的变异；它不是永久状态，这正是本地副本会过期的原因。）

Methods / tools to lift

  • Synonym binning — model the variant as an equivalence class of strings, not as a string. This is the single most portable idea in the paper.
  • Asynchronous, suggest-don't-sync updating — when the public source changes, propose a change and keep a human in the loop; never silently overwrite a clinically-curated local record. Correct for a clinical artifact; counterintuitive to a data engineer whose instinct is to sync.
  • Duplicate pre-emption at entry time — autocomplete against the existing collection before a new row can be created. Cheapest possible data-quality control: prevent, don't reconcile.
  • Edit-history as a first-class exportable object (.json) — the history is the evidence that drift happened.
  • Watching a real collection over two years — the study design, not the software, is what makes drift visible.
  • ClinVar public release files as the drift oracle (and, for CF, this must be supplemented by CFTR2 — see pushback).

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Serves: Karen / variant interpretation. This is the architecture for the variant-notation-harmonization opening, and it conspicuously leaves the empirical question unanswered — which is where the contribution is.

cftr-variant-drift-monitor (difficulty M) — takes a CSV of CFTR variant strings as they are actually written (legacy / protein / coding-DNA, mixed); normalizes each, groups them into synonym bins, and emits three change logs against a pinned ClinVar release and a pinned CFTR2 release: (1) bin collisions — rows that are secretly the same variant; (2) expression drift — strings that no longer match the current canonical form; (3) interpretation drift — classifications that have moved since a chosen date. The deliverable is not the tool, it's the number: the measured drift rate in CFTR variant notation over N years.

What to verify first

  • Which normalization library, pinned to which spec version. The tool that detects drift is itself a source of drift; an unpinned normalizer manufactures "changes" that are only version bumps in your own dependency.
  • Which transcript CFTR2 and ClinVar each actually use today. If they differ, normalizing to one silently disconnects you from the other → emit both and record the mapping.
  • Poly-T / TG tracts are length polymorphisms, not substitutions. Legacy CF notation for them may have no single unambiguous HGVS form. These rows fail quietly — and they are the most interesting rows in the file.
  • Is the drift signal even large enough to justify a tool? If CFTR classifications turn out to be highly stable, "there is no drift problem in CFTR" is the honest finding — and still a publishable sentence.
  • ClinVar alone is the wrong oracle for CF. The paper's ClinVar-only design is defensible for a cancer institute and insufficient for CFTR.
  • We have not seen the full text. Any claim about LocalVar's implementation, evaluation, or performance beyond the six capabilities listed in the abstract is unverified.
