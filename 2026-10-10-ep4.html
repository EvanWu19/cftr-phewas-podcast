Ep. 4 — Unsupervised anomaly detection of implausible EHRs in cancer registries

Track C, second episode of the day (registry data quality / anthropometric outlier detection). This episode covers a different, fully-documented paper from episode 3's title-only Hagedorn et al. piece on weight entry errors.

Blurb

Röchner and Rothlauf (BMC Medical Research Methodology, 2023) tackle a problem with the same shape as Liz's height/weight-flagging assignment, but on categorical rather than numeric data: can unsupervised anomaly detection find implausible electronic health records in a cancer registry without any labeled training set? They test two methods — FindFPOF (a pattern-frequency scorer) and an autoencoder (a reconstruction-error scorer) — against a random-selection baseline, on 21,104 real diagnostic records (breast, colorectal, prostate tumors; 16 categorical variables each) from the Cancer Registry Rhineland-Palatinate in Germany. Unlike most anomaly-detection papers, which test on synthetic/injected anomalies, this one has seven domain experts blindly adjudicate 785 real, distinct flagged-and-random records — making it one of the few real-world, human-adjudicated evaluations of this kind in the literature. It lands as a secondary candidate-generation signal (operating on categorical encounter metadata rather than numeric values) and, more durably, as a transferable evaluation design: a small random stratum plus blinded expert adjudication, which is how the paper computes honest sensitivity/specificity numbers that its model-flagged-only samples can't provide. The buildable artifact is a FindFPOF-style pattern-frequency scorer over categorical fields that typically accompany a height/weight encounter — visit type, measurement method/device, units-as-recorded — as a complementary signal, framed strictly as method transfer, not a claim about CFFPR's actual pipeline.

Glossary

  • Anomaly detection — Finding observations that are rare and different from the rest of a dataset, without needing labeled examples of what "bad" looks like.（中文：在没有标注数据的情况下,找出数据集中稀有且与众不同的观测值。）
  • Autoencoder (anomaly scoring) — A neural network trained to compress and reconstruct its input; records with high reconstruction error are flagged as anomalous because rare/unusual records are harder to reconstruct well.（中文：一种训练来压缩并重建输入数据的神经网络;重构误差高的记录被标记为异常,因为稀有或不寻常的记录更难被准确重建。）
  • Categorical variable — A field whose values are discrete labels with no natural numeric order (e.g., tumor site, sex), as opposed to a continuous numeric measurement.（中文：取值为离散标签、没有自然数值顺序的字段,例如肿瘤部位、性别,区别于连续数值型测量。）
  • Deterministic rule-based check — A hardcoded validity rule written by domain experts (e.g., "this diagnosis code requires this stage field") used for data quality before/alongside machine-learning methods.（中文：由领域专家编写的硬编码有效性规则，例如"这个诊断编码要求对应这个分期字段"，常在使用机器学习方法之前或同时用于数据质量控制。）
  • FindFPOF (Frequent Pattern Outlier Factor) — A pattern-based anomaly scoring algorithm for categorical data: it scores a record by how many "frequent patterns" (common value combinations) it matches — few matches means anomalous.（中文：一种针对分类数据的、基于模式频率的异常评分算法:根据一条记录匹配了多少"频繁模式"（常见值组合）来打分，匹配得少说明异常。）
  • ICD-10 / ICD-O — Standard diagnostic coding systems: ICD-10 classifies disease generally; ICD-O additionally codes tumor morphology (cell type/behavior) and topography (anatomical site) specifically for cancer registries.（中文：标准诊断编码系统:ICD-10是通用疾病分类;ICD-O则专门为癌症注册库编码肿瘤形态学（细胞类型/行为）和解剖部位。）
  • Implausible record — A record whose combination of individually-valid values doesn't agree with general medical knowledge (e.g., a male patient coded with a breast tumor); distinct from a record containing an outright invalid value.（中文：一条记录中各个字段的取值本身都合法，但组合起来与一般医学常识不符（例如男性患者被编码为乳腺肿瘤），区别于包含明显无效值的记录。）
  • Precision (in this paper) — The percentage of records in a flagged sample that domain experts confirmed were actually implausible.（中文：在被标记的样本中，经领域专家确认确实不合理的记录所占的百分比。）
  • Random-stratum + blinded-expert-adjudication design — An evaluation method: draw a small, fully random sample (not model-flagged), have domain experts review it without knowing which method (if any) flagged a record, and use only that stratum to compute sensitivity/specificity.（中文：一种评估方法:抽取一个完全随机（非模型标记）的小样本，让领域专家在不知道记录是否被某方法标记的情况下进行评审，并只用这一层样本来计算敏感性和特异性。）
  • Sensitivity / specificity (on the random stratum) — Here, computable only on the fully-labeled random 300-record sample, since that's the only stratum where both flagged and non-flagged records were checked by experts.（中文：在本文中，敏感性和特异性只能在完全标注的随机300条样本上计算，因为只有在这一层里，被标记和未被标记的记录都经过了专家核实。）
  • TNM staging — A standard cancer staging system describing Tumor size/extent (T), lymph Node involvement (N), and distant Metastasis (M).（中文：一种标准的癌症分期系统，描述肿瘤范围（T）、淋巴结受累情况（N）和远处转移（M）。）

Methods/tools to lift

  • FindFPOF (Frequent Pattern Outlier Factor) — pattern-frequency anomaly scoring for categorical data; parameters used: max pattern length 5, min frequency 10% (13,009 frequent patterns found on 21,104 records).
  • Autoencoder-based anomaly scoring — reconstruction-error scoring for categorical (one-hot encoded) data; architecture used: 3 hidden layers (16-8-16), ReLU, dropout 0.2, Adam (lr 0.001), batch size 32, 20 epochs, binary cross-entropy loss.
  • Random-stratum + blinded-expert-adjudication evaluation design — the transferable piece: a small, fully random sample reviewed blind to flagging method, used to compute sensitivity/specificity that a model-flagged-only sample cannot support.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Pipeline stage: (a) candidate generation [secondary categorical signal, complementary to numeric residual-based flagging] / (d) evaluation [random-stratum + blinded-adjudication design].

Buildable artifact — Categorical pattern-frequency anomaly scorer for encounter metadata.

MVP: Input is the categorical fields that typically accompany a height/weight encounter — visit type, measurement method/device, units-as-recorded, assurance/data-source flags. The scorer computes a FindFPOF-style frequent-pattern score per record and ranks encounters by how rare their categorical combination is, as a complementary signal alongside numeric spline/mixed-model residual flags — not a replacement for them. Framed strictly as method transfer; makes no claim about CFFPR's actual current pipeline.

Difficulty: S — a well-documented, parameter-light classical algorithm (itemset mining), no training loop, no GPU required.

What to verify first

  • Whether FindFPOF's frequent-pattern approach generalizes to a very different categorical schema (cancer-registry tumor fields vs. whatever visit/encounter metadata actually accompanies an anthropometric reading) — schema, cardinality, and correlation structure could differ substantially.
  • How costly/feasible it would be to replicate the paper's domain-expert-adjudicated random-stratum evaluation design at registry scale (seven experts reviewing even a few hundred records is real clinical time; unclear cost at a multi-million-encounter registry).
  • Whether the paper's headline 28% precision numbers (from model-flagged-only samples) would hold up under a fully random audit, rather than only being checked within the top-300 flagged by each method.

Error types covered / not covered

Covered: implausible categorical combinations — e.g., inconsistent disease/procedure/stage combinations, or demographically implausible disease-site pairings (the sex + tumor-localization example used throughout).

Not covered: this paper says nothing about numeric unit-conversion errors, decimal-shift errors, or digit-transposition errors in continuous measurements like height/weight — those are a different error class, addressed by spline/mixed-model residual methods, not by this paper's categorical pattern-frequency or autoencoder approach.
