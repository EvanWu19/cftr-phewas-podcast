Blurb

Daymont, Ross, Russell Localio, Fiks, Wasserman, and Grundmeier (Penn State / Penn / CHOP, JAMIA, 2017) built the canonical automated method for flagging implausible pediatric height and weight values in EHR data — the paper behind the widely used growthcleanr package. The core method compares each measurement, expressed as a standard-deviation score, against a weighted moving average built from that same child's other measurements over time (a within-child, EWMA-style reference rather than a population norm). Tested on 2,000,595 measurements from 280,610 patients (ages 1–21) across 46 primary care sites, it flagged 3.8% of weight and 4.5% of height values, with sensitivity 97% (95% CI 94–99%) and specificity 90% (95% CI 85–94%) against physician judgment on a stratified random 400-measurement sample, and caught 95%/98% of simulated weight/height errors. This is the direct EWMA-based comparator to a spline mixed-effects candidate generator for the Liz assignment. Buildable artifact: growthcleanr-benchmark-harness (M-difficulty), comparing within-child EWMA flagging against a spline mixed-effects approach on synthetic data.

Plain-English glossary

  • Growth chart — a plot of a child's height, weight, or BMI against age relative to a reference population.（中文：将儿童的身高、体重或BMI相对参照人群按年龄绘制的曲线图。）
  • Standard deviation score (z-score / SDS) — how far an individual measurement sits from the expected value for a given age and sex, in standard-deviation units, relative to a reference distribution.（中文：以标准差为单位,衡量某次测量值偏离特定年龄和性别预期值的程度,相对于某个参照分布。）
  • CDC growth charts vs. WHO growth standards — the two dominant pediatric reference standards; CDC (US survey data, typically age 2+) and WHO (multi-country healthy breastfed-infant sample, youngest ages) are not interchangeable.（中文：儿科两套主流参照标准;CDC基于美国调查数据,通常用于2岁以上;WHO基于多国健康母乳喂养婴儿样本,用于最小年龄段;二者不可互换。）
  • EWMA / weighted moving average (as used here) — comparing each measurement to a weighted average of that same child's other measurements over time, with nearer-in-time points weighted more heavily — a within-patient reference rather than a population-level model.（中文：将每次测量与同一儿童其他测量值的加权平均进行比较,时间上更接近的点权重更高——一种基于患者自身的参照,而非群体层面的模型。）
  • Candidate generation (pipeline stage) — the first stage of an outlier-detection pipeline: flagging which records are worth further scrutiny, as distinct from adjudicating whether a flagged record is truly an error.（中文：离群值检测流程的第一阶段:标记哪些记录值得进一步审查,区别于判定被标记的记录是否真的是错误的裁定阶段。）

  • The two-leg evaluation design: (1) stratified random sample compared against physician-reviewer ground truth, (2) accuracy against a dataset with deliberately simulated errors.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Pipeline stage: (a) candidate generation. growthcleanr's within-child, EWMA-style comparison is a real, validated alternative to a spline mixed-effects residual approach — cheaper to compute, no cohort-wide model fit required, but likely weaker for sparse-data patients where mixed-effects partial pooling is strongest. Buildable artifact: growthcleanr-benchmark-harness — implement the EWMA-style flagging logic against synthetic longitudinal height/weight data with injected simulated errors (per the paper's own two-leg design), and compare flag concordance/sensitivity/specificity against a spline mixed-effects candidate generator on the same synthetic data. Entirely synthetic/de-identified data; no claims about CFFPR's actual records or flags. Difficulty: M.

What to verify first

  • What error types were injected into the simulated-error dataset and at what rate, before assuming the 95%/98% catch rates generalize to registry-specific error modes (unit confusion, decimal-place shifts, transposition).
  • Why exclusion rate varied widely by primary care site — data-entry practice, case-mix, or something else — which needs the full methods text.
  • How a within-child moving-average reference handles a real, non-error trajectory inflection (e.g., a modulator-era weight jump) versus a true data error.
  • growthcleanr's current maintained-package defaults and documented edge-case handling, rather than re-deriving the method from the 2017 abstract alone.
  • This paper says nothing about CFFPR's current cleaning rules or what they do or don't catch — not assumed either way.

Error types covered / not covered

Covered (per abstract): outright implausible single values; values recorded repeatedly without remeasurement (stale carry-forward). Not stated in the abstract: unit conversion errors, digit transposition, decimal-place shift, or explicit real-clinical-event-vs-error discrimination — these would need the full methods text or the maintained package's documentation to confirm.
