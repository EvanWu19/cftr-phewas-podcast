Ep1 (Track A) — Homology-based variant-effect predictors break down on CYP pharmacogenes

Blurb. AlphaMissense and ESM-2 both score variants by evolutionary conservation — a paradigm that works for classic disease genes but breaks down on cytochrome P450 (CYP) pharmacogenes, which evolved for functional diversity rather than strict conservation. Xu, Samori, Nayar, and Altman (Stanford, bioRxiv preprint, 2026) show AlphaMissense assigns CYP variants to its "ambiguous" class at nearly twice the proteome-wide rate, and within that class its score is essentially uncorrelated with measured enzymatic activity (Spearman's rho 0.069 vs. 0.339–0.414 in the benign/pathogenic classes). A CYP2C9-specific k-nearest-neighbors model over ESM-2 embeddings, ensembled with AlphaMissense and ESM-2, recovers most of the lost signal against biochemical activity — but that accuracy does not transfer to clinical (ClinVar) or pharmacogenetic (PharmVar) labels, several of which correlate negatively. The likely culprit: CYP variant effects are substrate-dependent, and no current predictor conditions on which drug is involved. For CFTR, where modulator response is likewise a drug-binding phenomenon rather than a simple conservation signal, this is a caution to test before leaning on ambiguous-class variant scores for modulator-relevant variants — landing on the Karen Raraigh variant-interpretation track as a buildable audit rather than a tool to adopt outright.

Plain-English glossary

  • AlphaMissense — A deep-learning variant-effect predictor built on AlphaFold, fine-tuned on human/primate population-frequency data, that scores missense variants for pathogenicity.（中文：一种基于AlphaFold构建、利用人类和灵长类种群频率数据微调的深度学习变异效应预测模型，用于评估错义变异的致病性。）
  • ESM-2 — A protein language model trained by masked-language modeling across millions of protein sequences; repurposed here as a zero-shot variant-effect predictor via its masked marginal probability (MMP).（中文：一种通过在数百万条蛋白质序列上进行掩码语言建模训练出的蛋白质语言模型；本文中被用作零样本变异效应预测工具，依据其掩码边际概率(MMP)打分。）
  • Deep mutational scanning (DMS) — A high-throughput experimental method that measures the functional effect (e.g., enzymatic activity) of thousands of variants in parallel.（中文：一种高通量实验方法，可并行测量成千上万个变异的功能效应，例如酶活性。）
  • Homology-based / conservation-based prediction — Scoring a variant by how much it disrupts a protein position that has been kept unchanged across evolution, on the assumption that conserved positions are functionally important.（中文：一种根据变异对某个在进化中保持不变的蛋白质位点造成多大破坏来打分的方法，其前提假设是保守位点具有重要功能。）
  • Pharmacogene — A gene whose variants affect how a person metabolizes or responds to drugs, as opposed to causing disease on their own.（中文：一类变异会影响个体药物代谢或反应的基因，不同于直接致病的基因。）
  • PharmVar — A curated database of pharmacogene "star alleles" with assigned function classes (e.g., no function, decreased function, normal function) and evidence tiers.（中文：一个收录药物基因"星型等位基因"的数据库，为每个等位基因标注功能分类（如无功能、功能降低、正常功能）及证据等级。）
  • ClinVar / ACMG framework — A public archive of variant-disease relationships, classified using the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) evidentiary framework (e.g., pathogenic, likely benign, uncertain significance).（中文：一个收录变异与疾病关系的公共数据库，采用美国医学遗传学与基因组学学会(ACMG)的证据框架进行分类，如致病、可能良性、意义未明等。）
  • ClinPGx (formerly PharmGKB) — A literature-curated database of variant-drug associations, capturing how a specific variant affects response to a specific drug.（中文：一个通过文献整理构建的变异-药物关联数据库，记录特定变异如何影响个体对特定药物的反应，前身为PharmGKB。）
  • Substrate-conditioned prediction — A proposed approach where a variant's predicted effect depends on which drug (substrate) is being considered, rather than producing one substrate-agnostic score.（中文：一种设想中的预测方法，变异的预测效应会随所涉及的药物（底物）而变化，而不是给出一个与底物无关的单一分数。）
  • Star allele — A named, catalogued haplotype of a pharmacogene (e.g., CYP2C93) with an assigned functional classification.（中文：药物基因的一种已命名、已编目的单倍型（如CYP2C93），带有对应的功能分类。）

Full citation. Xu H, Samori I, Nayar G, Altman RB. "Homology-Based Variant-Effect Predictors Break Down on Cytochrome P450 Pharmacogenes." bioRxiv (preprint), 2026. PMID 42620075 | PMC13484531 | DOI: 10.64898/2026.07.30.741616

Methods/tools to lift

  • AlphaMissense — pathogenicity score + discrete class label (likely benign / ambiguous / likely pathogenic) usable as an off-the-shelf variant screen.
  • ESM-2 masked marginal probability (MMP) — zero-shot variant-effect score derivable from any protein sequence with no fine-tuning.
  • Gene-specific k-nearest-neighbors regression over protein-language-model embeddings — a lightweight way to specialize a general-purpose embedding to one protein's activity landscape.
  • Isotonic/sigmoid calibration — recalibrating an arbitrary predictor score onto a measured biological scale via cross-validated monotone regression.
  • Ridge-regression meta-learner ensembling — combining multiple variant-effect signals with inner cross-validation to avoid leakage.

Where it lands + buildable artifact. Serves Karen Raraigh / CFTR variant interpretation (method only). MVP: cftr-ambiguous-class-modulator-audit — pull every CFTR missense variant AlphaMissense assigns to its ambiguous class, check whether they cluster near known modulator-binding regions the same way CYP's binding-site-distance signal vanished specifically in that class, and cross-reference against published in vitro modulator-response data by variant. Input: CFTR variant list with AlphaMissense classes + published CFTR modulator functional data. Difficulty: M.

What to verify first

  • This is a bioRxiv preprint, not yet peer-reviewed — treat specific numbers as provisional.
  • All results past the ambiguous-class enrichment finding rest on a single DMS screen for one gene (CYP2C9); clinical-label correlations rest on as few as 7–9 labeled variants.
  • Whether CFTR's own conservation profile resembles a CYP (loosely conserved, functionally diverse) or a tightly conserved channel gene is itself unresolved — the ambiguous-class failure mode should not be assumed to transfer without checking this first.
  • No claims here about how CFFPR's own variant-classification pipeline currently handles ambiguous-class calls.
