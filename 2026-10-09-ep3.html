Blurb

This episode covers a short JAMIA paper on a problem adjacent to Runyu's CFFPR flagging task but attacked from a different angle: instead of statistical outlier detection on raw height/weight values, the authors try to infer and correct measurement-unit errors (cm vs. inches, kg vs. lb) across the many partner sites feeding into the All of Us (AoU) research cohort. This paper is abstract-only for us — the full text was not retrievable despite having a PMCID — so everything here is bounded by the abstract: three named ideas (distribution-shape unit inference, obesity-exception plausibility bounds, within-person longitudinal deviation checks), two headline numbers (15.8% of heights and 22.4% of weights missing unit-type info), and no reported sensitivity/PPV/post-correction flag-rate figures. The buildable takeaway is a lightweight per-source unit-plausibility distribution checker — a tool to sanity-check whether a data source's value distribution actually matches its stated unit.

Plain-English glossary

  • All of Us (AoU) — a large NIH-funded, multi-site precision-medicine research cohort in the US that aggregates EHR data from many different partner health systems rather than one hospital's records.（中文：美国NIH资助的大型多站点精准医学队列，从众多合作医疗系统汇总电子健康档案数据。）
  • AoU Researcher Workbench — the AoU program's secure cloud analysis environment where approved researchers query the aggregated data without downloading it locally.（中文：All of Us项目的安全云端分析环境，研究者可在其中查询汇总数据而不下载到本地。）
  • EHR (electronic health record) — the digital medical record system a clinic or hospital uses to store patient data, including height/weight measurements.（中文：医疗机构用于存储患者数据（包括身高体重测量值）的电子病历系统。）
  • Site-level heterogeneity — the general phenomenon in multi-site EHR aggregation where different sites use different vendors, templates, and local conventions, making data from one site not directly comparable to another without reconciliation.（中文：多站点EHR数据汇总中，不同站点使用不同厂商、模板和本地惯例，导致数据在没有协调处理前无法直接比较的现象。）
  • Unit-type inference from distribution shape — inferring whether a site recorded height in cm vs. inches (or weight in kg vs. lb) by examining the shape of that site's own value distribution, rather than trusting a (possibly missing or wrong) unit label.（中文：通过观察一个站点自身数值分布的形状，而不是依赖可能缺失或错误的单位标签，来推断该站点记录身高用厘米还是英寸、体重用公斤还是磅。）
  • Within-person (longitudinal) consistency check — a QC concept that flags a measurement as suspect when it deviates unusually far from that same individual's own prior recorded values over time, rather than comparing it to population norms.（中文：一种质量控制概念，当某次测量值明显偏离同一个人自己以往的历史记录时将其标记为可疑，而不是与人群常规范围比较。）

Methods/tools to lift

  • Treat a data source's recorded unit label as untrusted metadata to be checked, not a fact to rely on.
  • Infer a plausible measurement unit from the shape of a source's own value distribution rather than asserting it a priori.
  • Carve out plausibility-bound exceptions for clinical conditions that can legitimately produce extreme values (e.g., obesity), so extreme-but-real values aren't misclassified as errors.
  • Use within-person longitudinal history as a secondary consistency check, independent of population-level plausibility bounds.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Primarily (a) candidate generation — a unit-inference-from-shape step run upstream could catch and correct unit mix-ups before a spline/mixed-effects model on raw values ever has a chance to misclassify them as biological outliers. Secondarily (c) review budget — resolving an entire class of unit-mix-up flags programmatically, before any human sees them, is the kind of thing that could shrink review volume for that error class specifically. It also touches (b) adjudication (distinguishing "wrong unit" from "true extreme value via exception" gives reviewers a second axis) and (e) vocabulary/framing (distrust-the-metadata, infer-from-shape). It does not demonstrate (d) evaluation without a gold standard — that capability is not shown in this abstract.

Buildable MVP: A per-source unit-plausibility distribution checker. Input: raw height/weight values grouped by data source (site, clinic, or feed). Output: a flagged list of sources whose distribution shape looks inconsistent with the stated unit, or bimodal in a way suggesting mixed conventions. Difficulty: S.

What to verify first

  • Whether unit ambiguity (cm/in, kg/lb) is a realistic failure mode at all inside a single-registry-system pipeline, versus the multi-EHR-vendor aggregation setting this paper addresses.
  • Whether the obesity-exception plausibility bounds are specified anywhere beyond this abstract.
  • How the within-person deviation thresholds were chosen.
  • That the full paper would be needed to see the actual decision rule connecting the three described checks before building anything beyond a prototype.

Pipeline stage

(a) candidate generation [primary]; (c) review budget [secondary]; touches (b) adjudication and (e) vocabulary/framing; (d) evaluation-without-gold-standard not supported by this paper.

Error types covered / not covered

Covered: unit mix-up / mislabeling (cm vs. inches, kg vs. lb) at the site or source level; extreme-but-real values associated with known clinical conditions (obesity exception); within-person deviation from one's own longitudinal history.

Not covered (per this abstract): digit transposition, decimal-place shift errors, carry-forward/stale-value errors, confusion between a real clinical event and a data artifact, and any validated measure of correction accuracy (no sensitivity, PPV, or ground-truth comparison reported).
