How did this episode land for you?

Blurb

Shi, Korsiak, and Roth (2018, Annals of Epidemiology) tackle a specific gap in longitudinal childhood anthropometric data cleaning: cross-sectional cutoffs — whether WHO Child Growth Standards or a study population's own observed distribution — ignore that a child's own trajectory across serial visits is itself a plausibility signal. Building on Yang et al.'s conditional growth percentile method (a population-conditioned approach that cannot evaluate a child's first measurement), this paper proposes an individual-level alternative: fit a simple regression of each child's own z-scores, or raw measurements against square-root of age, then flag points using jackknife (externally studentized) residuals, which react more strongly to single outliers than ordinary standardized residuals. Validated on the MDIG trial (1,211 Bangladeshi infants) using artificially injected errors as a true gold standard, the method identifies the wrong point in a biologically-implausible pair 84–92% of the time, though overall sensitivity for any outlier at the standard cutoff is modest (11–14%) with high specificity (97–98%).

Plain-English Glossary (alphabetical)

  • BIV (biologically implausible value) — a term of art for a measurement that is physically implausible given known human growth biology (e.g., a decrease in length between visits), not just statistically unusual. 生物学上不合理值——指从人体生长规律来看根本不可能出现的测量值，而不仅仅是统计上少见的值。
  • Jackknife residual (externally studentized residual) — a residual computed by refitting the model with that point excluded, so the error estimate isn't inflated by the very point being judged; reacts more strongly to single outliers than an ordinary standardized residual. Jackknife residual（留一法学生化残差）——通过剔除该点后重新拟合模型来计算残差，使误差估计不被待判断的点本身拉高；对单个异常点比普通标准化残差更敏感。
  • Kappa — a statistic measuring agreement (here, between the method's error identification and the true injected error) beyond chance. kappa——衡量一致性（此处指方法识别出的错误与真实注入错误之间的一致程度）的统计量，扣除偶然一致的部分。
  • MDIG (Maternal Vitamin D for Infant Growth) — the vitamin D supplementation RCT in Dhaka, Bangladesh (1,211 infants) used as the validation cohort. MDIG（孕期维生素D补充婴儿生长试验）——在孟加拉国达卡开展的维生素D补充随机对照试验，纳入1211名婴儿，作为本文的验证队列。
  • SD (standard deviation) — used both as the WHO reference-population spread (basis of z-scores) and as the unit of the flagging cutoffs (±5/±6). SD（标准差）——既用作WHO参考人群的离散程度（z-score的基础），也用作标记截断值（正负5/正负6）的单位。
  • WHO Child Growth Standards — the fixed age/sex growth reference charts derived from the WHO Multicentre Growth Reference Study; basis of conventional cross-sectional cutoffs. WHO儿童生长标准——源自WHO多中心生长参考研究的固定年龄性别生长参考图表，是传统横断面截断值的基础。
  • WHO Multicentre Growth Reference Study — the prospective multi-country study of healthy, well-nourished children that established the WHO Child Growth Standards reference population. WHO多中心生长参考研究——追踪多国健康、营养良好儿童的前瞻性研究，建立了WHO儿童生长标准的参考人群。
  • z-score (pediatric growth) — how many SDs a measurement sits from the age/sex-specific reference median. z-score（儿科生长）——某测量值偏离特定年龄性别参考中位数的标准差数。

Methods/Tools to Lift

  • Per-child (individual-level) regression of z-score, or raw measurement against sqrt(age), as the plausibility model — no population model required.
  • Jackknife (externally studentized) residual computation per longitudinal point.
  • Reliability-scaled cutoff rule: ±5 primary, widened to ±6 for children with only 4-5 total measurements or for a child's first point.
  • Minimum-data gate: require ≥4 serial measurements per child before the model runs.
  • Artificial-error-injection validation design (magnitude drawn from reference-population measurement SDs) as a way to generate a true sensitivity/specificity answer key.
  • Four-way method benchmarking: individual jackknife (z-score vs. raw) vs. population-conditioned percentile method vs. conventional cross-sectional cutoffs.

Where It Lands + Buildable Artifact

Pipeline stage: primarily (a) candidate generation — an individual-trajectory/residual-based alternative or complement to population-conditioned or population-cutoff candidate rules; also touches (d) evaluation, via its clean simulated-ground-truth design as a template for measuring a flagging method's real sensitivity/specificity rather than eyeballing flag rates. No claims made here about the registry's actual current cleaning rules.

MVP: a synthetic-data generator + per-child regression/jackknife-residual scorer + injected-error scoring harness. Difficulty: S.

What to Verify First

  • Artificial random-shift errors may not resemble real registry data-entry error patterns (transcription flips, unit conversion, decimal shifts).
  • A genuine rapid true clinical change could look statistically identical to a simulated random-shift error — a flag is not automatically a data error.
  • Method requires ≥4 visits per child and cannot evaluate a child's first 3-4 measurements at all — a structural blind spot, not a tunable parameter.
  • The z-score and raw-measurement variants disagree substantially on which specific points they flag despite similar overall flag rates (0.48% overlap vs. ~1.5% each flagged) — this paper does not resolve which to trust.
  • Nothing here should be read as a claim about CFFPR's actual data or current cleaning-rule internals.

Pipeline Stage

(a) Candidate generation — primary. (d) Evaluation — secondary, via the simulated-ground-truth validation template.

Error Types Covered / Not Covered

Covers: artificial random-magnitude shift errors (magnitude ~ Normal(0, WHO reference SD)); biologically-implausible decrease-between-consecutive-points errors (the pair-identification task).

Does not cover: unit-conversion errors; decimal-shift errors; carry-forward/duplicate-value errors; transcription-flip errors; real-clinical-event vs. error confusion (e.g., a genuine rapid growth change misread as an error); errors occurring in a child's first 3-4 measurements; any child with fewer than 4 total measurements.
