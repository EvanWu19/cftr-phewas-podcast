Ep 3 — Track C (CF modifier-gene catalog) — 2026-07-21

Blurb

Ward and colleagues run the first genomic study of pain in cystic fibrosis — a phenotype with high reported prevalence and severity in people with CF, and one that modulator therapies (ivacaftor, lumacaftor, tezacaftor, elexacaftor) are reported not to affect. That last point is the strategic reason it belongs in the catalog: modulators have compressed the classical severity measures toward a ceiling, so a phenotype they do not touch is a phenotype where the variance a modifier study needs is still present. The pipeline is entirely in silico — five curated databases, a literature scan, four GEO expression datasets, CTD pathway mapping, STRING/GeneMANIA networks, DGIdb drug–gene lookup — and yields seven candidates: CTRC, SPINK1, TNF, ABCB1, PRSS1, TGFB1, plus CFTR. But the pipeline is structurally circular: genes were selected by co-occurrence in curated literature, then corroborated by a STRING network at medium confidence that includes text-mining edges drawn from that same literature. And the study's one orthogonal, measurement-based arm — the four GEO datasets — returned zero overlapping genes between any pain dataset and zero with the CF dataset, and the headline gene list was unaffected. The candidate worth carrying forward is ABCB1, because its mechanism is pharmacokinetic rather than inflammatory and it predicts an analgesic-response difference, which literature bias does not explain away. Buildable artifact: a literature-bias-deflated gene prioritiser.

Plain-English glossary

  • Allodynia — pain produced by a stimulus that should not hurt at all, such as light touch.（中文：异常性疼痛——本不该引起疼痛的刺激（如轻触）却造成疼痛。）
  • CTRC / SPINK1 / PRSS1 — the well-established chronic-pancreatitis modifier genes: PRSS1 encodes cationic trypsinogen (the trypsin precursor), SPINK1 is a trypsin inhibitor, and CTRC regulates trypsinogen activation and degradation.（中文：CTRC / SPINK1 / PRSS1——公认的慢性胰腺炎修饰基因：PRSS1 编码阳离子胰蛋白酶原（胰蛋白酶前体），SPINK1 是胰蛋白酶抑制剂，CTRC 调控胰蛋白酶原的激活与降解。）
  • Hub gene — a node in a network with an unusually high number of edges; the assumption is that disrupting a highly connected node has widespread downstream effects.（中文：枢纽基因——网络中边数异常多的节点；其假设是破坏高度连接的节点会产生广泛的下游效应。）
  • Hyperalgesia — increased sensitivity to a stimulus that is genuinely painful.（中文：痛觉过敏——对本身确实引起疼痛的刺激反应过度。）
  • In silico gene discovery — generating a candidate gene list without producing any new data, by intersecting existing curated databases, published gene lists, and public expression datasets. Output is a hypothesis list, not evidence.（中文：计算机模拟的基因发现——不产生任何新数据，通过对现有注释数据库、已发表基因列表和公开表达数据集取交集来生成候选基因清单；输出是假说清单而非证据。）
  • Nociceptive neurons — the nerves that carry pain signals; their sensitisation underlies hyperalgesia and allodynia.（中文：伤害感受神经元——传递疼痛信号的神经；其敏化是痛觉过敏与异常性疼痛的基础。）
  • P-glycoprotein / ABCB1 — a transmembrane ATP-dependent drug efflux pump, prominent at the blood–brain barrier; increased activity reduces the effective exposure of many analgesics.（中文：P-糖蛋白 / ABCB1——一种跨膜的 ATP 依赖性药物外排泵，在血脑屏障处尤为突出；活性增强会降低多种镇痛药的有效暴露量。）
  • Pancreatitis (in CF) — the CFTR defect impairs chloride and bicarbonate secretion in the pancreatic ducts; secretions become acidic and viscous, ducts obstruct, and inflammation follows. Pain is part of its diagnostic definition.（中文：（CF 中的）胰腺炎——CFTR 缺陷损害胰管的氯离子与碳酸氢根分泌，分泌物变得酸性黏稠，胰管阻塞并引发炎症；疼痛是其诊断定义的一部分。）
  • PPI network (protein–protein interaction network) — proteins drawn as nodes with an edge between two proteins where there is evidence they interact.（中文：PPI 网络（蛋白质–蛋白质相互作用网络）——把蛋白质画成节点，在有证据表明相互作用的两个蛋白质之间连边。）
  • Text-mining edges — network edges inferred purely from two proteins being mentioned together in the literature, rather than from experimental or curated interaction evidence.（中文：文本挖掘边——仅凭两个蛋白质在文献中被共同提及而推断出的网络连边，而非来自实验或人工整理的相互作用证据。）
  • TNF (tumour necrosis factor) — a major proinflammatory cytokine regulator; in CF associated with osteoporosis risk, liver disease, GI complications and pulmonary severity, and in pain a genuinely central sensitiser of peripheral and central pathways.（中文：TNF（肿瘤坏死因子）——主要的促炎细胞因子调控者；在 CF 中与骨质疏松风险、肝病、胃肠道并发症及肺病严重程度相关，在疼痛中则是外周与中枢通路的核心敏化因子。）

Methods / tools to lift

  • Five-database co-occurrence mining — OMIM, CTD (Comparative Toxicogenomics Database), HuGE Navigator (Phenopedia), PhenGenI, and the NHGRI GWAS catalog, queried with "cystic fibrosis" and "pain". Counts: OMIM 125/734 → 22 common; CTD 13/91 → 0; HuGE 352/967 → 107; PhenGenI 14/18 → 2; NHGRI 12/20 → 0. Totals 1,516 CF and 1,830 pain genes. The wildly uneven overlaps are themselves informative — these are five curation philosophies, not five independent measurements.
  • Human Pain Genetics Database (HPGD) — 537 genes implicated in pain; eight known CF modifiers also appear there (ACE, ADRB2, EDNRA, HFE, LTA, SERPINA1, STX1A, TLR2). TNF was common to all three mining exercises.
  • GEO + GEO2R + limma — differential expression on public microarray datasets with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control, then Bonferroni-corrected selection. Datasets: GSE40445 (CF vs non-CF nasal-brushing airway epithelium, 214 genes), GSE127208 (pain biomarkers, 60), GSE24982 (rat spinal-nerve-ligation neuropathic pain, 116), GSE176223 (knee osteoarthritis synovial fibroblasts, 4). 394 genes total, zero overlap anywhere.
  • CTD Venn Viewer — pathway mapping integrating KEGG, REACTOME and BioGRID. TNF ∩ TGFB1 = 26 shared pathways; the only pathway common to TNF, TGFB1 and CFTR was signal transduction (one of the largest top-level REACTOME categories — closer to a sanity check than a finding).
  • STRING v12.0 at medium confidence (0.400) — 14 nodes / 26 edges for TNF, CFTR, TGFB1, ABCB1; 17 nodes / 73 edges with all seven genes. Note: medium confidence includes text-mining edges.
  • GeneMANIA — ranked the interacting proteins; top-ranked was TNFRSF1A (TNF receptor 1).
  • DGIdb (Drug–Gene Interaction Database) — ABCB1 interacts with nine analgesics commonly used in CF (acetaminophen, aspirin, capsaicin, codeine, gabapentin, ibuprofen, methadone, naproxen, pentazocine); CFTR, TGFB1 and TNF one each.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Serves: modifier / WGS long game (Track C). The catalog gains a non-pulmonary phenotype axis that survives the modulator ceiling, one mechanistically distinct candidate (ABCB1), and a documented pancreatic pathway to pain. It does not gain a variant — everything here is at gene resolution, from literature, with no allele and no CF cohort.

literature-bias-deflated-gene-prioritiser (M) — for any candidate gene list produced by co-occurrence mining, pull each gene's total PubMed publication count and its per-phenotype count (NCBI E-utilities), then fit the simplest model of "how often would this gene appear in an intersection of two keyword searches purely as a function of how much it has been studied." Report each candidate as an observed-over-expected ratio rather than a raw hit. Two outputs: (1) a deflated ranking, which tells you whether you would have got TNF anyway; (2) a sleeper list — genes ranking high after deflation, i.e. co-occurring far more than their study volume predicts. That is where a genuinely novel modifier hides. Input: candidate gene list + E-utilities counts. The honest hard part: publication count is itself confounded with true biological importance, so the deflated ranking must be reported alongside the raw one, never instead of it.

What to verify first

  • Run the null the paper never ran — generate a candidate list from publication volume alone and see how much of the seven-gene result it reproduces. TNF is the most-studied modifier gene in CF and one of the most-studied genes in human biology; a literature-co-occurrence pipeline will return it for essentially any phenotype. TNF is not a hub of this network so much as a hub of PubMed.
  • Rebuild the STRING network with text-mining edges excluded, so the network evidence is actually independent of the selection evidence. Node and edge counts will drop; the question is whether the topology survives.
  • The pancreatic genes are quietly circular — pancreatitis is defined in part by pain, so a gene curated as a pancreatitis gene gets annotated as a pain gene almost by construction. CTRC/SPINK1/PRSS1 at the CF ∩ pain intersection is a rediscovery that CF causes pancreatitis and pancreatitis hurts. A real clinical pathway; not a novel modifier finding.
  • Redesign the expression arm rather than reporting its null in passing — matched species and tissue, effect-size-based comparison rather than intersecting Bonferroni-thresholded gene-symbol lists across a human nasal array, a rat nerve-ligation model and human knee synovium. The current test was underpowered by design.
  • Pain has to be defined before it can be a phenotype — it is subjective, multidimensional, and varies by site, chronicity and psychological context. Abdominal pain from pancreatitis, musculoskeletal pain from chronic cough, headache and neuropathic pain are not one trait. Any genetic study of pain in CF is rate-limited by whether pain was captured consistently enough to serve as an outcome at all.
  • Separate the two causal stories before designing anything — does a gene affect how much pain a person has, or how well their analgesics work? ABCB1 is the second kind, and a study powered for one is not powered for the other.
  • No variant-level claim is licensed by this paper. Seven gene names is where it stops.
  • External method on public data; no CFFPR-internals claims.

Gap-map / catalog

Closes:

  • Opens a non-pulmonary phenotype axis (pain) for the modifier catalog — strategically valuable precisely because modulators are reported not to affect it, so the phenotypic variance a modifier study needs is still present there. Extends the endpoint-ceiling thread (Gramegna 2026 RDA, Freitas 2026).
  • Supplies a first candidate set at gene resolution: CTRC, SPINK1, TNF, ABCB1, PRSS1, TGFB1 (+ CFTR), plus eight known CF modifiers that also appear in the Human Pain Genetics Database (ACE, ADRB2, EDNRA, HFE, LTA, SERPINA1, STX1A, TLR2).
  • Adds one mechanistically distinct and testable hypothesis: ABCB1 as a pharmacokinetic modifier of analgesic response in CF, with nine commonly used analgesics as substrates and no prior study of ABCB1 variation against CF clinical symptoms.

Leaves open:

  • No variant, no allele, no cohort, no association test. Gene resolution only.
  • Whether the seven-gene list survives a publication-volume null — untested, and the pipeline's construction makes the concern acute.
  • Whether the STRING corroboration is independent of the selection step (text-mining edges at medium confidence say probably not).
  • Why the four GEO datasets returned zero overlap, and what a properly powered, species- and tissue-matched expression comparison would show.
  • Whether pain in CF is one phenotype or several, and whether it can be captured consistently enough to serve as a genetic outcome.
  • Whether any candidate acts on pain experience vs analgesic response — two different studies.
