Ep3 — Track C: Data Quality Assessment in Healthcare, Dimensions, Methods and Tools (Systematic Review)

Paper: Hosseinzadeh E, Afkanpour M, Momeni M, et al. "Data quality assessment in healthcare, dimensions, methods and tools: a systematic review." BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making. 2025;25:296. Open access (CC BY-NC-ND). Full text retrieved via the Europe PMC BioC mirror after the fullTextXML MED route 404'd; not abstract-only.

A PRISMA systematic review mapping three things at once across 44 studies (screened down from 614 initially retrieved, across PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus): the data quality dimensions (DQDs) being measured, the methods used to measure each, and the tools built to implement those methods. Completeness dominates (93% of studies), followed by plausibility (49%) and conformance/accuracy (26% each); only 5% of studies used AI-based assessment methods; 55% of studies introduced an actual tool, most commonly built on Kahn et al.'s conformance/completeness/plausibility framework (29% of tool-introducing studies) and most commonly implemented in R (29%). The review deliberately proposes no new framework — its contribution is the structural crosswalk between dimensions, methods, and tools, plus the finding that dimension definitions overlap and conflict across the literature with no standardized vocabulary. Its explicit "structured tabular data only" inclusion criterion likely undercounts newer AI/NLP-based data-quality work on unstructured text (a direct tie to yesterday's LLM pathology-extraction episode). For the Liz-assigned CFFPR registry data-quality work, the paper supplies vocabulary and framework rather than a detection method, plus a secondary thread: three named open-source tools (daqapo, mosaicQA, TAQIH) worth a shortlist evaluation as off-the-shelf candidate-generation components.

Plain-English glossary

  • Completeness — The data quality dimension measuring whether expected data elements are present at all, as opposed to present-but-wrong.（中文：数据质量维度之一,衡量预期的数据元素是否存在,而非是否正确。）
  • Conformance — Whether a value meets a structural or syntactic constraint (correct format, correct unit), distinct from whether the value is believable.（中文：衡量一个数值是否满足结构或语法上的约束(正确的格式、正确的单位),与该数值是否可信是不同的概念。）
  • Kahn framework — A widely adopted, system-level data-quality framework operationalizing three core dimensions — conformance, completeness, plausibility — each checkable without an external gold standard.（中文：一个被广泛采用的系统层面数据质量框架,将数据质量操作化为三个核心维度——合规性、完整性、合理性——每个维度都可以在不依赖外部金标准的情况下进行检验。）
  • Plausibility — Whether a value is logically believable given everything else known about the record, as opposed to merely correctly formatted.（中文：结合记录中其他已知信息,判断某个数值在逻辑上是否可信,而不只是格式是否正确。）
  • PRISMA — A standardized reporting framework for systematic reviews, specifying how studies are searched for, screened, and included/excluded at each stage.（中文：系统综述的标准化报告框架,规定了文献检索、筛选以及每一阶段纳入/排除的流程。）
  • Wang and Strong framework — An older, broader data-quality framework splitting quality into intrinsic, contextual, representational, and accessibility dimensions, less common in clinical informatics specifically.（中文：一个较早期、更宽泛的数据质量框架,将数据质量划分为内在、情境、表征和可访问性四个维度,在临床信息学领域使用相对较少。）
  • Weiskopf framework — A 3×3 data-quality model crossing three constructs (complete, correct, current) against three contexts (patient, variable, time), giving nine operationalized assessment cells.（中文：一个3×3的数据质量模型,将三个构念(完整、正确、最新)与三个维度(患者、变量、时间)交叉组合,得到九个可操作化的评估单元。）

Error types covered / not covered

Covered (as vocabulary only, not as a specific detection rule): completeness, plausibility, conformance, accuracy, correctness, consistency, uniqueness, concordance, currency, timeliness, validity. Not covered: no specific error-type taxonomy for anthropometric data (unit conversion, digit transposition, decimal shift, carry-forward, real-clinical-event confusion) — this is a general healthcare data-quality landscape review, not an anthropometric-specific one, and offers no detection rule or gold-standard evaluation of its own.
