Ep3 (Track C — registry QC, slot 1/2): Scoring anthropometric data quality across survey programs

Corsi and colleagues (Global Health Action, 2017) compare child anthropometric data quality across 100 national surveys from three survey programs — DHS, MICS, and NNS — run across 23 countries in the West Central Africa region, asking whether these programs are even comparable ("apples and oranges"). For each survey they compute age/sex ratios, the percentage of children with missing or implausible height/weight data (per WHO 2006 z-score cutoffs), terminal digit preference scores, and HAZ/WAZ/WHZ distribution shape (SD, skewness, kurtosis), rolling all of it into a single SMART-methodology composite data quality score (0-90, lower is better). NNS scored best on nearly every indicator (mean composite score 14.0, vs. DHS 33.0 and MICS 40.9), but the authors flag their own caveat: NNS's real-time field-correction software may produce data that looks "in range" without the child ever being remeasured, and removing the heavily-weighted missing/implausible component from the score shrinks NNS's advantage considerably. This episode treats the paper as a program- or site-level evaluation metric (pipeline stage (d)), with a buildable artifact adapting the composite score to score CFFPR clinic-sites or encounter-years as a triage dashboard, entirely separate from any record-level statistical flagger.

Plain-English glossary

  • Z-score (HAZ/WAZ/WHZ) — How many standard deviations a child's height-for-age, weight-for-age, or weight-for-height sits above or below the expected value for their age and sex, per the WHO 2006 Child Growth Standards.（中文：某儿童的身高年龄、体重年龄或体重身高指标，相对于WHO 2006儿童生长标准中该年龄和性别所对应的预期值，偏离了多少个标准差。）
  • WHO implausibility cutoffs — The specific numeric bounds (e.g. WHZ beyond ±5 SD, HAZ beyond ±6 SD) past which a z-score is treated as a measurement or recording error rather than a real biological value.（中文：WHO规定的具体数值界限（例如WHZ超出正负5个标准差，HAZ超出正负6个标准差），超出该界限的z分数会被当作测量或记录错误，而非真实的生理数值。）
  • Terminal digit preference / digit preference score (DPS) — A chi-square-based measure (0-100) of whether recorded values cluster suspiciously on certain last digits (e.g. many heights ending in 0 or 5); scores above 20 indicate a statistically real preference.（中文：一种基于卡方检验的指标（取值0到100），用于衡量记录数值是否可疑地聚集在某些末位数字上（例如大量身高以0或5结尾）；超过20分表示存在统计学上真实的偏好。）
  • SMART/ENA composite data-quality score — A weighted combination of missing/implausible rate, age/sex ratio deviation, digit preference, and z-score distribution shape into a single 0-90 score, lower being better.（中文：将缺失/不合理比例、年龄性别比偏差、数字偏好以及z分数分布形态加权汇总成一个0到90的单一分数，分数越低越好。）
  • Primary sampling unit (PSU) — The basic geographic cluster (a village or urban census block) a survey samples from.（中文：一项调查抽样所依据的基本地理聚类单位，即一个村庄或城市普查区块。）

Methods / tools to lift

  • SMART-methodology composite anthropometric data-quality score — a reusable, weighted multi-indicator index (missing/implausible rate, age/sex ratio, digit preference, z-score distribution shape) for scoring a whole survey, site, or time period rather than a single record.
  • Terminal digit preference scoring (chi-square departure from uniform digit distribution) — a cheap, interpretable signal of recorder/instrument-driven measurement artifact, distinct from any model-based outlier flag.
  • Cross-program/cross-source benchmarking against an external reference (NHANES) — using a trusted outside dataset as an informal quality yardstick rather than relying on internal consistency alone.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Pipeline stage: (d) evaluation, primary (program-/site-level monitoring, not record-level flagging), with secondary (a) candidate generation via its reliance on off-the-shelf WHO z-score implausibility cutoffs. Buildable artifact — CFFPR Site/Encounter-Year Composite DQ Score: adapt the SMART-style index (digit preference + missing/implausible rate + z-score SD/skewness/kurtosis) to score each clinic-site or encounter-year as a triage dashboard metric, sitting alongside, not inside, any record-level flagger. Difficulty: S-M (small to medium) — each component is simple to compute; the work is defining sensible site/time groupings.

What to verify first

  • Whether terminal digit preference is even a meaningful signal in a modern clinical setting using digital scales and stadiometers, versus a field survey relying on manual tape measures and recording — the underlying mechanism may not transfer.
  • Whether WHO 2006 under-five z-score cutoffs are the right implausibility bound for a registry spanning ages well past five, which would need CDC or adult BMI conventions instead — a growth-reference question, not a registry-internals one.
  • Whether a composite score built to compare entire survey programs translates cleanly to comparing individual sites within one program, where per-site sample sizes are far smaller, and whether the SMART weighting scheme (acknowledged by the authors as conventional rather than empirically derived) needs re-deriving for that use.

Pipeline stage

(d) evaluation, primary; (a) candidate generation, secondary.

Error types covered / not covered

Covered (as a program/site-level signal, not a record-level rule): missingness, biologically implausible values (per WHO z-score cutoffs), terminal-digit heaping/rounding, non-representative age/sex sampling. Not covered: no record-level sensitivity/specificity or gold-standard validation; does not address unit conversion, decimal-shift, carry-forward, or real-clinical-event-vs-error discrimination directly.
