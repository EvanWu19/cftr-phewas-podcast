Blurb (~150w)

A twin study used as a nature-vs-nurture probe. Fischer, Stanke, and Tümmler sequenced the T-cell-receptor beta-chain repertoire (the CDR3 region) in 16 monozygotic (identical) cystic fibrosis twin pairs — 14 of them F508del-homozygous — from the European CF Twin and Sibling Study. With CFTR genotype held identical, they asked whether genes still shape the immune repertoire and whether CF stamps a disease-specific signature. Findings: each twin's V/J-segment usage was closest to their own twin (genes shape the repertoire) — except in the most clinically discordant/severe pairs, where infection-driven clonal expansion overwrote the shared blueprint; and of ~40,000 shared receptor sequences, 99.8% were already public (non-CF) clones, so there is no CF-specific signature. The proposed direction: use the F508del-homozygous subset as a fixed-CFTR-genotype stratum for modifier hunting, with monozygotic-twin discordance as the empirical ceiling on the genetic signal and environment kept in the model.

Plain-English glossary

  • Amplicon sequencing: Copying and reading one targeted DNA stretch (here CDR3β) millions of times.
  • CDR3 (complementarity-determining region 3): The hyper-variable, antigen-gripping tip of the receptor at the V-D-J seam; what "repertoire sequencing" mostly reads.
  • Clonality (Simpson): How dominated a repertoire is by a few expanded clones; low = mostly naïve/fresh T cells.
  • Clonotype: One specific receptor sequence; many T cells sharing it = a "clone."
  • Clonal expansion: Runaway multiplication of particular T cells, e.g. driven by recurrent infection.
  • immuneACCESS: A public reference database of known TCR sequences.
  • Jensen-Shannon distance: A number scoring how different two distributions are — here, how differently two people use V/J segments. Small = similar.
  • Monozygotic twins: Identical twins from one split egg; share essentially the whole genome — a natural genetics-vs-environment control.
  • p.Phe508del / F508del: The most common CF-causing CFTR variant; deletes phenylalanine at position 508, misfolding the protein.
  • Public clones: Common receptor sequences carried by many unrelated people.
  • Repertoire: A person's whole collection of receptors — their immune fingerprint.
  • TCR (T-cell receptor): The surface "hand" a T cell uses to grip antigen fragments; this paper reads the beta chain.
  • VDJ recombination: The shuffle of interchangeable V, D, and J gene segments that builds billions of distinct receptors from one genome.

Now the magic: you have billions of T cells, and each one has a slightly different receptor, so collectively they can recognize almost anything. How does the body build billions of different receptors from one genome? Through a shuffling process called VDJ recombination. Your DNA carries a library of interchangeable gene segments — V segments, D segments, J segments — and as each T cell matures, it randomly picks one V, one D, one J and stitches them together. Different picks, different receptor.

And a clonotype is one specific receptor sequence; if many T cells share it, that's a "clone" that has expanded. They read all this with amplicon sequencing — a technique that copies and reads one targeted stretch of DNA, here the CDR3 of the beta chain, millions of times over.
