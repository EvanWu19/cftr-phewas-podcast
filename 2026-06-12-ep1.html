Blurb

Winters et al. (Circulation, 2026) run a genome-first study of familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) in 104,300 African-ancestry individuals across three US biobanks (All of Us, BioMe, MyCode). The headline: the prevalence of clearly pathogenic FH variants is essentially identical in African- and European-ancestry groups (~1 in 306 vs ~1 in 273), so FH is not biologically rarer. But African-ancestry individuals were 1.61× more likely to carry a variant of unknown significance (VUS) (p=1.2×10⁻¹²), and those VUS were not benign: they tracked ~10 mg/dL higher LDL-C and — uniquely in the African-ancestry group — conferred heart-attack risk (OR 1.91) comparable to a known pathogenic variant. The difference is being manufactured by European-centric variant-classification catalogs, not by biology. Proposed direction: test whether VUS burden in CFTR and candidate modifier genes is unevenly distributed by ancestry in the CF cohort, and whether that burden tracks a CF severity phenotype after accounting for known causal genotype. Open question: is a few-thousand-patient CF cohort even powered to detect an ancestry-by-VUS effect that here needed >100,000 people?

Methods to lift

  • Genome-first ascertainment — scan an already-sequenced biobank for variant carriers regardless of enrollment reason, then read out their EHR — avoids clinic-ascertainment blind spots.
  • Pathogenic-vs-VUS stratification using a standardized expert panel (ClinGen FH Variant Curation Expert Panel) — split carriers into "known bad" vs "gray zone."
  • Genetically-defined ancestry groups (similarity to reference populations) rather than self-report.
  • Per-cohort analysis + meta-analysis across All of Us / BioMe / MyCode, adjusted for age and sex.
  • Two outcomes: a continuous biomarker (LDL-C) and a hard event (MI) — gives both a graded and a binary readout of variant impact.

Proposed direction (conditional)

Conditional on inspection of CFF WGS variant calls and registry phenotype linkage: test whether the per-person burden of variant-of-unknown-significance calls in CFTR and in candidate modifier genes differs by genetically-defined ancestry, and whether VUS burden is associated with a CF severity phenotype after accounting for known causal genotype — mirroring Winters et al.'s finding that unclassified variants carry real risk specifically in the under-catalogued ancestry group.

What to verify first

  • Whether the CF cohort (~few thousand) is powered to detect an ancestry-by-VUS effect that here required >100,000 individuals.
  • Whether the CFTR / modifier variant-classification pipeline used in our data carries the same European-centric tilt (currently an assumption, not verified).
  • Whether a clean, agreed CF severity outcome exists to play the role LDL-C/MI played here.
  • That ancestry inference in our cohort is stable at our sample size (coarse "African vs European" binning hides within-group diversity).
  • Reminder: CFF WGS variant calls have not been inspected and no design has been chosen — this is one illuminated direction, not a decision.

You take a huge population that's already been genetically sequenced, you scan everyone for FH-causing variants regardless of why they enrolled, and then you ask, "What do the carriers' health records look like?" You're letting the genotype — the actual DNA a person carries — pick out the patients, instead of letting the clinic do it.

JORDAN Why does flipping the order matter so much?

ALEX Because patient-first studies only ever see the people who already got noticed by the medical system. If a whole group of patients is systematically under-diagnosed, the patient-first approach never even sees them — they're invisible. Genome-first finds carriers who were never flagged.
