Ep3 (Track C, 1/2) — Evaluating LLMs for Automated Data Cleaning and Feature Engineering in Clinical Datasets

Blurb

This Cureus (2026) study from the University of Ibadan benchmarks three large language models (LLMs) — GPT-4, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Gemini 1.5 Pro — against a conventional rule-based pipeline on five data-cleaning tasks (missing-value imputation, outlier detection, data-type correction, duplicate removal, unit standardization) and two feature-engineering tasks, across three public clinical datasets: MIMIC-IV, eICU, and NHANES 2017-2020. Each model was prompted with a data dictionary, 50 example rows, and a chain-of-thought instruction to generate executable pandas/scikit-learn code at temperature 0.0, with ground truth from two board-certified clinical informaticians who hand-labeled 2,000 records per dataset (Cohen's kappa 0.91). All three LLMs beat the rule-based baseline on cleaning F1 (Claude 3.5 Sonnet 0.90, GPT-4 0.89, Gemini 1.5 Pro 0.85, baseline 0.77) and on downstream in-hospital mortality prediction AUROC (0.851 vs. 0.803 for the baseline and 0.761 for no cleaning). LLM cleaning also generalized better across datasets than the static rule-based pipeline. The authors are explicit that 13-26% of LLM-proposed features were judged clinically implausible on expert review, and that domain-expert oversight remains essential. For our purposes, this is an external-methods paper only — it says nothing about CFFPR's own data, rules, or flag rates, and supplies candidate-generator ideas, not a validated solution.

Glossary

  • AUROC (area under the receiver operating characteristic curve) — a standard classifier discrimination metric; not re-explained on-air since Runyu already knows it. (中文：接收者操作特征曲线下面积，一种标准的分类器区分能力指标。)
  • Cohen's kappa — a statistic measuring agreement between two raters beyond chance; here used to quantify agreement between the two clinical informaticians who built ground truth (kappa = 0.91). （中文：一种衡量两位评分者之间一致性的统计量，扣除偶然一致的部分；此处用于衡量两位临床信息学专家标注真值时的一致性。）
  • eICU (eICU Collaborative Research Database) — a public, multi-center ICU dataset (208 US hospitals, PhysioNet) used here for external/cross-institutional validation of cleaning performance. （中文：一个公开的多中心ICU数据集，涵盖美国208家医院，托管于PhysioNet，本文用于跨机构外部验证。）
  • Jaro-Winkler similarity — a string-similarity metric used here (threshold >0.95) to flag likely near-duplicate records. （中文：一种字符串相似度度量，本文用阈值大于0.95来标记可能的近似重复记录。）
  • Kahn data-quality framework — a widely cited taxonomy (Kahn et al.; also Weiskopf & Weng) for describing EHR data-quality problems in standard vocabulary — dimensions include conformance (format/type correctness), completeness (missingness), and plausibility (believability given clinical/statistical context). This paper organizes its five cleaning tasks around that vocabulary. （中文：一个被广泛引用的分类框架，用标准词汇描述EHR数据质量问题，维度包括一致性、完整性和合理性；本文用它来组织五项清洗任务。）
  • MCAR / MAR / MNAR — missing-data mechanism categories (missing completely at random, missing at random, missing not at random) that the LLMs were asked to infer before choosing an imputation strategy; standard missing-data theory, not re-taught on-air. （中文：缺失数据机制的分类——完全随机缺失、随机缺失、非随机缺失；论文要求LLM先推断机制再选择插补策略。）
  • MIMIC-IV (Medical Information Mart for Intensive Care) — a de-identified public EHR dataset from Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (300,000+ admissions, hosted on PhysioNet), the default benchmark dataset in this study and in clinical ML generally because it's free, large, and well-documented. （中文：一个去标识化的公开电子健康档案数据集，来自贝斯以色列女执事医疗中心，是本研究及临床机器学习领域常用的默认基准数据集。）
  • NHANES (National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey) — a CDC-run national health examination survey (non-ICU, population-level data), used here to test generalization to a different data-quality failure profile (notably higher unit heterogeneity, 14.6%). （中文：美国疾控中心主持的全国健康体检调查，非ICU、人群层面的数据，本文用它测试清洗方法在不同数据质量缺陷模式下的泛化能力。）

Full citation

Methods/tools to lift

  • Pairing a fixed-temperature (0.0), chain-of-thought LLM prompt (system instruction + data dictionary + 50 example rows + task instruction requesting reasoning-then-code) with multi-run stability checks (5 runs/task, median reported) as a reproducibility discipline for any LLM-assisted cleaning pilot.
  • Using the Kahn/Weiskopf-Weng data-quality taxonomy (conformance/completeness/plausibility) as shared vocabulary when scoping which error types a tool is meant to catch.
  • Benchmarking any new cleaning method against both "no cleaning" and a documented rule-based baseline, not just reporting its own numbers in isolation.
  • Measuring intrinsic cleaning accuracy (F1 against expert-labeled ground truth) and downstream task impact (AUROC shift), rather than either alone.
  • Having independent experts rate generated features for clinical plausibility before any are added to a model, with a reported accept/reject rate (here 74-87%).

Where it lands + buildable artifact

  • Relevant to pipeline stage (a), the candidate generator — the paper's strongest, most reproducible result is LLMs' performance on structural error types (type correction, unit standardization, duplicate detection) that are orthogonal to a spline/mixed-effects model's statistical-extremity detection, suggesting a complementary rather than overlapping candidate pool.
  • Buildable artifact: a structural-anomaly scanner run alongside the statistical outlier model as a second candidate generator. MVP: feed it a field-level data dictionary plus a small labeled sample of known height/weight entry errors; have it propose pandas-style rule checks for unit mismatches, type anomalies, and near-duplicate encounters; output a separate candidate list layered onto existing flags for human review only (no auto-correction). Difficulty: M — prompting itself is straightforward; the real work is building a determinism/audit-logging harness so every flag is traceable.

What to verify first

  • Whether the paper's task framing (data dictionary + 50 example rows, LLM reasons then writes code) transfers to a longitudinal, repeated-measures registry structure rather than single-admission ICU/survey records.
  • Whether expert-plausibility calibration (kappa 0.91 on these specific datasets) would hold for height/weight judgments across a pediatric-to-adult population — not asserted or tested in this paper.
  • Possible memorization/data-leakage effects, since MIMIC-IV and NHANES are heavily discussed public benchmarks the models may have encountered in pretraining; the paper does not test or control for this.
  • The paper's own stated risk: LLMs can produce subtle, clinically plausible but incorrect transformations that are hard to catch without comprehensive unit testing — any pilot needs its own independently-labeled held-out test set.
  • Inference cost/latency at scale was not evaluated in this paper at all and remains an open unknown.

Pipeline stage

(a) Candidate generator — complementary structural-error detector (unit/type/duplicate) alongside a statistical outlier model; not shown to help with (b) adjudication, (c) review-budget reduction, or (d) gold-standard-free evaluation in this paper.

Error types covered / not covered

  • Covered (strong, reproducible results): data-type mismatches (e.g., numeric values stored as strings), unit heterogeneity/standardization, near-duplicate record detection (Jaro-Winkler-style), missingness-mechanism-aware imputation.
  • Covered but with caveats: physiologically-implausible outlier detection — handled via capping/removal/flag-and-review, but evaluated only against expert labels on public ICU/survey data, not validated for registry-specific plausibility ranges.
  • Not covered / not addressed: digit transposition and decimal-shift errors are not called out as a distinct tested category in this paper; carry-forward errors in longitudinal data are not evaluated since the datasets used are largely single-admission/cross-sectional; distinguishing a true data-entry error from a real, unusual clinical event (the adjudication question) is explicitly not what this paper's design tests — its ground truth is "is this cleaning operation correct," not "is this flagged value a real patient."
