Ep4 (Track C, #2) — Peak BMI and Peak Weight Velocity in Infancy

Wood CT, Truong T, Skinner AC, Armstrong SC, Perrin EM, Woo JG, Green CL. 2023. The Journal of Pediatrics, 257, 113356. Abstract-only (no PMCID indexed).

Blurb

Using retrospective EHR growth data from one health system (children born ≥34 weeks gestation, 2014-2017, N=6,425), this paper extracts peak BMI (pBMI) and peak weight/length velocity — trajectory-level features computed from several growth points rather than a single visit — after excluding implausible growth data with an unnamed "algorithm created for childhood growth." Mean pBMI was 17.9 kg/m² at a mean age of 9.6 months; children with obesity at age two (14.5% of the cohort) had higher peak velocities and pBMI roughly 2 kg/m² greater than those without. Each one-unit increase in pBMI was associated with more than fourfold greater odds of obesity at two years. For CFFPR, the relevant lesson isn't the obesity-prediction finding itself but the feature-engineering pattern: derived trajectory quantities like peak velocity can flag physiologically implausible growth patterns that a single-value screen would miss entirely.

Plain-English glossary

  • Peak BMI (pBMI) — the highest point an infant's BMI reaches during the well-documented early-life BMI rise-then-dip pattern, typically in the first year.（中文:峰值体重指数,婴儿在生命早期BMI"先升后降"这一已被充分记录的模式中所达到的最高点,通常出现在第一年内。）
  • Peak weight velocity — the fastest rate of weight gain an infant reaches before growth naturally decelerates.（中文:峰值体重增长速度,婴儿在生长自然减速之前所达到的最快体重增长速率。）
  • Trajectory feature — a quantity computed from multiple longitudinal data points (e.g., a peak or a rate of change) rather than read directly from a single visit.（中文:轨迹特征,由多个纵向数据点计算得出的量(例如峰值或变化速率),而非直接从单次就诊读取。）

Methods / tools to lift

  • Peak BMI and peak weight/length velocity as individualized (trajectory-shape-adapted) growth features, rather than fixed-age snapshots.
  • Physiologically-implausible-range checks on a derived trajectory quantity (peak velocity) as a complementary detection angle to single-value screens.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

cftr-registry note (method only): derived peak-velocity/peak-BMI features could feed an existing spline- or mixed-effects-based candidate generator as additional inputs, catching cases where no single underlying value looks implausible in isolation but the implied trajectory is physiologically impossible.

Buildable artifact: peak-velocity-feature-extractor (S-M) — given a longitudinal infant weight/length series (public or synthetic), compute pBMI, peak weight velocity, and peak length velocity per this paper's definitions, and flag any peak-velocity estimate outside a plausible physiological range as a candidate for review.

What to verify first

  • Which implausible-growth-data algorithm was actually used (unnamed in the abstract) and how lenient or aggressive its exclusions are — this could let contamination survive into peak estimates, or bias the peak-velocity distribution by discarding real fast-growing infants.
  • The confidence interval on the >4-fold pBMI odds ratio, and whether it holds after excluding extreme-tail pBMI children.
  • Generalizability of the racial/ethnic composition and single-health-system setting beyond this cohort.
  • Whether the Hispanic-ethnicity association reflects biology/environment or unmeasured social determinants correlated with ethnicity in this catchment area.

Pipeline stage: (a) candidate generation — primary. Supplies engineered trajectory features as additional candidate-generator inputs, not a full detection method on its own.

Error types covered / not covered: Covered — implausible growth-velocity patterns detectable only at the trajectory level (not visible from any single value in isolation). Not covered — unit conversion, digit transposition, decimal shift, straightforward carry-forward/duplicate values, or real-clinical-event confusion; and the paper's own implausible-value exclusion step is itself unspecified (unnamed algorithm), a gap for this catalog entry.
