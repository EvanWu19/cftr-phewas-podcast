Blurb

Heude et al.'s 2019 Lancet Digital Health paper (abstract-only here, genuinely closed access) tackles the growth-reference side of anthropometric QC: both national French and WHO growth charts had been found poorly calibrated to real contemporary children in many countries, and the traditional fix — a dedicated validation cohort — is slow and expensive. The authors instead harvested routinely collected anthropometric measurements from 32 randomly sampled French paediatricians and 10 GPs sharing the same EMR software, applied an automated (but undetailed in the abstract) cleaning step to remove measurement/transcription errors, and fit new reference curves with GAMLSS/BCPE on 1,458,468 height and 1,690,340 weight measurements from 238,102 children. The new curves sat distinctly above the old French and WHO charts at every age — an average gap of 0.75 SD for height and 0.50 SD for weight — and validated reasonably well against independent national surveys, with small differences only at 14-15 years. The direct relevance to a QC pipeline isn't flagging individual records; it's the substrate layer: this paper is a concrete demonstration that an external reference curve, however well-established, can be measurably miscalibrated against the population it's actually applied to, by a margin large enough to change who gets flagged. Buildable: a reference-curve calibration-gap audit, quantifying the SD-level offset between any comparison reference and a real population's empirical distribution before trusting it as a flagging baseline.

Plain-English glossary

  • Growth chart / percentile curve — for a given age and sex, the population distribution of a measurement like height or weight, usually shown as a set of percentile curves so a clinician can place one child relative to peers.（中文：生长曲线图／百分位曲线，针对特定年龄和性别，身高或体重等测量值在人群中的分布，通常以一组百分位曲线呈现，便于临床医生判断某一儿童相对同龄人的位置。）
  • Calibration (of a reference curve) — how well a reference curve's assumed distribution matches the real distribution of the population it's being applied to; a poorly calibrated chart can make normal measurements look artificially high or low.（中文：（参考曲线的）校准，指参考曲线假设的分布与其实际应用人群的真实分布之间的匹配程度；校准不佳的曲线图会让正常的测量值显得异常偏高或偏低。）
  • LMS method — the classic method (used in WHO/CDC charts) fitting three smoothly varying curves per age: Lambda (skewness), Mu (median), Sigma (spread).（中文：LMS方法，WHO／CDC曲线图采用的经典方法，针对每个年龄拟合三条平滑曲线：Lambda（偏度）、Mu（中位数）、Sigma（离散程度）。）
  • GAMLSS (generalised additive models for location, scale, and shape) — the modern successor to LMS, modeling additional distributional parameters (not just location and scale) as smooth functions of age.（中文：GAMLSS（位置-尺度-形状广义加性模型），LMS方法的现代升级版，将更多分布参数（不只是位置和尺度）都建模为年龄的平滑函数。）
  • Box-Cox power exponential (BCPE) distribution — a four-parameter distribution (adding a kurtosis/tail-heaviness parameter beyond LMS's three) often paired with GAMLSS for growth-reference fitting.（中文：Box-Cox幂指数分布（BCPE），一种四参数分布（在LMS的三个参数之外增加了控制峰度／尾部厚度的参数），常与GAMLSS搭配用于生长参考曲线拟合。）
  • Secular growth trend — a population-level shift in average height/weight over successive birth cohorts, which can make an older reference chart systematically mismatched to a contemporary population.（中文：世代生长趋势（secular growth trend），连续出生队列之间人群平均身高/体重的变化，会使较旧的参考曲线图与当代人群产生系统性不匹配。）

Methods / tools to lift

  • Routine EHR data as a scalable alternative to a dedicated validation cohort — harvesting already-recorded office-based measurements at a scale (1.4M+ height records) no purpose-built cohort study could match.
  • GAMLSS/BCPE reference-curve fitting — the modern four-parameter successor to the classic LMS method, with an explicit kurtosis parameter relevant to tail (implausible-value) behavior.
  • Dual validation design — comparing new curves against prior reference charts directly, and separately against independent national cross-sectional surveys the new curves had no part in building.
  • Shared-EMR-software multi-provider extraction — recruiting providers on a common EMR system to keep extracted fields structurally consistent without cross-system reconciliation.
