Blurb

A five-module GPT-4 system for the most expensive, most boring part of evidence generation: screening thousands of abstracts against inclusion criteria for a health-technology-assessment systematic review. The design choice that carries the paper is that GPT-4 is prompted with the PICOs criteria as separable rules and must return not just include/exclude but the specific criterion it excluded on — which turns every decision into an auditable object and lets you build a confusion matrix per criterion instead of one global accuracy. Averaged over four evaluation sets: recall 90%, precision 76%, F1 82, accuracy 89%, Cohen's κ 0.71, PABAK 0.78; data extraction F1 93 under a stringent all-seven-fields-correct rule. But the best result in the paper is reported as an error analysis: when clinical reviewers adjudicated 30 of the system's "false negatives" against a commercial SLR vendor's gold standard, they sided with the system on 23 of 30 — the reference standard was wrong. Where it lands: AI-tooling / career. Buildable artifact: picos-screener — and the reusable idea is not the screener, it's the disagreement ledger.

Plain-English glossary

  • Abstract screening — the stage of a systematic review where thousands of retrieved citations are cut down to a few dozen on title+abstract alone; ~85% of citations are typically excluded here, and it is the most time-consuming step.（中文：系统性综述中仅凭标题和摘要把数千篇文献筛到几十篇的阶段；通常约 85% 的文献在此被排除，是最耗时的一步。）
  • HTA (health technology assessment) — the process by which a national payer decides whether a drug is worth reimbursing; runs on systematic reviews under hard deadlines (EU Joint Clinical Assessment: 100 days for a new molecular entity, 60 for a line extension).（中文：卫生技术评估——国家支付方决定某药是否值得报销的流程；依赖系统性综述，且期限极紧。）
  • PABAK (prevalence-adjusted bias-adjusted kappa) — Cohen's κ corrected for class imbalance and rater bias; on the 5.3%-relevance melanoma set, κ was 0.67 while PABAK was 0.92, which tells you how much of κ here is a statement about prevalence.（中文：经患病率与偏倚校正的 kappa 系数；在类别极不平衡时与原始 kappa 差别巨大。）
  • PICOs — Population, Intervention/Comparator, Outcome, study type: the decomposition clinical-evidence reviewers use to turn a vague question into a screenable rule set. The decomposition is the trick — it's what makes per-criterion auditing possible.（中文：人群、干预/对照、结局、研究类型——把模糊的研究问题拆解为可筛选规则集的框架；这个"拆解"本身就是全篇的诀窍。）
  • SLR (systematic literature review) — a comprehensive, protocol-driven synthesis of the literature on a question; the highest form of evidence in evidence-based medicine, and traditionally takes 6–16 months.（中文：系统性文献综述——循证医学中证据等级最高的形式，传统上需 6–16 个月。）
  • Zero-shot / in-context learning — the model is given the criteria in the prompt and no labeled training examples at all; the appeal is that it eliminates the annotation burden, the cost is that the "model" is now a prompt with no version pinning.（中文：零样本／上下文学习——只在提示词里给出标准，完全不给标注训练样本；好处是免去标注负担，代价是"模型"变成了一个未锁定版本的提示词。）

Methods / tools to lift

  • Criterion-attributed decisions — force the LLM to return the rule it fired on, not just the label. This converts a classifier into an auditable system and is the single portable idea here.
  • Per-criterion confusion matrices — the output that matters is not "89% accurate," it's "criterion P2 is where you and the model disagree, 17 times."
  • Mechanized human-in-the-loop — mandatory adjudication of ≥10% of retrieved abstracts (or 30 if <300 retrieved); disagreements must be attributed to a criterion or entered as free text; metrics recomputed; iterate the prompt, not the model.
  • "Include when uncertain" prompt instruction — buys sensitivity, pays in precision. Know that you're buying it (661 false positives on a 3,665-abstract set).
  • PABAK alongside Cohen's κ — mandatory when relevance rates are 3–20%, which is the real-world regime.
  • Adjudicating your own gold standard — the paper's accidental best result: sample the disagreements and check the reference, not just the model.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Serves: AI-tooling / career. A shippable, portfolio-grade tool in a lane (LLM-assisted evidence curation) that is directly adjacent to RWE × genomics data-science roles.

picos-screener (difficulty S/M) — inputs: a PubMed query + a PICOs spec as YAML (one block per criterion). Fetches abstracts, screens each with an LLM, and for every exclusion records the criterion ID it fired on. Samples 10% for human adjudication and emits a per-criterion confusion matrix plus a disagreement ledger. Dogfood target: this podcast's own biobank_papers_index.xlsx — every row is already human-screened with a reason, which makes it a labeled reference set of known provenance. First thing you learn: whether your own inclusion criteria are internally consistent.

What to verify first

  • No trivial baseline is reported. What does a keyword filter score on the same abstracts? What does an off-the-shelf active-learning screener score (the paper notes those typically find 95% of relevant refs after screening 40%)? Without a comparator, κ=0.71 leans against nothing.
  • Time and cost savings are claimed but never measured — explicitly listed under future work.
  • Both test cases are oncology drug-effectiveness questions with unusually standardized outcome vocabularies. CF / rare-disease reviews have messier endpoints and far fewer trials; generalization untested (the authors concede this).
  • GPT-4 zero-shot with no version pinning discussed → the system is not reproducible even in principle, and the code is not public.
  • The myeloma vendor set has a 56.5% relevance rate, vs 3–20% in real reviews — every threshold-dependent number from that set describes a construction, not a review.
  • The relational field is the weak one: extracting a value is easy; binding it to the right study arm is where it broke (hazard ratio F1 67, and one abstract where it missed 2 of 4 study groups and invented a fifth).

JORDAN A machine has *different* failure modes. 机器有*另一种*失败模式。

JORDAN So the gold standard isn't gold. 所以金标准并不是金的。
