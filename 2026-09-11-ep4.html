Ep4 (Track C) — Are "Biologically Implausible" Values Actually Errors?

Freedman and colleagues (2016, Obesity) test the canonical WHO/CDC "biologically implausible value" (BIV) cutoffs against 13.7 million PedNSS records from 8.8 million low-income US children aged 2-4, using repeated measurements on the same 3.6 million longitudinally-tracked children as a stand-in ground truth: if a value is a one-off error, it shouldn't predict a repeat flag at an independent follow-up visit; if it's real, high recurrence is exactly what you'd expect. The result: recurrence is very high — odds ratios of 254 (BMI), 329 (weight), and 29 (height) — and stays above 50% all the way out to a baseline modified z-score of nearly 15, with sensitivity of only 20-43% and PPV topping out at 64% for the current WHO-based cutoffs. Real errors (misplaced decimals, height/weight swaps) cluster only at the most extreme z-scores (roughly >15-25). Widening the cutoffs, by any of several alternative schemes, raises measured obesity prevalence by about 1 point and roughly doubles severe obesity — with the exact width of the wider cutoff mattering far less than simply choosing one wider than the 1995 WHO guidance. This is the classic candidate-generator calibration paper for anthropometric BIV thresholds, and its cross-classification design is a reusable technique for evaluating any threshold-based flag when no chart-reviewed gold standard exists.

Plain-English glossary

  • CDC growth charts vs. WHO growth standards — CDC charts are built from a historical US reference population (1960s-1990s); WHO standards are a prescriptive, multi-country sample of breastfed infants representing optimal growth; the two aren't interchangeable and switching between them can shift obesity classification with zero real change in the child.（中文:CDC生长曲线图基于历史美国参考人群(1960-1990年代);WHO生长标准基于多国母乳喂养婴儿的规范性样本,代表理想生长状态;两者不能互换,即使孩子没有真实变化,切换参照标准也会改变肥胖分类结果。）
  • Z-score / LMS method — how many standard deviations a value sits from the age/sex reference mean, computed via three age-specific parameters (Box-Cox power L, median M, coefficient of variation S) that handle non-normal body-size distributions.（中文:某数值距离该年龄性别参考均值的标准差数,通过三个随年龄变化的参数(L、M、S)计算,用以处理身体测量指标的非正态分布特征。）
  • Modified z-score — a fix for standard LMS z-scores' instability in extreme tails: anchors the scale to half the distance between z=0 and z=2, then extrapolates linearly beyond that.（中文:针对标准LMS z分数在极端尾部不稳定这一问题的修正:以z=0和z=2之间距离的一半为尺度基准,超出范围后线性外推。）
  • Biologically implausible value (BIV) — a measurement flagged, per 1995 WHO expert-committee cutoffs, as too extreme to be believable and "likely to be errors."（中文:根据1995年WHO专家委员会截断标准,被判定为过于极端、不可信、"很可能是错误"的测量值。）
  • Obesity / severe obesity — BMI at/above the 95th percentile for age and sex; severe obesity is 120% of that threshold or higher (not re-explained on air beyond definitions — standard pediatric convention).（中文:BMI达到或超过该年龄性别第95百分位即为肥胖;达到或超过该阈值120%为重度肥胖。）
  • PedNSS / WIC — the Pediatric Nutrition Surveillance System, a public-health system tracking low-income children in federally funded nutrition programs (mostly WIC, the Special Supplemental Nutrition Program for Women, Infants, and Children).（中文:儿童营养监测系统,追踪联邦资助营养项目(主要是WIC,妇女、婴儿和儿童特别补充营养计划)中低收入儿童的公共卫生系统。）
  • Recurrence-based validation — using a patient's own second, independent measurement as a proxy ground truth when no chart-reviewed gold standard exists.（中文:在没有病历审核金标准的情况下,用患者自己第二次独立测量的结果作为真值的替代。）

Methods / tools to lift

  • Cross-classification / recurrence-rate design — bin records by baseline z-score, compute the empirical recurrence rate at an independent follow-up measurement per bin; a reusable proxy-ground-truth technique.
  • Modified z-score extrapolation — anchoring extreme-tail scaling to half the 0-to-2 z-score distance rather than trusting standard LMS math past its calibrated range.
  • Alternative-cutoff sensitivity analysis — recomputing downstream prevalence estimates under several widened thresholds to show how much (or little) the exact cutoff choice matters.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Pipeline stage: (a) candidate generator calibration, primary; (d) evaluation, secondary (the recurrence-based cross-classification is a reusable technique for validating any threshold-based flag without a chart-reviewed gold standard). Buildable artifact: a threshold recurrence calibrator — for any anthropometric field and candidate cutoff, bin records by initial z-score, compute empirical recurrence rate at a genuinely independent follow-up measurement per bin, and plot the resulting recurrence curve (this paper's Table 2, generalized) before adopting any inherited or borrowed threshold. Difficulty: S (simple cross-tabulation; the real requirement is having two genuinely independent measurements per patient).

What to verify first

  • "High recurrence = real value" is a reasonable working assumption, not a proven fact — it doesn't fully rule out a repeated systematic error (e.g., a persistently miscalibrated scale, or the same transcription mistake made twice).
  • This is a general pre-modulator pediatric population (ages 2-4, 2008-2011) with no CF-specific considerations; CFTR modulator initiation is associated with real, sometimes rapid weight gain — exactly the kind of legitimate extreme change a fixed population-derived threshold would misclassify as implausible, so any CF-cohort calibration would need to treat modulator-era timing as its own variable.
  • The authors explicitly do not address "inliers" — errors that fall within the currently plausible range — so even a perfectly calibrated threshold-widening approach solves only half the data-quality problem.
  • The reported longitudinal association may understate the true recurrence rate (matching errors between visits, regression to the mean both bias downward) — the real signal could be stronger than reported, not weaker.

Error types covered / not covered

Covered: extreme out-of-range values via fixed z-score/modified-z-score thresholds; identifies decimal-misplacement and height/weight-swap as the recognizable error patterns clustering at the most extreme z-scores. Not covered: in-range ("inlier") errors, real extreme values driven by legitimate clinical change (e.g., modulator-era weight gain), carry-forward errors, and unit-conversion errors not manifesting as extreme z-scores.
