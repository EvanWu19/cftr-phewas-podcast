Show Notes — Track B — The Shaky Foundations of LLMs and Foundation Models for EHRs (Wornow et al., 2023)

Blurb. Wornow, Shah, and colleagues at Stanford reviewed 84 clinical foundation models — 50 CLaMs (clinical language models) and 34 FEMRs (foundation models for electronic medical records) published before March 2023 — and built a taxonomy of their architectures, training data, and evaluation tasks. Their central finding: nearly every model demonstrates only one of the six promised benefits of foundation models (improved predictive accuracy), with scant evidence for the other five (less labeled data, cheaper deployment, emergent capabilities, multimodality, novel interfaces). Most CLaMs train on just MIMIC-III and PubMed (~18.5B words, MIMIC-III frozen at 2012); roughly half were never validated on clinical text; FEMRs lack any canonical evaluation set, so the same nominal task is defined differently across papers. The fix: evaluate benefit-by-benefit, and for predictive performance report ranking-based metrics (Top-K precision/recall) and subgroup calibration instead of bare AUROC, because real clinical workflows are capacity-constrained. Where it lands: AI-tooling / career. Buildable artifact: a capacity-aware evaluation harness that reports Top-K, calibration, and sample-efficiency curves for any registry prediction model. Full text via Europe PMC BioC (open access).

Plain-English glossary

  • CLaM (clinical language model) — a foundation model specialized on clinical/biomedical text; ingests and outputs text (e.g., extracts drug names, summarizes notes).（中文：专门处理临床/生物医学文本的基础模型，读入并输出文本。）
  • FEMR (foundation model for electronic medical records) — a model that ingests a patient's temporally-ordered sequence of coded medical events and outputs a machine-readable patient representation.（中文：读入病人按时间排序的编码医疗事件序列，输出病人机器可读表示的模型。）
  • MIMIC-III — the widely-used public ICU EHR dataset (~2M notes, <40k patients, one hospital, 2001–2012); the default training corpus for most clinical FMs.（中文：广泛使用的公开 ICU 电子病历数据集，约两百万条病历，单家医院 2001–2012。）
  • Structured vs. unstructured EMR data — structured = coded fields (LOINC labs, SNOMED diagnoses, meds); unstructured = free-text notes. The review covers both, excludes images/genetics/wearables.（中文：结构化＝编码字段如化验、诊断、用药；非结构化＝自由文本病历。）
  • Capacity-constrained workflow — the clinical reality that staff can act on only a limited number of model alerts per day, which makes ranking-based metrics more relevant than threshold-free ones.（中文：临床现实是医护每天只能处理有限数量的模型警报，因此基于排序的指标比无阈值指标更相关。）

Full citation

Wornow M, Xu Y, Thapa R, Patel B, Steinberg E, Fleming S, Pfeffer MA, Fries J, Shah NH. The shaky foundations of large language models and foundation models for electronic health records. npj Digit Med. 2023;6:135. PMID: 37516790 · PMC: PMC10387101 · DOI: 10.1038/s41746-023-00879-8

Methods / tools to lift

  • CLaM/FEMR taxonomy — a clean two-axis way to classify any clinical foundation model by input type (text vs. coded timeline).
  • Two-pass evaluation re-sorting — list tasks as the field reports them, then re-bucket each task under the benefit it actually tests; the gap between the two views is the contribution.
  • Capacity-aware metrics — Top-K precision/recall, subgroup calibration, sample-efficiency curves ("AUROC using K labeled examples"), human-evaluation for generated text (ROUGE/BLEU correlate poorly with human judgment).
  • HAIM (Holistic AI in Medicine) — cited example of a MIMIC-III-based benchmark constructed to require multimodal reasoning.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Serves AI-tooling / career. Tool sketch (paste-ready MVP): Build a framework-agnostic capacity-aware evaluation harness — a small Python package that takes a predictions+labels CSV (plus a subgroup column) for a registry prediction task and outputs Top-K precision/recall at a configurable alert budget K, subgroup-stratified calibration curves, and a sample-efficiency curve (performance vs. labeled training size) — not just AUROC. Input: predictions/labels CSV + subgroup field. Difficulty: S–M. Serves the RWE-tooling career target; framework-agnostic, evaluates anyone's model.

What to verify first

  • Whether the "only accuracy is measured" critique still holds in 2024–2025 work, or whether newer papers report the other five benefits (the review is explicitly non-systematic).
  • What a realistic alert budget K looks like for a given clinical workflow before hard-coding Top-K thresholds.
  • Coverage and quality of subgroup fields before reading a fairness/calibration gap as real.
