Ep4 — Track C — Registry Data Quality & Anthropometric Outlier Detection (2 of 2)

Blurb

Kodra et al. (2017, Advances in Experimental Medicine and Biology) is a framework chapter, not an applied study: it defines data quality for a rare-disease patient registry as the totality of features and characteristics of a dataset that bear on its ability to satisfy the needs of its intended use, and lays out seven assessment dimensions — completeness, validity, coherence and comparability, accessibility, usefulness, timeliness, and duplicate-record prevention — plus two structural prerequisites (a standardized Case Report Form and secure informatics infrastructure). The chapter's concrete deliverable is a standardized registry-description template, offered as the necessary first step before any dimension-by-dimension quality assessment can be applied consistently across registries. There's no cohort, flag rate, or empirical validation reported in the abstract — this is vocabulary and structure, not a detection method. It lands as answer (e) for the CFFPR outlier pipeline: nothing directly actionable, but a coverage-check framework worth having on hand to see which quality dimensions a detection method like yesterday's IQR paper does and doesn't address. Abstract-only, closed access, no PMCID.

Plain-English glossary

  • Case Report Form (CRF) — the standardized data-collection instrument, paper or electronic, defining exactly what fields get captured and how for every patient enrolled in a registry or study.（中文：一种标准化的数据采集工具，纸质或电子形式，明确规定注册系统或研究中每位入组患者需采集哪些字段、如何采集。）
  • Data quality (registry definition) — the totality of features and characteristics of a dataset that bear on its ability to satisfy the needs resulting from the intended use of the data; in a registry, the "product" is the data itself.（中文：数据集的全部特征与属性中，与其能否满足因数据预期用途而产生的需求相关的部分；在注册系统中，被"生产"出来的"产品"就是数据本身。）
  • Kahn framework — a 2016 electronic-health-record data-quality framework built around conformance, completeness, and plausibility, referenced here as a comparison point to this earlier, registry-specific framework.（中文：2016年提出的电子健康档案数据质量框架，围绕一致性、完整性和合理性展开，本文将其作为对照点，与这套更早、专门面向注册系统的框架相区分。）
  • Weiskopf and Weng dimensions — a 2013 data-quality dimension set (correctness, completeness, concordance, plausibility, currency) developed mainly for EHR data, also referenced as a comparison point.（中文：2013年提出的数据质量维度体系（正确性、完整性、一致性、合理性、时效性），主要针对电子健康档案数据开发，同样作为对照点提及。）
  • Coherence and comparability — whether data collected across different sites or time points can be meaningfully compared, a real structural challenge for multi-site registries.（中文：不同站点或不同时间点采集的数据能否被有意义地相互比较，对多站点注册系统来说是一项真正的结构性挑战。）

Methods/tools to lift

  • Seven-dimension data-quality checklist — completeness, validity, coherence and comparability, accessibility, usefulness, timeliness, duplicate-record prevention — usable as a coverage check against any existing QC program.
  • Standardized registry-description template — a structured way to document what a registry collects and how, as a prerequisite for any comparable quality assessment.
  • CRF standardization + informatics security controls — named as structural prerequisites for data quality, not optional extras.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Serves Liz / CFFPR data quality (method only). Pipeline stage: (e) nothing directly, but supplies useful vocabulary/framework. Buildable artifact: registry-dq-dimension-scorecard — input a short structured description of an existing data-quality initiative (which checks exist, what they cover), modeled on this paper's registry-description template; output a scorecard mapping each of the seven Kodra dimensions plus Kahn/Weiskopf-and-Weng dimensions to covered/partially covered/not covered. Difficulty: S (a structured checklist, not a modeling exercise).

What to verify first

  • The actual registry-description template fields and per-dimension operational guidance — unavailable from the abstract alone, would need the full book chapter.
  • Whether a more current registry-specific framework has superseded or absorbed this 2017 one, given how much follow-on work exists for Kahn (2016) and Weiskopf-and-Weng (2013).
  • Concrete operational definitions for "usefulness" and "coherence and comparability" before either could feed into a checkable scorecard.
  • This paper says nothing about how CFFPR itself is structured, documented, or scored against any of these dimensions — separate, unverified question.

Pipeline stage / error types covered

  • Pipeline stage: (e) vocabulary/framework only — this is a conceptual contribution, not a detection method, and doesn't itself flag, adjudicate, or evaluate any specific data point.
  • Error types covered/not covered: not applicable in the usual sense — the chapter names dimensions of quality (e.g., validity, duplicate records) rather than specific error mechanisms (unit conversion, digit transposition, decimal shift, carry-forward, real-clinical-event confusion), and reports no empirical error-detection results.
