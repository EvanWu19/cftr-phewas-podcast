Blurb

Stanke et al. (Human Genetics, 2006) is a foundational candidate-gene CF-modifier study. From 467 sibling pairs in the European CF Twin and Sibling Study they selected 37 F508del-homozygous families with extreme, discordant phenotypes (one mild, one severe) and genotyped two biologically-motivated chromosomal neighborhoods: 12p13 (TNFRSF1A + SCNN1A) and 16p12 (SCNN1B + SCNN1G). The bet — CF is a disease of ion transport plus host defense, so modifiers should hide in the epithelial sodium channel (ENaC, subunits SCNN1A/B/G) and the TNF-α receptor (TNFRSF1A). It paid off: ENaC was implicated as a CF modulator via transmission disequilibrium at SCNN1G and association with intrapair discordance at SCNN1B, and a TNFRSF1A intron-1 haplotype associated with disease severity — with risk-haplotype carriers underrepresented, read as a survival effect. Proposed direction: treat SCNN1A/B/G + TNFRSF1A as a pre-specified candidate set and re-test them against a CF severity phenotype in the WGS cohort. Open question: did these 37-family, abstract-only (no effect sizes) signals ever replicate at modern scale, and does our sequencing capture the non-coding/regulatory variants where the signal lived?

Plain-English glossary

  • Candidate-gene study — test a small, pre-chosen set of biologically-motivated genes rather than scanning the whole genome.
  • CFTR — Cystic Fibrosis Transmembrane conductance Regulator; the gene mutated in CF; normally moves chloride out of airway/gut cells.
  • Discordant phenotype — two related individuals differ on a trait (here, one mild, one severe).
  • ENaC (amiloride-sensitive epithelial sodium channel) — channel that moves sodium into airway cells; works in opposition to CFTR's chloride export to set airway-surface-liquid salt/water balance; built from subunits SCNN1A (α), SCNN1B (β), SCNN1G (γ).
  • F508del — the most common CF mutation; "homozygous for F508del" = carrying it on both gene copies.
  • Haplotype — a block of nearby variants inherited together.
  • Intron 1 — the first non-coding stretch inside a gene; a signal here is regulatory, not protein-changing.
  • Modifier gene — a gene that doesn't cause CF but tunes its severity.
  • TNF-α / TNFRSF1A — TNF-alpha is a major pro-inflammatory signal; TNFRSF1A encodes its receptor (a host-defense/inflammation candidate).
  • Transmission disequilibrium — family-based test: a variant passed to affected children more often than the expected 50% implicates it.

Methods to lift

  • Extreme-discordant sib-pair selection — pick mild/severe pairs sharing CFTR genotype to concentrate modifier signal (37 of 467 pairs).

Break CFTR and the mucus turns thick and sticky, which sets up chronic lung infection and inflammation — and infection and inflammation are what actually kill people with CF. The single most common CF mutation has a name you'll hear constantly: F508del. Someone "homozygous for F508del" carries that same mutation on both copies of the gene.

If CFTR alone determined the outcome, that couldn't happen. So something else is varying. Two candidates: environment — infections, treatment, luck — and other genes. Genes other than CFTR that nudge the severity of CF are called "modifier genes." That's the central concept of this whole strand of our reading. A modifier gene doesn't cause CF; it tunes how bad it is.
