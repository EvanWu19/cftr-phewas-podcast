Ep4 — Track C — Identifying biologically implausible values in big longitudinal data (de Mello e Silva 2024)

The "big data" companion to ep3's Harrall paper — same growth-data-cleaning problem, but applied to Brazil's SISVAN national nutrition surveillance system at a genuinely enormous scale: 15,885,550 children, 55,264,792 measurements, 2008–2017. A two-stage pipeline flags population outliers (POs, via WHO z-score cutoffs on HAZ/WAZ/WHZ/BMIZ) first, then longitudinal outliers (LOs, via mixed-effects model residuals with restricted cubic splines on age) on whatever remains. POs did nearly all the work — 5.74%/5.31% of height and 5.19%/4.74% of weight flagged (boys/girls), well above WHO's own 1% data-quality threshold — while LOs, even at the loosest cutoff, never exceeded 1.5% and moved prevalence estimates only marginally. A forced methodological compromise — switching from studentized to computationally cheaper scaled residuals purely because standard software couldn't handle studentized residuals at this scale — is itself a useful data point for anyone estimating compute budgets for a registry-scale QC pipeline. No gold standard exists anywhere in this study; every number describes flag rate, not accuracy. The authors' own candid concern that removing distribution-tail outliers may bias prevalence estimates against legitimately large or heavy children is worth carrying into any candidate-generation design.

Plain-English glossary

Population outlier (PO) — A measurement flagged because it falls outside a reference population's expected range, independent of the individual's own measurement history.（中文:因落在参照人群预期范围之外而被标记的测量值,与该个体自身的测量历史无关。）

Longitudinal outlier (LO) — A measurement flagged because it breaks an individual's own growth trajectory, even if the raw value looks unremarkable in the general population.（中文:因打破个体自身生长轨迹规律而被标记的测量值,即便该数值在一般人群中看起来并不异常。）

HAZ / WAZ / WHZ / BMIZ — WHO child growth standard z-scores for height-for-age, weight-for-age, weight-for-height, and BMI-for-age respectively — each expresses how many standard deviations a measurement sits from the age-and-sex-matched median on the WHO reference curves.（中文:分别为身高别年龄、体重别年龄、体重别身高、体重指数别年龄的WHO儿童生长标准z分数,表示某测量值与WHO参照曲线上同龄同性别中位数相差的标准差数。）

SISVAN — Brazil's national food and nutrition surveillance system, where anthropometric data is collected at routine primary healthcare visits and consolidated from three separate underlying systems into one national dataset.（中文:巴西国家食品与营养监测系统,人体测量数据在常规基层医疗就诊中采集,并从三个独立的底层系统合并为一个全国数据集。）

Methods / tools to lift

  • Two-tier staged pipeline: cheap WHO population-level z-score cutoffs first, then a more expensive trajectory-based mixed-effects model only on what's left.
  • Restricted cubic splines on age, knots placed at WHO growth-curve inflection points, fit separately by sex.
  • Scaled residuals as a computationally cheaper substitute for studentized residuals at extreme scale, validated on a subsample before full-scale use.
  • A dedicated "decreasing heights" rule (>2cm implausible drop between visits) as a secondary, targeted cleaning pass separate from the core PO/LO framework.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Pipeline stage: primarily (a) candidate generation (the two-tier staging pattern itself), also touches (d) evaluation (explicit no-gold-standard limitation, plus the authors' own concern about tail-removal bias against legitimately large/heavy children). Buildable: po-lo-staged-flagger (M) — implement the same two-stage pipeline on synthetic registry-scale longitudinal height/weight data, and benchmark the scaled-vs-studentized-residual compute-time trade-off at a smaller (~3M-record) scale to see whether the compromise this paper made is actually necessary there.

What to verify first

  • No gold standard exists anywhere in this study — every flag-rate number describes what was flagged, not whether the flag was correct.
  • The scaled-residual substitution was checked on a 1,000-boy subsample only, not exhaustively validated across the full range of trajectory shapes at this scale.
  • The authors' own unresolved concern that removing distribution-tail POs may bias prevalence estimates against legitimately large or heavy children — a candidate-generation-level bias question, not just a review-capacity one.
  • Mixed-effects models used only a random intercept (no random slope), a simplification attributed to dataset size — worth knowing before assuming LO flagging fully captures individual growth rate, not just starting level.

Error types covered / not covered: Covers extreme single-value population outliers (WHO z-score tails) and trajectory-residual-based longitudinal deviations, plus a targeted rule for implausible height decreases. Does not cover unit-conversion errors, digit transposition, or decimal-shift errors as distinct categories, and does not distinguish data-entry error from a real rapid clinical change within the LO framework.
