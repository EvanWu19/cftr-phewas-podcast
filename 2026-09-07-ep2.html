A large language model framework for literature-based disease-gene association prediction

Li, et al. Briefings in Bioinformatics, 2024.

Blurb

This paper introduces LORE (LLM-based Open Relation extraction and Embedding), a two-stage, unsupervised-representation pipeline that reads PubMed abstracts and turns them into a verifiable, traceable disease-gene knowledge graph, then ranks gene pathogenicity from it with a ClinVar-supervised ranker (ML-Ranker). Applied to 1.7 million abstracts via GPT-3.5, it extracted 11 million relations and built a 2,097-disease dataset (PMKB-CV) covering 200x more disease-gene pairs than ClinVar curates directly. ML-Ranker hit 79.9% mean average precision overall (90.0% restricted to LLM-ORE-annotated pairs) versus 69.4% for simple co-occurrence counting and 31.7% for asking GPT-4o directly — evidence that distilling literature into verifiable atomic relations first beats querying a general LLM's opaque parametric knowledge. Critically for reproducibility, swapping in the open-source Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct model matched GPT-3.5's performance and scaled to all ~4 million PubMed abstracts with disease-gene co-occurrence. Not a CFTR paper, but the recipe — and specifically its taxonomy-driven relation tagging — is directly reusable for mining CF/CFTR-modifier literature at scale, including as a potential aid to this podcast's own genes-and-variants catalog maintenance.

Plain-English glossary

  • ClinVar — a public, NIH-run, expert-curated database recording clinical assertions linking specific genetic variants (and by extension genes) to disease.（中文:美国NIH运营的一个公开的、经专家审核的数据库,记录将特定基因变异(及相关基因)与疾病关联起来的临床论断。）
  • Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) — an LLM technique that restricts the model's answer to a small set of retrieved documents per query, intended to improve traceability but limited by shallow sentence-similarity retrieval.（中文:一种LLM技术,将模型的回答限制在每次查询检索到的一小部分文档内,旨在提高可追溯性,但受限于浅层的句子相似度检索。）
  • Open relation extraction (ORE) — a text-mining task where a model extracts subject-predicate-object relations from text without being constrained to a predefined, fixed set of relation types.（中文:一种文本挖掘任务,模型从文本中抽取主语-谓语-宾语关系,而不局限于预先定义好的固定关系类型集合。）
  • Knowledge graph — a structured representation of entities (e.g., genes, diseases) and the relationships between them, here built so each relationship traces back to a specific source article.（中文:一种对实体(如基因、疾病)及其相互关系的结构化表示,本文构建的知识图谱中,每条关系都能追溯回具体的来源文章。）
  • Embedding — a dense numerical vector representation of an entity or relationship, here a 512-dimensional vector per disease-gene pair, that downstream models can compute distances and rankings over.（中文:实体或关系的稠密数值向量表示,本文中每个疾病-基因对被表示为一个512维向量,供下游模型计算距离和排序。）
  • Mean average precision (MAP) — a ranking-quality metric averaging precision across all correct-answer positions in a ranked list, then averaged across all diseases/queries evaluated.（中文:一种排序质量评估指标,对排序列表中所有正确答案位置的精确率取平均,再对所有评估的疾病/查询取平均。）
  • Leave-one-disease-out cross-validation — a validation scheme where the model is repeatedly retrained holding out one disease's data at a time, then evaluated on the held-out disease, to test generalization to unseen diseases.（中文:一种验证方案,每次留出一种疾病的数据不参与训练,用这种疾病来评估模型,以此检验模型对未见过疾病的泛化能力。)

Full citation

Li, et al. A large language model framework for literature-based disease-gene association prediction. Brief Bioinform. 2024. PMID: 39998433 | PMC11851487 | DOI: 10.1093/bib/bbaf070. Open access.

Methods / tools to lift

  • LLM-ORE: prompt-based open relation extraction turning each article into atomic, source-traceable factual statements.
  • LLM-EMB: compressing all relations for an entity pair (across all articles mentioning it) into a single dense embedding.
  • A hand-curated semantic taxonomy (105 terms, 4 classes) tagging relation type before embedding, to disambiguate "causes" from "tested and ruled out."
  • ML-Ranker: a GBDT ranking model (lambda objective) trained with sparse authoritative labels (ClinVar) over dense unsupervised embeddings.
  • Reproducing the pipeline with a small open-source LLM (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) instead of an enterprise API.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Serves AI-tooling / career. Buildable: a CFTR-LORE mini pipeline — apply the two-stage LORE recipe (open-source LLM, atomic relation extraction, small CF/CFTR-specific semantic taxonomy, embedding + ranking) to CF/CFTR modifier-gene literature, producing a ranked, evidence-linked gene table. Meta-application: could help systematize and partially automate this podcast's own genes-and-variants catalog maintenance, with every entry traceable to its source sentence and PMID. Difficulty: M.

What to verify first

  • LORE only curates from abstract-level co-occurrence; in this paper's own data, 1,307 of 4,311 known ClinVar pathogenic disease-gene pairs had zero abstract co-occurrence — a CF-specific version would structurally miss full-text-only evidence.
  • Extracted relations need manual spot-checking against source text before trusting a ranked output — traceability is only as good as extraction accuracy, and hallucinated-but-plausible atomic statements are an easy-to-miss failure mode.
  • Literature-co-occurrence-based ranking will bias toward already well-studied genes, the opposite of what's wanted if the goal is surfacing under-explored modifier candidates.
  • Output should be treated as a prioritized, citation-linked reading list, not an automatic pathogenicity verdict.

ALEX Today's Track B pick is "A large language model framework for literature-based disease-gene association prediction," by Li and colleagues, published in Briefings in Bioinformatics in 2024. 今天Track B选读的是李(Li)等人发表在《Briefings in Bioinformatics》上的论文,题目是《一种基于大语言模型的文献疾病-基因关联预测框架》,发表于2024年。 It's fully open access, and it ships something we can actually reuse: a two-stage LLM pipeline, called LORE, that reads PubMed abstracts and builds a traceable knowledge graph of disease-gene relationships, then ranks gene pathogenicity from it. 这篇论文完全开放获取,而且提供了我们可以直接复用的东西:一个名为LORE的两阶段LLM流程,能够阅读PubMed摘要、构建可追溯的疾病-基因关系知识图谱,并据此对基因致病性进行排序。 This isn't about CFTR specifically — it's a general-purpose literature-mining tool — but it's exactly the kind of infrastructure this very podcast leans on manually every day. 这篇论文本身不针对CFTR——它是一个通用的文献挖掘工具——但它正是我们这档播客本身每天都在手动依赖的那类基础设施。

JORDAN Before the methods — what's ClinVar, and why does this paper keep treating it as the answer key? 在讲方法之前——ClinVar是什么,为什么这篇论文一直把它当作标准答案来用?

ALEX ClinVar is a public, expert-curated database run by the NIH that records clinical assertions about the relationship between specific genetic variants — and by extension, genes — and disease, submitted and reviewed by clinical labs and expert panels. ClinVar是由美国国立卫生研究院(NIH)运营的一个公开的、经专家审核的数据库,记录了关于特定基因变异——进而扩展到基因本身——与疾病之间关系的临床论断,由临床实验室和专家小组提交并审核。 It's authoritative but slow and small relative to the literature: this paper cites just 3,004 disease-gene pairs with ClinVar-curated pathogenicity, against 652,701 pairs that simply co-occur somewhere in PubMed abstracts for those same 2,097 diseases — a 200-times gap between what's been formally curated and what's merely been written about. 它具有权威性,但相对于文献总量而言,更新速度慢、规模也小:这篇论文引用的数据显示,针对同样的2,097种疾病,ClinVar经过审核标注致病性的疾病-基因对只有3,004对,而仅仅在PubMed摘要中共同出现过的疾病-基因对却有652,701对——也就是说,已经被正式审核过的内容和仅仅被提及过的内容之间存在着200倍的差距。 That gap is exactly the opportunity this paper is chasing: can an LLM read the unstructured 652,701 and rank which of them are most likely to deserve ClinVar-grade curation attention next? 而这正是这篇论文所瞄准的机会:一个LLM能否读懂这未经结构化的652,701对关系,并排序出哪些最有可能值得下一步获得ClinVar级别的审核关注?

JORDAN And the core problem with just asking an LLM directly — why build this two-stage pipeline instead? 直接问LLM本身有什么核心问题——为什么要专门搭建这样一个两阶段流程?

ALEX Two problems, and the paper names both directly. 两个问题,论文都直接点明了。 First, LLMs hallucinate — they generate fluent, plausible-sounding claims that aren't actually grounded in any specific source, which is a serious liability if you're trying to build a knowledge base someone might act on clinically. 第一,LLM会产生幻觉——它们会生成流畅、听起来合理,但实际上并没有落在任何具体来源上的说法,如果你想构建一个可能被用于临床决策的知识库,这是一个严重的隐患。 Second, even when an LLM isn't outright hallucinating, its knowledge lives in opaque internal parameters, so there's no way to trace a specific claim back to the specific paper and sentence that supports it. 第二,即使LLM没有直接产生幻觉,它的知识也存在于不透明的内部参数中,因此无法把某一条具体的论断追溯回支持它的具体论文和句子。 Retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG, tries to fix the traceability problem by restricting the model to a small set of retrieved documents per query, but that retrieval step is usually a shallow sentence-similarity search, which misses nuanced or indirectly-phrased evidence and doesn't scale to synthesizing across the whole literature at once. 检索增强生成(RAG)试图通过把模型限制在每次查询检索到的一小部分文档内来解决可追溯性问题,但这种检索步骤通常只是浅层的句子相似度搜索,会遗漏措辞含蓄或间接表达的证据,也无法扩展到一次性综合整个文献库的规模。 LORE's answer is to flip the order: instead of retrieving a few documents per question, read every document once, up front, and distill each one into small, verifiable, atomic factual statements — then build the knowledge graph from those statements, so every claim in the final graph traces back to one specific article. LORE给出的答案是反过来做:不是针对每个问题检索几篇文档,而是提前把每一篇文档都读一遍,把每篇文章提炼成一些简短的、可验证的原子事实陈述——然后从这些陈述出发构建知识图谱,这样最终图谱中的每一条论断都能追溯回一篇具体的文章。

ALEX On the methods themselves: LLM-ORE, the first stage, stands for open relation extraction — they prompt GPT-3.5 with a worked example showing exactly what a good atomic relation statement looks like, then apply the same prompt to real disease-gene articles from pubmedKB, an existing literature-annotation resource. 具体方法上:第一阶段LLM-ORE,代表"开放关系抽取"——他们用一个示范样例来提示GPT-3.5,展示一条优质的原子关系陈述应该长什么样,然后把同样的提示应用到来自pubmedKB(一个已有的文献标注资源)的真实疾病-基因文章上。 Running this over 1.7 million abstracts produced 11 million relations and 358,888 distinct semantic patterns, or lemmas, which the authors then hand-curated down into a taxonomy of 105 key semantics — tags like "mutation cosegregates with disease" — organized into four classes covering relation type, mutation information, disease genetics, and cohort context. 将这一流程运行在170万篇摘要上,产生了1100万条关系和358,888种不同的语义模式(lemma),作者随后手工整理出一套包含105个关键语义的分类体系——比如"突变与疾病共分离"这样的标签——归入四大类,分别涵盖关系类型、突变信息、疾病遗传学和队列背景信息。 The second stage, LLM-EMB, takes all the relations extracted for a given disease-gene pair across every article that mentions it, and compresses them into a single dense 512-dimensional numerical vector — an embedding — that downstream models can actually compute with. 第二阶段LLM-EMB,把针对某个特定疾病-基因对、在所有提及它的文章中抽取出的全部关系,压缩成一个单一的、稠密的512维数值向量——也就是嵌入向量——供下游模型直接计算使用。 On top of those embeddings, they trained ML-Ranker, a gradient-boosted decision tree using a ranking-specific loss function, with supervision from ClinVar labels, to rank genes by predicted pathogenicity for each disease. 在这些嵌入向量的基础上,他们训练了ML-Ranker,一个使用排序专用损失函数的梯度提升决策树模型,以ClinVar标签作为监督信号,针对每种疾病对基因按预测致病性进行排序。

ALEX One thing worth being precise about: the paper calls its knowledge-graph construction "unsupervised," and stage one genuinely is — no labels go into LLM-ORE or LLM-EMB. 有一点值得说清楚:论文把它的知识图谱构建过程称为"无监督"的,而第一阶段确实如此——LLM-ORE和LLM-EMB都没有用到任何标签。 But the headline ranking numbers all come from ML-Ranker, which is trained with ClinVar supervision — so the pipeline as a whole is better described as an unsupervised representation feeding a supervised ranker, not an end-to-end unsupervised system, which matters if you're deciding how much of this recipe transfers to a domain where you don't already have a ClinVar-equivalent label source to train against. 但头条的排序性能数字全部来自经过ClinVar监督训练的ML-Ranker——所以整个流程更准确的描述是"无监督表征喂给一个有监督排序器",而不是端到端的无监督系统,这一点在你考虑这套方法能在多大程度上迁移到一个没有ClinVar级别标签来源的领域时,是很重要的。

在可复现性方面,我认为对我们最相关的一点是:把GPT-3.5换成开源的Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct模型,得到的MAP几乎完全一致,为79.5%,对比GPT-3.5的79.9%;而把这个更小的开源模型运行在全部3,997,496篇存在疾病-基因共现的摘要上——也就是完整范围,而不只是能用ClinVar验证的子集——产生了7400万条关系,覆盖了91.3%的已知ClinVar疾病-基因对,MAP达到81.6%。 JORDAN You mentioned the embeddings hold structure even before the supervised ranker touches them — what does that look like? 你之前提到,在有监督排序器介入之前,这些嵌入向量本身就已经带有结构——具体是什么样的?

ALEX This is the part I find genuinely elegant. 这一部分我觉得确实很精妙。 When they projected the 512-dimensional LLM-EMB embeddings down to two or three dimensions with UMAP and PCA and colored each point by its ClinVar pathogenicity label, the known-pathogenic disease-gene pairs didn't scatter randomly — they clustered into a specific subspace, with a smooth, continuous gradient connecting a dense cluster of "probably not pathogenic" points to a curved arm of increasingly pathogenic ones. 当他们用UMAP和PCA把512维的LLM-EMB嵌入向量投影到二维或三维空间,并按ClinVar致病性标签给每个点上色时,已知致病的疾病-基因对并没有随机散布——而是聚集在一个特定的子空间里,呈现出一条平滑连续的梯度,从一个"大概率非致病"的密集点云,连接到一条致病性逐渐增强的弯曲弧线上。 They call this a "pathogenic flow": because ClinVar's curated labels are sparse compared to the full literature, plenty of gray, unlabeled points sit right in the middle of that gradient, unlabeled not because they're ambiguous but simply because no expert panel has gotten to them yet — and the smooth flow itself is what lets ML-Ranker assign a sensible graded score to those uncurated pairs instead of just a binary in/out-of-ClinVar guess. 他们把这称为"致病性流"(pathogenic flow):由于ClinVar审核过的标签相对于整个文献而言非常稀疏,大量灰色的、未标注的点恰好落在这条梯度的中间地带,之所以未标注,并不是因为它们本身模棱两可,而只是因为还没有专家小组处理到它们——而正是这种平滑的"流",才使得ML-Ranker能够为这些尚未审核的疾病-基因对给出一个有意义的分级评分,而不只是"是否在ClinVar中"这样的二元猜测。 They also found this flow pattern was broadly consistent across different diseases, not a one-off artifact of a single well-studied condition, which is what makes it a plausible general-purpose signal rather than something that only works for the handful of diseases with the richest literature. 他们还发现这种流动模式在不同疾病之间大体上是一致的,而不是某个研究充分的单一疾病特有的偶然现象,这也正是它之所以可能成为一种通用信号、而不只是对文献最丰富的少数几种疾病才有效的原因。
