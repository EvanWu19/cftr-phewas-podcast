True anthropometric errors are rare — the cheapest error-catcher is just a second measurement

Sierra Velez and colleagues (JPGN Reports, 2025) ran a secondary analysis of a Boston Children's Hospital QI project that increased anthropometric measurement rates (weight 67%→84%, height 50%→70%, BMI 42%→70% hospital-wide). Comparing 2016 (pre-QI, n=752 encounters) and 2019 (post-QI, n=970), they chart-reviewed every Z-score extreme (|Z|>6) and every large repeat-measurement difference (|ΔZ|>1) within 26 hours of admission. Extreme-value rates were low throughout (0.2–1.8% across weight/height/BMI), and true errors among those extremes were rarer still (≤0.23% for weight/BMI, ≤1.2% for height) — most extremes were biologically plausible, not data-entry mistakes. Repeat weight measurement actually dropped post-QI (97.6%→83.0%) while repeat BMI rose sharply (17.9%→53.6%, since BMI required a manual second calculation step), and post-QI repeat BMI measurements were far more likely to change a patient's nutrition classification (8.9% vs 2.3% pre-QI) — some by two or three categories. The buildable artifact is a standardized chart-review coding sheet implementing this paper's error-type taxonomy, for coding whatever flagged records need human adjudication.

Plain-English glossary

  • BMI Z-score (pediatric) — a patient's BMI expressed as standard deviations from the mean for their age and sex, using a reference growth curve (CDC in this US study); the standard metric for pediatric nutritional-status classification.（中文：某患者的BMI相对于其同龄同性别人群平均值的标准差数，基于参考生长曲线（本研究中使用CDC标准）；是儿科营养状况分类的标准指标。）
  • PYMS / STAMP — the Pediatric Yorkhill Malnutrition Score and the Screening Tool for the Assessment of Malnutrition in Paediatrics, two formal bedside screening instruments that use BMI/Z-score classification as an input.（中文：儿科约克山营养不良评分和儿科营养不良评估筛查工具，两种以BMI/Z分数分类为输入的正式床旁筛查工具。）
  • "Detected" error (this paper's definition) — an error is only counted as detected if a later, non-erroneous measurement in the record corrects it; encounters without a repeat measurement can't be classified this way, biasing the reported error rate toward a lower bound.（中文：本文中，只有当病历中存在后续一次无误的测量对其进行纠正时，该错误才被计为"已检出"；没有重复测量的病例无法以此方式分类，这使报告的错误率偏向于一个下限。）
  • Conversion error — an anthropometric value entered under the wrong unit system (e.g., pounds recorded as kilograms), the most common true-error type found for height in this study.（中文：以错误的单位制录入的人体测量数值（例如把磅记成千克），是本研究中身高测量最常见的真实错误类型。）
  • Carry-forward / calculation-chain dependency — BMI here is not auto-calculated from weight and height; staff must trigger a separate calculation step, which is why repeat-BMI rates responded so strongly to the QI intervention.（中文：本文中BMI不会由体重和身高自动计算得出；工作人员必须主动触发一个单独的计算步骤，这也是为什么重复BMI测量率对该QI干预反应如此强烈的原因。）

JORDAN Set the scene — what was the actual quality improvement project this is a secondary analysis of? 先介绍一下背景——这是对哪个质量改进项目做的二次分析？

ALEX Boston Children's ran a multidisciplinary QI project to increase how often weight, height, and BMI actually get measured and documented on admission — things like staff training on stadiometers and scales, daily nursing reports, bedside visual reminders, and nutrition champion teams. 波士顿儿童医院开展了一个多学科质量改进项目，旨在提高入院时体重、身高和BMI实际被测量和记录的频率——比如对身高测量仪和体重秤的员工培训、每日护理报告、床边视觉提醒，以及营养倡导团队。 That primary project already showed documented weight rose from 67% to 84%, height from 50% to 70%, and BMI from 42% to 70% hospital-wide. 该主项目已经显示，全院范围内体重的记录率从67%提升到84%，身高从50%提升到70%，BMI从42%提升到70%。 This paper is the secondary analysis asking the natural follow-up question: now that we're measuring more often, are those measurements actually more accurate, and does measuring twice change anything clinically? 这篇论文是对该项目的二次分析，追问了一个自然而然的后续问题：既然我们测量得更频繁了，这些测量结果是否真的更准确了？而重复测量是否会带来任何临床上的实质变化？

JORDAN Before the results — I want the clinical layer here, since I know Z-scores and mixed models cold but not the pediatric nutrition-screening conventions. 在讲结果之前——我想先了解一下临床层面的知识，因为Z分数和混合模型我很熟，但儿科营养筛查的惯例我不太清楚。 What's actually being classified? 具体在对什么进行分类？

ALEX Nutritional status — underweight or malnourished, normal, overweight or obese — gets classified from BMI Z-scores derived from CDC age-and-sex-matched growth curves, and that classification directly feeds formal screening tools used at the bedside, like the Pediatric Yorkhill Malnutrition Score and the Screening Tool for the Assessment of Malnutrition in Paediatrics. 营养状况——体重过轻或营养不良、正常、超重或肥胖——是根据CDC按年龄和性别匹配的生长曲线得出的BMI Z分数来分类的，这个分类结果会直接输入到床旁使用的正式筛查工具中，比如儿科约克山营养不良评分（Pediatric Yorkhill Malnutrition Score）和儿科营养不良评估筛查工具（Screening Tool for the Assessment of Malnutrition in Paediatrics）。 That matters because these Z-scores aren't just descriptive — malnutrition classification directly drives clinical urgency, and weight in kilograms drives medication and fluid dosing, so an error here isn't a cosmetic data problem, it can become a dosing problem. 这一点很重要，因为这些Z分数不仅仅是描述性的——营养不良的分类直接决定了临床处理的紧迫程度，而以千克为单位的体重直接决定了用药和补液剂量，因此这里出现的错误不是无关紧要的数据问题，它可能演变成用药剂量问题。 The error-type vocabulary they use for chart review is also worth knowing: extreme values got classified as either concordant with the patient's growth trend — meaning biologically real — or as a conversion error, a typographical error, or unspecified, while extreme differences between a first and repeat measurement got classified as an error in the first measurement, an error in the second, a genuine clinical change like fluid administration, or biologically plausible variation. 他们在病历复核中使用的错误类型词汇也值得了解：极端数值被分类为与患者生长趋势一致——即生物学上真实存在——或分类为换算错误（conversion error）、录入笔误（typographical error）、或无法归类；而首次测量与重复测量之间的极端差异，则被分类为首次测量错误、二次测量错误、真实的临床变化（比如输液），或生物学上合理的正常波动。

JORDAN How exactly did they define "Z-score extreme," and how did chart review actually work? 他们具体是怎么定义"Z分数极端值"的？病历复核又是怎么进行的？

ALEX A single measurement — weight, height, or BMI — with a Z-score above 6 or below negative 6 counted as an extreme. 单次测量——体重、身高或BMI——Z分数高于6或低于负6，就被判定为极端值。 A difference of more than 1 Z-score, in either direction, between a patient's first and most recent measurement within 26 hours of admission counted as an extreme of difference. 在入院26小时内，患者首次测量与最近一次测量之间Z分数相差超过1（无论正负），则被判定为差异极端。 Every one of those got a full chart review — growth trend, medical status, physical exam — to judge plausibility, and critically, an error only got labeled "detected" if a later, non-erroneous measurement was documented in the record to correct it. 每一个这样的情况都会接受完整的病历复核——生长趋势、医疗状况、体格检查——以判断其合理性，而关键在于，只有当病历中记录了后续一次未出错的测量来纠正它时，这个错误才会被标记为"已检出"（detected）。 That last part is the design choice worth pushing on: the whole detection mechanism depends on a correcting repeat measurement existing at all, so any encounter without one simply can't be classified this way — meaning the reported error rates are likely a lower bound, biased toward encounters that happened to get remeasured. 最后这一点正是值得深挖的设计选择：整个检出机制完全依赖于是否存在一次能够起到纠正作用的重复测量，因此任何没有重复测量的病例根本无法用这种方式被分类——这意味着报告出来的错误率很可能是一个下限，且偏向于那些恰好被重新测量过的病例。

JORDAN What were the actual numbers on extreme-value rates? 极端值发生率的具体数字是多少？

ALEX Low across the board. 总体都很低。 Weight extremes: 0.7% of encounters in 2016, 0.3% in 2019. 体重极端值：2016年占0.7%，2019年占0.3%。 Height extremes: 1.5% in 2016, 1.8% in 2019. 身高极端值：2016年1.5%，2019年1.8%。 BMI extremes: 1.1% in 2016, 0.2% in 2019. BMI极端值：2016年1.1%，2019年0.2%。 And of those extremes, most weren't errors at all — they were biologically plausible. 而在这些极端值中，大多数根本不是错误——它们是生物学上合理的。 Overall, true errors accounted for no more than 0.23% of weight and BMI measurements, and no more than 1.2% of height measurements, and that didn't differ significantly between the pre- and post-QI cohorts. 总体而言，真正的错误在体重和BMI测量中占比不超过0.23%，在身高测量中不超过1.2%，且在QI项目前后两个队列之间没有显著差异。 Height was the interesting outlier here — proportionally more of its extremes turned out to be real errors, mostly unit-conversion mistakes, which the discussion attributes partly to stadiometers at this hospital displaying both metric and US customary units side by side, and partly to height being harder to obtain accurately from a child who's uncooperative or bedbound. 身高在这里是个有意思的例外——按比例来看，它的极端值中有更多被证实是真实错误，主要是单位换算错误，讨论部分将其部分归因于该医院的身高测量仪同时显示公制和美制单位，也部分归因于对一个不配合或卧床的儿童而言，准确测得身高本身就更困难。

JORDAN And the repeat-measurement rates — that's where the QI project's actual behavioral effect shows up, right? 而重复测量率——这才是QI项目实际行为效应体现出来的地方，对吧？

ALEX Right, and it moved in three different directions depending on which measurement you look at. 没错，而且这三种测量各自的变化方向都不一样。 Repeat weight measurement actually dropped, from 97.6% of encounters in 2016 to 83.0% in 2019 — statistically significant. 重复体重测量的比例实际上下降了，从2016年的97.6%降到2019年的83.0%——具有统计学显著性。 Repeat height stayed flat around 28%, no significant change. 重复身高测量基本持平，维持在约28%左右，没有显著变化。 Repeat BMI shot up, from 17.9% to 53.6% — because at this hospital BMI isn't auto-calculated from weight and height, it requires the staff member to take a deliberate second step, and the QI project's education apparently made staff much more aware that step needed to happen. 重复BMI测量则大幅上升，从17.9%升至53.6%——因为在这家医院，BMI不会由体重和身高自动计算得出，需要工作人员主动执行一个额外步骤，而QI项目的培训显然让工作人员更清楚地意识到需要完成这一步。 And here's the finding that actually matters clinically: when a repeat BMI measurement was obtained post-QI, it changed the patient's nutrition classification 8.9% of the time, versus only 2.3% pre-QI — some of those were jumps of two or even three classification levels, like severe malnutrition being reclassified as normal. 而这里有一个在临床上真正重要的发现：在QI项目之后，如果获得了重复的BMI测量，8.9%的情况下会改变患者的营养分类，而在QI项目之前这一比例只有2.3%——其中有些甚至跨越了两级、乃至三级分类，比如严重营养不良被重新分类为正常。

JORDAN The discussion mentioned medication dosing risk — how big a deal is a weight error, practically speaking? 讨论部分提到了用药剂量风险——从实际角度看，一个体重错误有多严重？

ALEX Big enough that the paper cites a separate study where 34% of weight-measurement errors were associated with an actual medication dosing error, since pediatric drug doses, fluids, and electrolytes are calculated directly from weight in kilograms. 严重到论文引用了另一项研究，其中34%的体重测量错误伴随着实际发生的用药剂量错误，因为儿科药物剂量、补液量和电解质剂量都是直接根据以千克为单位的体重来计算的。 That's part of why the scales at this hospital are hard-locked to metric units, preventing staff from accidentally switching to pounds — a simple engineering control that removes an entire error category rather than relying on someone catching it downstream. 这也是为什么这家医院的体重秤被硬性锁定为公制单位，防止工作人员意外切换成磅——这是一个简单的工程控制手段，直接消除了一整类错误，而不是依赖有人在下游发现并纠正它。 Height doesn't carry the same dosing risk day to day, which the authors suggest is part of why repeat height measurement gets less institutional attention than weight, even though height extremes turned out to be more error-prone in this data. 身高在日常中不承担同样的用药风险，作者认为这也是为什么重复身高测量在机构层面得到的重视程度不如体重，尽管在这份数据中，身高的极端值实际上更容易出错。

JORDAN So which pipeline stage does this land on — candidate generation, adjudication, review budget, or evaluation? 那么这项工作对应到流程的哪个环节——候选生成、人工核定、审核工作量，还是评估？

ALEX Squarely adjudication, plus a genuinely useful evaluation benchmark. 明确落在人工核定环节，外加一个真正有用的评估基准。 The detection threshold itself — a fixed Z-score cutoff of plus or minus 6 — is cruder than a spline or mixed-effects candidate generator, so this isn't offering a better flagging rule. 检测阈值本身——固定的正负6的Z分数截断值——比样条模型或混合效应候选生成器要粗糙，所以它提供的不是一个更好的标记规则。 What it does offer is a reusable, chart-review-validated taxonomy for coding flagged extremes once a human sits down with them: conversion error, typographical error, biologically plausible, unspecified, plus first-versus-second-measurement attribution for repeat-difference cases. 它真正提供的是一套可复用的、经过病历复核验证的分类体系，用于在人工审核者坐下来处理这些被标记的极端值时对其进行编码：换算错误、录入笔误、生物学合理、无法归类，以及针对重复测量差异案例的首次/二次测量归因。 On evaluation, it gives a genuine prior: across a large pediatric inpatient sample, extreme values are rare — well under 2% of measurements — and true errors among those extremes are rarer still, which is a useful sanity check for anyone sizing an adjudication queue, even though it comes from a single hospital's 26-hour inpatient window rather than a national longitudinal outpatient registry. 在评估方面，它给出了一个真实的先验：在一个大型儿科住院样本中，极端值是罕见的——远低于2%的测量比例——而在这些极端值中真正的错误更是少之又少，这对任何试图估算人工核定队列规模的人来说，都是一个有用的合理性检验，尽管这个数据来自单一医院26小时住院窗口，而不是全国性的纵向门诊登记库。

JORDAN What's the buildable artifact? 可搭建的工具是什么？

ALEX A structured adjudication coding sheet implementing this paper's exact taxonomy — conversion error, typographical error, biologically plausible, unspecified for single extremes, plus first-versus-second attribution for repeat-difference extremes — as a standardized review form for however many flagged records need a human look. 一个结构化的人工核定编码表，实现这篇论文的确切分类体系——针对单次极端值使用换算错误、录入笔误、生物学合理、无法归类，针对重复测量差异极端值使用首次/二次归因——作为一份标准化的审核表单，供任意数量需要人工查看的标记记录使用。 MVP is a review-form template plus a small script to tally category distributions and check inter-rater consistency across reviewers — difficulty S. 最小可行版本是一份审核表单模板，加上一个用于统计各类别分布、检查审核者之间一致性的小脚本——难度评级为S。

JORDAN Is there a limitation the authors flag that you think matters more than they gave it credit for? 作者提到的局限性里，有没有哪一条你觉得比他们自己承认的更重要？

ALEX The Z-score-only definition of "extreme" is worth sitting with. 仅用Z分数来定义"极端值"这一点值得仔细琢磨。 The authors themselves note that for patients above the 97th percentile, Z-score differences can miss large differences in raw BMI, because the CDC reference charts have sparse data at the extreme upper end, which compresses the Z-score scale there. 作者自己也指出，对于处于第97百分位以上的患者，Z分数差异可能会漏掉原始BMI数值上的巨大差异，因为CDC参考图表在极端高值端的数据本就稀疏，这会压缩该区域的Z分数量表。 That means this study's low error rate is specifically a low rate of Z-score-flaggable errors, and a meaningfully different — and by the authors' own admission, uncounted — category of error could be sitting in exactly the patients where accurate weight matters most clinically, like a child already flagged for obesity-related care. 这意味着这项研究得出的低错误率，具体指的是"可被Z分数标记出的错误"的低发生率，而在临床上最需要精确体重的那部分患者身上——比如已经因肥胖相关问题接受治疗的儿童——很可能恰恰存在着另一类在研究中被承认未被统计到的错误。
