Blurb

Abstract-only, genuinely closed access. A short system-description paper from the Studies in Health Technology and Informatics proceedings series introducing crique, described as free and open-source software that extracts formal eligibility-criteria definitions from clinical study protocols and exports them to OHDSI Atlas for use against the OMOP common data model. The motivating use case named in the abstract is a digital recruitment assistant — matching patients to open trials, or counting feasible candidates for a trial at a site. The paper presents a modular extraction process, describes the implementation, and shows examples of extracted criteria; it then closes with challenges and proposes a strategy to evaluate the accuracy of extracted criteria. That last point is the decisive one for how to file this paper: no accuracy, precision, recall, protocol count, criterion count, timing, or comparison-to-manual-curation number exists in it. It is a tool-availability data point, not an evidence data point. The structural observation worth carrying forward is that exporting to Atlas forces two very different difficulty classes into one pipeline — concept-set resolution (every clinical phrase to standard OMOP concept identifiers with correct domain, vocabulary, and standard-concept flag) and temporal logic ("within the previous twelve months", "at least two occurrences") — and these fail differently: a concept-mapping error is visible on inspection, a temporal-logic error looks entirely reasonable in the Atlas interface. A single pooled accuracy figure would hide that split.

Plain-English glossary

  • Atlas — OHDSI's web application for authoring cohort definitions against an OMOP database.（中文：OHDSI的网页应用，用于针对OMOP数据库编写队列定义。）
  • Cohort definition — A machine-executable specification of which patients count as the study population, expressed as concept sets plus temporal logic.（中文：队列定义，一份机器可执行的规格说明，规定哪些患者算作研究人群，以概念集加时序逻辑表达。）
  • Concept set — An enumerated list of standard vocabulary concept identifiers standing in for a clinical idea like "history of chronic lung disease".（中文：概念集，把"慢性肺病史"这类临床概念逐项列举为标准词汇表概念标识符的清单。）
  • Digital recruitment assistant — A system that matches patients to eligible open trials, or counts feasible candidates at a site for a given trial.（中文：数字化招募助手，把患者匹配到符合条件的在招试验，或为某项试验统计某中心的可行候选人数。）
  • Eligibility criteria — The inclusion and exclusion rules in a study protocol, normally written as natural-language prose for human readers with clinical judgement assumed.（中文：入排标准，研究方案中的纳入与排除规则，通常写成供人阅读的自然语言散文，并假定读者具备临床判断力。）
  • Feasibility counting — Estimating how many eligible candidates exist in an institution's data before deciding whether to open a trial site.（中文：可行性计数，在决定是否开设试验中心前，估算某机构数据中存在多少符合条件的候选者。）
  • OHDSI — Observational Health Data Sciences and Informatics; the open research community that maintains OMOP and its tooling.（中文：维护OMOP及其工具链的开放研究社区。）
  • OMOP CDM — Observational Medical Outcomes Partnership common data model; a standardized table-and-vocabulary schema institutions map their source data into so the same analytic code runs in multiple places.（中文：OMOP通用数据模型，一套标准化的表结构与词汇表方案，各机构把源数据映射进去，从而让同一份分析代码可在多处运行。）
  • Temporal logic (in a cohort definition) — The time-window and occurrence-count portion of a criterion, as opposed to the concept-identity portion.（中文：队列定义中的时序逻辑，指标准里的时间窗与出现次数部分，与概念身份部分相对。）

Methods / tools to lift

  • The protocol-to-executable-query round trip as a reusable pattern, independent of this particular tool.
  • Export-to-a-standard-stack as a design requirement: making OMOP/Atlas the target is what makes the extraction problem well-posed at all; without a common data model you write one extractor per institution.
  • Splitting evaluation by criterion type (concept resolution vs temporal logic) rather than pooling into one accuracy figure — our own addition, motivated by the differing failure visibility.
  • Surfacing rejected alternatives: an extractor that silently resolves ambiguity removes the clinical disagreement that formalization is supposed to expose. Flag every choice point instead.
  • Caution to carry: OMOP conformance is not OMOP uniformity — two sites can both be validly conformant yet represent the same clinical fact in different domains or granularities, so an extractor tuned on one site's conventions can degrade silently at the next.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Priority served: AI-tooling / career positioning (one only). Not the registry DQ track — the reusable thing here is the portfolio pattern, not a CF-registry application.

Artifact: criteria-to-cohort round-trip harness. Difficulty M.

MVP line: take three to five published study protocols whose cohort definitions already exist publicly in OMOP form, run the prose criteria through any extractor, and score the output on cohort agreement rather than text similarity — do the two definitions select overlapping patient sets on the same OMOP instance, and where they diverge, is it a concept-set difference or a temporal-logic difference?

Tool sketch: a CLI taking two cohort-definition JSON files, running both against an OMOP instance (or a credible synthetic OMOP dataset), and emitting a three-panel diff — (1) set-overlap statistics, (2) concept-set-level symmetric difference, (3) a flat list of every temporal window that differs between the two specifications.

The M is entirely in having an OMOP instance to run against, not in the diff logic. Why this artifact: the evaluation strategy this paper proposes but does not execute is the gap, and a cohort-agreement harness is the general-purpose version — it evaluates any extractor including a hand-written one, and small honest reusable evaluation tooling reads well in a tool-development portfolio.

What to verify first

  • Is crique publicly available now, and where — the abstract says free and open source, but does the paper name a live repository?
  • What exactly does it emit: an Atlas-importable cohort-definition JSON, or an intermediate representation a human finishes in Atlas?
  • Does the concept-resolution step handle vocabulary and domain selection, or defer it to the user? That single question decides whether this saves hours or minutes.
  • Does the proposed evaluation strategy in the conclusion measure text-level extraction fidelity or cohort-level agreement, and is it reported per criterion type?
  • On our own side: for any registry-facing use of this pattern, what fraction of the inclusion logic we'd actually want is expressible in the Atlas cohort-definition grammar versus needing custom SQL?
