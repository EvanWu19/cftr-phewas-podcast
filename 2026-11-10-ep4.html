Ep4 (Track C — registry QC, slot 2/2): Disentangling team from geography in anthropometric variance

Dwivedi and colleagues (SSM - Population Health, 2022) use India's NFHS-4 survey (225,002 children under five) to ask how much of the variance in child anthropometric z-scores is attributable to the measuring field team versus the district or community (PSU) they work in. Because one team works across multiple districts and one district has multiple teams, the authors use a cross-classified multilevel model (CCMM) rather than a simple nested one, estimating team- and district-level random effects simultaneously. National unadjusted z-score SDs (1.7 for stunting, 1.4 for wasting, 1.2 for underweight) sit at or above the DHS-recommended 0.8-1.2 band; adjusting for team-level workload covariates reduces these only marginally, while the full variance-partition model shows PSU/community-level factors contribute the largest single share of unexplained variance, with team-level contribution consistently exceeding district-level contribution (0.4-5.4% vs. 0.1-1.5% for stunting, varying by state) — counterintuitively larger in lower-, not higher-, workload states. This episode pairs with ep3 as two different altitudes on the same question (program-level scoring vs. within-program variance decomposition), and lands on adjudication: a buildable diagnostic that partitions residual variance from a primary flagging model across measurement-context strata, to help distinguish artifact from real signal.

Plain-English glossary

  • Primary sampling unit (PSU) — The basic geographic cluster (village or urban census block) a survey samples from, the level at which "community" effects (local nutrition, health-system access) typically show up.（中文：一项调查抽样所依据的基本地理聚类单位（村庄或城市普查区块），通常是"社区"层面效应（如当地营养状况、医疗资源可及性）显现出来的层级。）
  • Cross-classified structure (team × district) — A non-hierarchical arrangement where one team works across multiple districts and one district is covered by multiple teams, requiring a cross-classified rather than simple nested multilevel model to estimate both effects simultaneously.（中文：一种非层级化的结构，一个团队在多个地区工作，一个地区由多个团队覆盖，需要用交叉分类而非简单嵌套的多层模型来同时估计两种效应。）
  • DHS-recommended SD band (0.8-1.2) — The external benchmark from DHS methodological reports for what a well-measured single cross-sectional survey's anthropometric z-score standard deviation should look like.（中文：DHS方法学报告给出的外部参照基准，说明一次测量良好的单次横断面调查，其人体测量z分数的标准差应落在什么范围内。）
  • Measurement-context provenance — "Who measured this, and in what context" as a factor distinct from "where does this patient live or get care" — a registry-provenance concept analogous to a field team's identity in a household survey.（中文："是谁测量的，在什么情境下测量的"，作为一个与"病人居住或就医地点"相区别的因素——这是一个与注册数据溯源相对应的概念，类似于家庭调查中田野团队的身份信息。）

Full citation

Methods / tools to lift

  • Cross-classified multilevel model (CCMM) for measurement-context variance partitioning — estimating team-level and geography-level random effects simultaneously when the two factors don't nest cleanly, to attribute unexplained variance to "who/where measured" rather than patient-level factors.
  • Stepwise adjusted-SD-reduction analysis — a simple first-pass check (OLS, comparing raw vs. adjusted standard deviation) before committing to a full multilevel variance decomposition.
  • External SD-band benchmarking — using an established methodological convention (DHS's 0.8-1.2 SD range) as a sanity-check target rather than a record-level gold standard.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Pipeline stage: (b) adjudication, primary (separating measurement-context artifact from real clinical signal), with secondary (d) evaluation (the external 0.8-1.2 SD band as a benchmark). Buildable artifact — CFFPR Measurement-Context Variance Partition Diagnostic: fit a crossed random-effects model on residuals from the primary growth-trajectory flagging model, using clinic-site and encounter-period as crossed context factors, to estimate what share of residual variance is attributable to measurement context versus patient-level factors. Difficulty: M (medium) — the modeling is routine for someone fluent in mixed models; the work is defining clean, available context-level groupings.

What to verify first

  • Whether a CF clinic's own measurement context (typically a single trained clinician, digital scale and stadiometer, single visit) carries anything like the team/interviewer-driven error profile of a household field survey with rotating two-person teams — the mechanism may simply not transfer.
  • Whether "team" as a concept even maps onto anything meaningful in a clinic context where measurement is usually single-clinician per visit, which could require rethinking the whole disentangling-team-from-site framing before it transfers.
  • Whether the registry's own measurement-context metadata (site, device, time period) is captured at a granularity fine enough to serve as a crossed random-effect factor at all — a question about the registry's own data model, not something this episode can assert.

Not covered: no record-level flag or threshold of its own; does not address unit conversion, digit transposition, decimal-shift, or carry-forward error types; offers no gold-standard-validated sensitivity/specificity figures.

ALEX A primary sampling unit, or PSU, is the basic geographic cluster a survey samples from — a village for rural areas, or a census enumeration block for urban areas in this case — and it's the lowest level at which you'd normally expect "community" effects, like local nutrition or health-system access, to show up. 初级抽样单元，简称PSU，是一项调查抽样的基本地理聚类单位——在这个研究里，农村地区是村庄，城市地区是人口普查登记区块——这是你通常预期"社区"层面效应（比如当地的营养状况或医疗资源可及性）会显现出来的最低层级。 Here's the wrinkle: one field team doesn't work in just one district, and one district isn't covered by just one team — teams get rotated across multiple districts within a state, so team and district form a crossed, not nested, structure. 这里有一个复杂之处：一个田野调查团队并不只在一个地区工作，一个地区也不只由一个团队覆盖——团队会在一个邦内的多个地区之间轮换，所以团队和地区形成的是一种交叉（crossed）结构，而不是嵌套（nested）结构。 That's exactly the situation a cross-classified multilevel model, or CCMM, is built for, letting you estimate team-level and district-level random effects simultaneously without forcing one inside the other. 这正是交叉分类多层模型（cross-classified multilevel model，简称CCMM）所针对的情形，它能让你同时估计团队层面和地区层面的随机效应，而不必强行把一个嵌套进另一个里面。 It's worth noting this maps loosely onto a registry-provenance idea too — "who measured this, and in what context" as a factor distinct from "where does this patient live or get care" — which is the bridge we'll come back to in Beat 6. 值得一提的是，这也和注册数据溯源（provenance）的一个概念大致对应——"是谁测量的，在什么情境下测量的"，作为一个与"病人居住或就医的地理位置"相区别的因素——这也是我们在第六部分会再回过头来讨论的桥梁。
