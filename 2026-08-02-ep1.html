Show notes — Track A — 2026-08-02

Comprehensive review and assessment of multi-species splicing variant prediction: task-specific deep learning models and genomic foundation models — Sun Y, Wang X, Jia Y, Imoto S, Li F, Li C, Song J. Briefings in Bioinformatics, 2026.

Blurb

This paper does double duty: it reviews 35 splice-prediction methods (2018–2026) and then benchmarks 10 of them — task-specific deep-learning models (SpliceAI, Pangolin, MMSplice, SpliceTransformer) against genomic foundation models (DNABERT-2, Evo 2, Nucleotide Transformer, Genos, AlphaGenome) — on matched human, rat, pig, and chicken datasets. The headline: human-trained splice predictors lose ~21–33% AUROC when applied to other species, i.e. they don't generalize on their own. The rescue: freezing a foundation model's embeddings and training a lightweight MLP head on top cuts the cross-species gap to ~9–24% for rat and pig, while zero-shot foundation-model scoring stays weak. For us this lands on Karen / CFTR variant interpretation: the frozen-embedding-plus-light-classifier pattern is a cheap recipe for a CFTR splice-triage tool that ranks intronic and near-splice variants and flags where predictors disagree (the VUS worth human review).

Plain-English glossary

  • AUROC — area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve; ranking-quality score for a binary classifier.（中文：受试者工作特征曲线下面积，衡量二分类模型把阳性排在阴性之前的能力。）
  • Cryptic splice site — a latent sequence that the spliceosome wrongly uses as an exon boundary when a variant activates it.（中文：隐蔽剪接位点——平时不用、被变异激活后被错误当作外显子边界的序列。）
  • Embedding — a learned numeric feature vector a model produces to represent an input sequence.（中文：嵌入表示——模型为输入序列学到的数值特征向量。）
  • Exon / Intron — exons are the retained coding pieces of a transcript; introns are the spliced-out segments between them.（中文：外显子是转录本中被保留的编码片段；内含子是它们之间被剪除的片段。）
  • Genomic foundation model (GFM) — a large model pretrained on raw genome sequence with no specific task, like a language model for DNA.（中文：基因组基础模型——在原始基因组序列上、不针对具体任务预训练的大模型，相当于 DNA 的语言模型。）
  • HGVS — the standardized nomenclature for writing DNA/protein variants.（中文：变异命名的标准化书写规范。）
  • Missense / Nonsense variant — missense swaps one amino acid; nonsense introduces a premature stop codon.（中文：错义变异替换一个氨基酸；无义变异引入提前终止密码子。）
  • Splicing / splice variant — splicing stitches exons and removes introns from pre-mRNA; a splice variant disrupts that process.（中文：剪接把外显子拼接、去除内含子；剪接变异会破坏这一过程。）
  • sQTL — splicing quantitative trait locus; a variant statistically associated with population differences in transcript splicing.（中文：剪接数量性状位点——在群体中与转录本剪接差异有统计关联的变异。）
  • VUS — variant of uncertain significance; not confidently classified pathogenic or benign.（中文：意义未明的变异——尚未被有把握地判定为致病或良性。）
  • Zero-shot scoring — using a pretrained model's raw output with no task-specific fine-tuning.（中文：零样本打分——直接用预训练模型的原始输出，不做任务微调。）

Methods / tools to lift

  • SpliceAI — deep CNN scoring per-position splice-site probability from raw DNA; workhorse splice predictor.
  • Pangolin — splice-effect predictor extending the SpliceAI idea across tissues/species.
  • MMSplice / SpliceTransformer — modular and transformer-based splice-effect models.
  • Frozen-embedding + light MLP head — take a foundation model's embeddings (Evo 2, DNABERT-2, Genos), freeze them, train a small classifier on labeled variants; the paper's key transfer recipe.
  • sQTL-based benchmark construction — using population sQTLs as positives + class-balanced neutral variants as negatives to build labeled sets where experimental validation is scarce.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Serves: Karen / CFTR variant interpretation. MVP (paste-ready): A notebook that scores every CFTR intronic + near-splice-site variant with SpliceAI and Pangolin, then adds Evo 2 embeddings into a small logistic head trained on CFTR2's classified splice variants. Input: CFTR variants in HGVS. Output: ranked splice-disruption table with a tool-disagreement flag for triage. Difficulty: M.

What to verify first

  • Calibration — high SpliceAI scores are not pathogenicity probabilities; map scores to CFTR2 clinical classes before reading as risk.
  • Off-distribution performance — check specifically on deep-intronic CFTR variants (e.g. 3849+10kbC>T, 3272-26A>G), not just canonical-site variants; the paper's cross-species drop is the warning.
  • Label leakage — ensure Evo 2 / SpliceAI didn't see CFTR2 variants in training; use a clean held-out split.

JORDAN Before we go further, I want the vocabulary straight. 在往下走之前，我想先把词汇搞清楚。 What exactly is a splice variant? 到底什么是剪接变异 (splice variant)？

ALEX Good place to start. 这是个好起点。 Your genes are not read as one continuous stretch; the pre-messenger-RNA is chopped up, and only the exons — the coding pieces — get stitched together, while the introns between them are cut out. 你的基因并不是被当作一整段连续序列来读取的；前体信使 RNA (pre-mRNA) 会被切开，只有外显子 (exon)——也就是编码片段——被拼接在一起，而它们之间的内含子 (intron) 则被剪除掉。 That cutting-and-stitching is splicing, and the cell decides where to cut using short sequence signals at the exon–intron boundaries plus a swarm of nearby regulatory motifs. 这种切割与拼接就是剪接 (splicing)，细胞依靠外显子—内含子边界上的短序列信号，加上附近一群调控基序 (regulatory motif) 来决定在哪里下刀。 A splice variant is a DNA change that disrupts one of those signals, so the cell cuts in the wrong place — it might skip an exon, keep an intron, or activate a hidden "cryptic" splice site. 剪接变异就是一处破坏了这些信号的 DNA 改变，使细胞在错误的位置下刀——它可能跳过一个外显子、保留一个内含子，或者激活一个隐藏的“隐蔽”剪接位点 (cryptic splice site)。 The catch for people like us is that a variant can sit inside an intron, far from any protein-coding letter, and still wreck the protein by breaking splicing. 对我们这类人来说，棘手之处在于：一个变异可以位于内含子深处，离任何蛋白质编码字母都很远，却仍然通过破坏剪接而毁掉蛋白质。

JORDAN So these are the variants that a naive "does it change an amino acid" filter would miss entirely. 所以这些正是那种朴素的“它有没有改变氨基酸”过滤器会完全漏掉的变异。 And in CF that matters because CFTR has famous intronic culprits. 在囊性纤维化 (CF) 里这很重要，因为 CFTR 基因就有著名的内含子元凶。

ALEX Exactly — 3849+10kbC>T and the 3272-26A>G variant are both deep-intronic splice changes in CFTR that create cryptic splice sites. 正是如此——3849+10kbC>T 和 3272-26A>G 都是 CFTR 中位于内含子深处的剪接改变，会制造出隐蔽剪接位点。 If you only score missense and nonsense changes, you never see them coming. 如果你只给错义 (missense) 和无义 (nonsense) 改变打分，你根本无法预见它们。 That's the whole reason splice predictors exist. 这正是剪接预测工具存在的全部理由。

JORDAN Okay, so what was the problem this paper set out to solve? 好，那么这篇论文想解决的问题是什么？

ALEX The background is that splice prediction in humans is, frankly, a solved-ish problem. 背景是这样的：在人类身上，剪接预测坦白说已经算是差不多解决了的问题。 Tools like SpliceAI — a deep convolutional network from 2019 that reads raw DNA sequence and outputs a probability that each position is a splice site — reach very high accuracy on human variants. 像 SpliceAI 这样的工具——一个 2019 年的深度卷积网络 (convolutional network)，读取原始 DNA 序列并输出每个位置是剪接位点的概率——在人类变异上达到了很高的准确度。 SpliceAI（一个读取 DNA 序列、预测剪接位点概率的深度学习模型）在人类数据上表现极好。 The authors' worry is different: does any of that skill transfer to other species? 但作者担心的是另一件事：这种能力能不能迁移到其他物种身上？ For agricultural genomics, comparative biology, and non-model organisms, you often have no experimentally validated splice variants to train on. 对于农业基因组学、比较生物学和非模式生物来说，你往往没有经过实验验证的剪接变异可供训练。 So they ask: if a model learned splicing on human data, how badly does it fail on rat, pig, or chicken? 于是他们问：如果一个模型是在人类数据上学会剪接的，那它在大鼠、猪或鸡身上会失败得多惨？

ALEX Because the failure mode is the real lesson. 因为这种失败模式本身才是真正的教训。 Cross-species transfer is a stress test of whether a model learned the actual biology of splicing or just memorized human-specific quirks. 跨物种迁移是一次压力测试，检验模型究竟学到了剪接的真实生物学，还是只是记住了人类特有的怪癖。 The second question underneath it — can a large pretrained "genomic foundation model" rescue the transfer? — is exactly the design question you face when you decide which predictor to trust on an under-studied CFTR variant. 藏在它下面的第二个问题——一个大型预训练的“基因组基础模型 (genomic foundation model)”能不能挽救这种迁移？——正是你在决定该信任哪个预测工具去评估一个研究不足的 CFTR 变异时所面对的设计问题。 A genomic foundation model, or GFM, is a large model pretrained on huge amounts of raw genome sequence with no specific task in mind, the way a language model is pretrained on text. 基因组基础模型，即 GFM，是在海量原始基因组序列上、不针对任何特定任务进行预训练的大模型，就像语言模型在文本上预训练那样。 GFM（在海量基因组序列上预训练、不针对具体任务的大模型，类比文本语言模型）。

JORDAN Before the build — why is this even hard? 在讲构建之前——这为什么很难？ Couldn't you just align the human model onto another genome? 你不能直接把人类模型对齐到另一个基因组上吗？

ALEX You'd think so, but the splicing code itself drifts between species. 你会这么以为，但剪接码本身在物种之间会漂移。 The core intron-exon boundary signals are conserved, but the surrounding auxiliary motifs — the enhancers and silencers that fine-tune where the cut happens — diverge, sometimes a lot. 核心的内含子—外显子边界信号是保守的，但周围的辅助基序——那些微调下刀位置的增强子和沉默子——会发生分歧，有时分歧很大。 A model trained on human data over-fits to human-specific auxiliary grammar, so when you feed it a rat sequence it's reading a dialect it never learned. 一个在人类数据上训练的模型会过拟合到人类特有的辅助语法，所以当你喂给它一段大鼠序列时，它读的是一种它从未学过的方言。 That's why simple sequence alignment doesn't save you — the letters may line up while the regulatory meaning does not. 这就是为什么单纯的序列比对救不了你——字母也许对得上，但调控含义对不上。

JORDAN Why does freezing the big model matter, rather than fine-tuning the whole thing? 为什么是冻结大模型，而不是对整个模型做微调？

ALEX Two reasons, and both are practical for someone building on a small labeled set. 两个原因，对在小标签集上搭建的人来说都很实际。 First, cost — a genome foundation model has billions of parameters, and fine-tuning all of them needs serious compute and a lot of labeled data you usually don't have. 第一，成本——一个基因组基础模型有数十亿参数，对所有参数做微调需要巨大的算力和大量你通常没有的标签数据。 Second, over-fitting — with only a few hundred or few thousand labeled CFTR splice variants, training a small head on frozen features is far less likely to memorize your tiny dataset than unfreezing the whole network. 第二，过拟合——在只有几百或几千个标签化 CFTR 剪接变异的情况下，在冻结特征上训练一个小头，比解冻整个网络要不容易记住你那点小数据集得多。 The frozen-embedding recipe is precisely the move that fits the small-data regime we actually live in. 冻结嵌入这个配方，恰恰是契合我们实际身处的小数据情境的那一招。
