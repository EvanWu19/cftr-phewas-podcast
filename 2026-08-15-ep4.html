How did this episode land for you?

Blurb

Van Buuren (2023, Journal of Statistical Software) presents the broken stick model — a two-step approach to irregular longitudinal data that first converts each subject's idiosyncratic visit timing into a shared, aligned set of repeated measures at fixed knots (breakpoints), then hands those repeated measures to whatever substantive analysis is of interest. The model is formally a special case of the linear mixed model, recoding time as a linear B-spline with subject as the grouping factor; between-knot trajectories are assumed straight, random effects multivariate normal, and missingness at random (including with respect to future values). The R package brokenstick v2.5.0 implements it, with two estimation methods and options to stabilize the random-effects covariance in sparse regions. Six worked applications are demonstrated on Dutch growth cohorts (SMOCC, Terneuzen), including critical-period detection for adult overweight (the 4-6y BMI-gain window improved adult-BMI prediction from 45.3% to 53.6% explained variance, p=0.00019), model-based curve interpolation that correctly reproduces early-infancy growth acceleration where naive linear interpolation does not, and curve matching — a nearest-neighbor donor technique for personalized trajectory prediction. This is the direct theoretical reference for spline random-intercept/random-slope trajectory models used as candidate generators in registry outlier-detection pipelines, and lands primarily on (a) candidate generation.

Plain-English Glossary (alphabetical)

  • Broken stick model — represents a subject's trajectory as a series of connected straight lines between fixed, shared knots; formally a linear mixed model with time coded as a linear B-spline. 断棍模型——将受试者的轨迹表示为固定共享节点之间一系列相连的直线段；形式上是一个把时间编码为线性B样条的线性混合模型。
  • Critical period (developmental epidemiology) — an age window during which a change in a trait (e.g., BMI gain) has an independent, non-redundant contribution to predicting a later outcome (e.g., adult overweight), beyond just the trait's value at the window's end. 关键窗口期（发育流行病学）——某个年龄区间内某项特征的变化（如BMI增量），除了该区间末端的数值之外，还对预测后期结局（如成年超重）有独立、非冗余的贡献。
  • Irregular longitudinal data — data where each subject's actual measurement times differ from both the scheduled times and from each other, as opposed to values being flatly missing at otherwise-common timepoints. 不规则纵向数据——每个受试者的实际测量时间既偏离计划时间、彼此之间也各不相同的数据，区别于在共同时间点上单纯缺失数值的情况。
  • Knot (breakpoint) — a user-chosen, fixed time point (e.g., birth, 1y, 2y) shared across all subjects where the fitted trajectory is allowed to bend; between two consecutive knots the trajectory is assumed to be a straight line. 节点（断点）——用户选定的、所有受试者共享的固定时间点（如出生、1岁、2岁），拟合轨迹在此处可以发生弯折；两个相邻节点之间轨迹假设为直线。
  • MAR (missing at random), future-inclusive — this model's specific missingness assumption: missingness may depend on the observed data at all timepoints, including future, not-yet-observed outcomes — more permissive than typical past-only MAR assumptions, and what makes the model usable for imputing intermittent gaps. MAR（随机缺失），涵盖未来——本模型特有的缺失假设：缺失情况可依赖于所有时间点上的已观测数据，包括尚未观测到的未来结局——比典型的仅涉及过去的MAR假设更宽松，也是该模型能用于插补间歇性缺口的原因。
  • SDS (standard deviation score) — same concept as a z-score in growth data; how many SDs a measurement sits from an age/sex-specific reference median. SDS（标准差评分）——生长数据中与z-score相同的概念；指某测量值偏离特定年龄性别参考中位数的标准差数。

Methods/Tools to Lift

  • Broken stick model: linear mixed model with time recoded as a linear B-spline (relative interval position) and subject as grouping factor — no global intercept, k+1 local knot-level intercepts instead.
  • brokenstick R package (v2.5.0): fit, predict, impute, and visualize broken stick estimates; two estimation methods plus random-effects covariance stabilization options (constrained matrix, Kasim-Raudenbush sampler, Argyle correlation model).
  • Explicit knot-placement guidance: keep knot count at or below average visits per subject; avoid placing knots in sparsely-filled data regions.
  • Critical-period detection design: test whether a period's gain score has an independent contribution to predicting a later outcome beyond the endpoint value alone (nested-model F-test).
  • Model-based curve interpolation as an alternative to naive linear interpolation in raw or SDS scale.
  • Curve matching: nearest-neighbor donor-trajectory retrieval for personalized future-outcome prediction, visualized as a bundle of plausible-futures curves.

Where It Lands + Buildable Artifact

Pipeline stage: primarily (a) candidate generation — this is the direct theoretical/methodological family that spline random-intercept/random-slope trajectory candidate generators belong to, the reference to check any such model's assumptions against. Secondarily touches (d) evaluation, since a well-fit broken-stick trajectory supplies a principled expected-value baseline and prediction interval at any knot, which is what a residual-based flagging rule needs. No claims made about any registry's actual current trajectory model specification, knot choices, or fit quality.

Buildable artifact: brokenstick-baseline-notebook — fit the brokenstick R package to a synthetic longitudinal height/weight dataset with a knot grid chosen per this paper's own guidance, then use the fitted per-knot predicted values and prediction intervals as an expected-trajectory baseline for a residual-based flagging rule, benchmarked against a simpler two-knot or population-cutoff baseline. MVP: synthetic-data generator + brokenstick fit + side-by-side fitted-vs-naive-linear baseline comparison at held-out ages. Difficulty: S.

What to Verify First

  • The straight-line-between-knots assumption can bias genuinely concave-in-time processes (e.g., raw height growth); the paper recommends modeling the SDS-transformed outcome specifically to avoid this — a deliberate scale choice, not a default.
  • Knot placement is a judgment call informed by the data's own visit-timing density, not fully automatic — two analysts could reasonably choose different grids and get different local behavior, especially at sparse ages, which matters for reproducibility.
  • The future-inclusive missing-at-random assumption is a real modeling choice with real implications for what "expected value" means at a knot — understand it fully before treating a broken-stick prediction as a neutral, assumption-free yardstick.
  • Random-effects covariance stabilization (constrained matrix / Kasim-Raudenbush / Argyle) trades some model flexibility for stability in sparse regions — which option (if any) is appropriate depends on the data's own sparsity pattern.
  • Nothing here says anything about how any registry's own trajectory model is currently specified, what knots it uses, or how well it fits.

Pipeline Stage

(a) Candidate generation — primary. (d) Evaluation — secondary, via the expected-value/prediction-interval baseline the fitted model supplies.

Error Types Covered / Not Covered

Covers: none directly — this is a trajectory-modeling substrate, not an error-flagging rule; it supplies the expected-value and uncertainty machinery a residual-based flagging rule would build on top of.

Does not cover: any specific error type (unit conversion, digit transposition, decimal shift, carry-forward, real-clinical-event confusion) — flagging logic and thresholds are left entirely to whoever builds a rule on top of the fitted model; the paper itself never proposes a flagging cutoff.
