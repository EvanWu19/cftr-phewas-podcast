Ep4 — Track C — Comparing Methods for Identifying Biologically Implausible Values in Height, Weight, and BMI Among Youth (Lawman 2015)

Abstract-only (no full-text route available — flagged on-air). Lawman and colleagues surveyed the epidemiologic literature on childhood anthropometrics and found roughly 41% of large studies (n>1,000) didn't address biologically implausible values (BIVs) at all. Among studies that did, they catalogued eleven distinct BIV identification methods and applied all eleven to the same longitudinal dataset — 13,662 students across 55 low-income Philadelphia schools, kindergarten through 8th grade, baseline plus one-year follow-up, 2011–2012. The result: severe-obesity prevalence estimates were fairly stable across methods (7.2%–8.6%, about a 1.2-fold spread), but BIV prevalence estimates ranged from 0.04% to 1.68% — roughly a 40-fold spread on the exact same measurements from the exact same children, purely from method choice. The eleven methods mostly agree on who counts as obese; they mostly disagree on which values to exclude before making that count. This is primarily a pipeline-stage (a) candidate-generation finding — hard evidence that method choice itself is a major, quantified source of flagging variability — and touches (d) evaluation in that a 40-fold spread with no gold standard argues for reporting a sensitivity range rather than a single point estimate. Buildable: biv-method-sensitivity-sweep (S/M) — reimplement several published BIV method families on synthetic pediatric data and report the spread in flagged prevalence, as an explicit sensitivity-to-method-choice check before committing to one rule.

Plain-English glossary

Biologically implausible value (BIV) — A height, weight, or BMI measurement outside what's physiologically possible or plausible for that person; the population/growth-chart-cutoff-based cousin of yesterday's individually-conditioned "implausible value."（中文:身高、体重或BMI测量值超出该个体生理上可能或合理的范围;是昨天讲的"以个体为条件的不合理值"在基于人群/生长图表阈值这一脉络下的对应概念。）

Severe obesity (pediatric) — A defined clinical category, generally BMI at or above 120% of the age- and sex-specific 95th percentile on a reference growth chart.（中文:一个明确定义的临床类别,通常指BMI达到或超过参考生长图表上按年龄和性别划分的第95百分位数的120%。）

Cross-sectional BIV method — A rule that flags implausibility by comparing a single measurement to a population reference, without accounting for that individual's own measurement history (contrast with yesterday's conditional-percentile approach).（中文:一种通过将单次测量值与人群参考标准比较来标记不合理性的规则,不考虑该个体自身的测量历史,与昨天的条件百分位数方法形成对比。）
