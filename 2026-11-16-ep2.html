How did this episode land for you?

PGS Browser: a public platform for personalized polygenic score analysis and interpretation

Kolosov and colleagues on the FinnGen team benchmark 3,168 polygenic score (PGS) models — most drawn from the public PGS Catalog — against 473,681 FinnGen biobank participants, a cohort genuinely distinct from UK Biobank, the source of most PGS development data. They build ancestry-adjusted reference distributions for every score, run a four-design PheWAS atlas yielding 439,070 significant phenotypic associations, and show that combining multiple scores (elastic-net multi-PGS models) improves prediction over a single best score for most binary disease endpoints. Only 22 of 3,168 models cleared a quality bar for public release, and of those, only 11 survived validation in non-White-British UK Biobank participants across at least half of six tested ancestry groups — a sobering generalization-attrition number worth remembering alongside the headline claim. The release itself, a public platform at pgs.nchigm.org backed by pgsb-cli (an open-source, Docker-based command-line tool), is the real Track B artifact: it computes an individual's ancestry-adjusted percentile entirely on the user's own machine and sends only that aggregated number to the web interface, never raw genotypes. That local-compute-then-share-only-the-summary pattern is the piece worth porting into any registry or RWE context — a Federated PGS Percentile Client is this episode's buildable artifact.

Plain-English glossary

  • PGS (polygenic score) — A single risk or trait score for an individual, computed by summing the small effects of thousands to millions of genomic variants.（中文：多基因评分，把全基因组里成千上万甚至上百万个位点各自的微小效应加总起来，为个体计算出的单一风险或性状评分。）
  • PRS (polygenic risk score) — The disease-specific version of a PGS.（中文：多基因风险评分，是专门针对某种疾病的PGS。）
  • PheWAS (phenome-wide association study) — Testing one genetic score or variant against many phenotypes at once, rather than testing many variants against one pre-chosen phenotype (the reverse of a GWAS).（中文：表型组关联分析，对一个遗传评分或变异同时检验它与许多种表型的关联，而不是像GWAS那样对许多变异检验与一个预先选定表型的关联，方向正好相反。）
  • FinnGen — A large Finnish biobank project that links genotype data to national health registries; notable here for having minimal sample overlap with UK Biobank, the cohort most PGS Catalog models were originally built on.（中文：一个将基因型数据与芬兰国家健康登记系统关联起来的大型生物样本库项目，在本文中之所以重要，是因为它和大多数PGS Catalog模型最初训练所用的UK Biobank队列几乎没有样本重叠。）
  • PGS Catalog — The main public repository of published polygenic score models.（中文：收录已发表多基因评分模型的主要公开数据库。）
  • Liability-scale variance explained (R²) — How much of a binary disease's underlying, continuous susceptibility ("liability") scale a score accounts for, allowing comparison of explanatory power across diseases with very different prevalence.（中文：一个评分能解释二元疾病背后那个连续的潜在"易患性"量尺中多大比例的方差，用于在患病率差异很大的疾病之间比较评分的解释力。）
  • MHC region (major histocompatibility complex) — A gene cluster on chromosome 6 with an unusually dense and complex linkage pattern, often excluded from genetic analyses because it can generate spurious associations with unrelated, especially autoimmune, traits.（中文：六号染色体上的主要组织相容性复合体基因簇，这段区域的变异连锁模式异常密集复杂，分析时常被单独剔除，因为它容易对无关性状（尤其是自身免疫性状）产生虚假关联。）
  • D-calibration — A statistical check of whether a survival model's predicted probability distribution matches observed outcomes, used here as one of the criteria for releasing a public risk model.（中文：一种统计检验方法，用于判断生存模型预测的概率分布是否与观测到的实际结局相符，本文把它作为发布公开风险模型的筛选标准之一。）

Full citation

Kolosov N, et al. "PGS Browser: a public platform for personalized polygenic score analysis and interpretation." Nature Communications. 2026. PMID: 42323305 | PMCID: PMC13439439 | DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-74461-7

Methods / tools to lift

  • pgsb-cli — a Docker-based local-compute command-line tool that matches a user's variants, computes ancestry-adjusted scores and percentiles, and sends only aggregated percentiles to the web interface — never raw genotypes.
  • PLINK 2.0 --score — the underlying raw PGS calculation engine.
  • 1000 Genomes PC-projection ancestry adjustment — residualizing a score on principal components so percentiles are more comparable across ancestry groups.
  • Elastic-net multi-PGS integration (scikit-learn / scikit-survival) — combining many single-trait scores into one predictive model for a target endpoint.
  • Four-design PheWAS atlas — intact, survival, exclusion (drops target-phenotype cases), and noMHC (drops MHC-region variants) as complementary ways to characterize a score's secondary associations.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Lands on AI-tooling / career (not Karen/Liz — no claim about CFFPR-to-OMOP mapping). Buildable artifact: Federated PGS Percentile Client — a small local tool, modeled on pgsb-cli, that takes a public PGS Catalog score file plus a synthetic/toy genotype dataset, matches and scores locally, projects onto 1000 Genomes PCs for an ancestry-adjusted percentile, and outputs only that single aggregated number — demonstrating the privacy-preserving local-compute-then-share-only-the-summary pattern on toy data. Input: a PGS Catalog score definition file + synthetic genotype data. Difficulty: M.

What to verify first

  • The generalization-attrition rate itself: only 11 of 22 candidate public models survived non-White-British UK Biobank validation, and even those only held up in roughly half (3 of 6) of the ancestry groups tested — the "ancestry-adjusted" framing needs to be read against this, not instead of it.
  • The hidden sample-overlap risk the authors themselves flag: manual review excluded 460 models with known FinnGen overlap, but incomplete cohort-provenance reporting (especially for meta-analysis-derived scores) means some residual, undisclosed overlap likely survived that filter, leaving every within-FinnGen AUC and multi-PGS gain number with an unquantified circularity risk.
  • Whether the specific numeric thresholds used to gate public release (tdAUC > 0.65 in FinnGen, D-calibration p > 0.99, ΔAUC ≥ 0.01 over null) are the right bar for a different use case, rather than assuming they transfer as-is.
