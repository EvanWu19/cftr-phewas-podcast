Blurb

Wang et al. (2020, Applied Clinical Informatics) report a single-center case study that compiled, implemented, and evaluated 63,397 rule-based data-quality checks for electronic health record data, using a design-science framework to design, demonstrate, test, and evaluate a scalable rule-management approach. The 63,397 rules were partitioned into just 28 logic templates managed through a knowledge table — the governance mechanism that keeps a rule set this large maintainable. Applying the rules surfaced 819,683 discrepancies, generated by only 4.5% of the rules (a long-tail utilization pattern), and 9 of 11 participating clinical and operational leaders confirmed the flagged items were real data-quality problems worth acting on. This is an abstract-only source: we flag that explicitly, and do not go beyond the numbers the abstract reports. For Runyu, this lands as a method-only reference for Liz's CFFPR data-quality assignment — specifically as a candidate-generation pattern (templates + knowledge table for managing many hard plausibility rules), not as any claim about CFFPR's actual rules or flag rates. The buildable artifact is a small "Height/Weight Plausibility Rule-Bank": a handful of hard logical checks (unit-mismatch, biologically-impossible single-visit jump, impossible BMI, decimal-shift) organized into logic templates with one parameter lookup table, run over a flat visit-level CSV of patient-id/visit-date/height/weight to output a flagged-candidate list with a reason code — difficulty S.

Plain-English glossary

  • CDC vs WHO growth charts — Two different pediatric growth-chart reference standards (U.S. CDC charts and the international WHO charts) that convert a child's raw height/weight into age- and sex-adjusted percentiles or z-scores; they're built from different reference populations, so the same raw measurement can map to a different percentile depending on which chart is used.（中文:CDC生长曲线图(美国疾病控制与预防中心)与WHO生长曲线图(世界卫生组织)是两套不同的儿科生长参照标准,用于把儿童的身高体重原始值换算成按年龄和性别调整后的百分位数或z分数;由于两者的参照人群不同,同一个原始测量值在两套图表下可能对应不同的百分位。）
  • Kahn framework — A widely-cited taxonomy from OMOP-adjacent data-quality work that categorizes EHR data checks into dimensions such as completeness, conformance, and plausibility, giving a common vocabulary for what a given rule is actually checking.（中文:Kahn框架是在OMOP相关数据质量工作中被广泛引用的一套分类体系,把电子健康记录的数据检查分为完整性、一致性、合理性等维度,为描述一条规则到底在检查什么提供了通用词汇。）
  • LMS-based growth modeling — A statistical method (Lambda-Mu-Sigma) for converting raw anthropometric measurements into age-specific z-scores by modeling how the skewness, median, and spread of the measurement distribution change with age; it underlies both the CDC and WHO growth-chart systems.（中文:基于LMS(Lambda-Mu-Sigma)的生长建模是一种统计方法,通过建模测量值分布的偏度、中位数和离散程度如何随年龄变化,把原始人体测量值转换为按年龄调整的z分数;CDC和WHO的生长曲线系统都建立在这一方法之上。）
  • Modulator-era weight effects — A general finding in the published CF literature that highly effective CFTR modulators (e.g., elexacaftor-tezacaftor-ivacaftor) are associated with shifts in weight trajectories for many patients, meaning a weight change that would have looked implausible in an earlier era may now be plausible, if unusual.（中文:"调节剂时代体重效应"是已发表的囊性纤维化(CF)文献中的一个普遍性发现,指高效CFTR调节剂(如elexacaftor-tezacaftor-ivacaftor)与许多患者体重轨迹的变化相关,这意味着在更早的时代看起来不合理的体重变化,如今可能变得合理,尽管仍属不寻常。）
  • Plausibility rule — A hand-written logical constraint that a data value must satisfy (e.g., "weight cannot be negative," "a measurement cannot jump outside a biologically possible range between two visits"), as distinct from a statistical model that learns a distribution and flags values in the tail.（中文:合理性规则是数据值必须满足的一条人工编写的逻辑约束(例如"体重不能为负""两次就诊之间的测量值不能跳出生物学上可能的范围"),这与那种学习数据分布、把落在尾部的值标记出来的统计模型不同。）
  • z-score (growth-chart z-score) — The number of standard deviations a measurement sits from the reference median for a child's age and sex, as defined by a chart such as CDC or WHO; a statistical deviation measure, distinct from the hard logical constraints this paper's rule-based system uses.（中文:生长曲线z分数是指某项测量值与特定年龄和性别的参照中位数相差的标准差数,由CDC或WHO等图表定义;这是一种统计偏差度量,与本文的基于规则系统所使用的硬性逻辑约束不同。）

Methods/tools to lift

  • Design-science framework — a four-step research methodology (design, demonstrate, test, evaluate) for building and validating a practical artifact rather than just shipping code.
  • Logic templates — a small set of generalized rule patterns (28, in this paper) that many specific rule instances can be expressed against, instead of writing each rule from scratch.
  • Knowledge table — a parameter table that maps each specific rule instance back to its logic template, which is the mechanism that keeps a very large rule set governable and maintainable.
  • Clinician/operational-leader review loop — surfacing flagged discrepancies to domain leaders and capturing whether they judge each flag as a real problem plus what corrective action they'd take.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Serves Liz's CFFPR data-quality assignment, method only — no claim is made here about CFFPR's actual rules, flag rates, or cleaning logic.

Buildable artifact: Height/Weight Plausibility Rule-Bank — a Python script that takes a CSV of patient-id, visit-date, height, and weight, runs the records through roughly ten to fifteen hard-coded plausibility templates (unit-mismatch, biologically-impossible single-visit jump, impossible BMI, decimal-shift), using one small lookup table for per-template parameters, and outputs a flagged-candidate list naming which template fired and why. Input: flat visit-level anthropometric CSV. Difficulty: S.

What to verify first

  • Whether any curated, maintained set of plausibility templates for anthropometrics already exists to borrow from — the authors flag "lack of curated and maintained knowledge sources" as an open, unsolved challenge, not something this paper solved.
  • Whether a template-plus-knowledge-table structure is proportionate for a rule bank of ten to fifteen rules, given this approach was validated at a 63,397-rule scale.
  • What organizational capacity would exist to act on flagged records, since the paper separately names "lack of organizational resources to support leaders" as a real bottleneck distinct from whether the rules work.
  • What review cadence a rule bank like this would need, since the paper does not report how often templates or thresholds get revisited.

Pipeline stage

(a) Candidate generation — this paper's transferable contribution is a method for producing and managing a flagged-candidate list at scale, not a method for adjudicating candidates or setting a human-review budget. There is a loose, secondary echo of (c) human review budget in the clinician-feedback loop described, since that loop effectively calibrates what gets acted on, but (a) is the primary mapping.

Error types covered / not covered

Covered (per the general plausibility-rule concept discussed, not per any rule text given in the abstract, since the abstract does not publish individual rules): unit mismatches, cross-field inconsistency, biologically-impossible single-value or single-jump constraints, reference-list/range violations.

Not stated as covered or not covered by this abstract, and therefore open: digit transposition, decimal-shift detection as a named category, carry-forward-from-prior-visit detection, and discrimination of a real clinical event (e.g., a genuine rapid weight change) from a data error. The abstract gives us the scale and validation outcome of the rule system, not an enumerated taxonomy of which specific error types its 63,397 rules targeted.
