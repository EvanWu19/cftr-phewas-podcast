Ep3 (Track C, #1) — Alternatives to BMI Z-Scores for Very High BMI

Freedman DS, Davies AJG, Kompaniyets L, et al. 2021. The Journal of Pediatrics, 235, 156-162. Abstract-only (no PMCID indexed).

Blurb

Standard CDC BMI z-scores are built on the LMS method, which behaves badly once a child's BMI sits far out in the tail beyond roughly the 97th percentile — z-scores can compress or move non-sensibly with real BMI changes. This paper compares five alternatives (modified BMIz, percent of the CDC 95th percentile, extended BMIz, percent of median, dispersion-adjusted percent of median) using longitudinal tracking (intraclass correlation) in 1.17 million PEDSnet children. All alternatives outperformed standard BMIz for children with obesity, but none won everywhere; extended BMIz and extended percentiles are recommended because they match the standard charts below the 95th percentile and only diverge in the extreme tail. For CFFPR, this is a direct candidate-generation concern: any spline- or mixed-effects flagging rule scoring deviation via ordinary BMIz inherits this exact ceiling artifact for the highest-BMI patients.

Plain-English glossary

  • BMIz (BMI z-score) — how many standard deviations a child's BMI sits from the age/sex-specific median, per the CDC LMS reference.（中文:BMI z分数,表示儿童BMI偏离CDC参考曲线中该年龄性别中位数的标准差数量。）
  • Extended BMIz / extended percentile — a modification that matches standard CDC BMIz/percentiles below the 95th percentile but uses a different formula above it to avoid the ceiling artifact.（中文:扩展BMIz/扩展百分位数,在第95百分位以下与标准CDC指标一致,但在其上采用不同公式以避免天花板伪影问题。）
  • ICC (intraclass correlation coefficient) — a statistic measuring how much of a metric's total variance is between-child versus within-child, used here to compare longitudinal tracking quality across metrics.（中文:组内相关系数,用于衡量一个指标的总方差中个体间差异与个体内部差异的比例,本文用它比较不同指标的纵向追踪质量。）
  • LMS method — the statistical method (Lambda-Mu-Sigma: skewness, median, spread) CDC and WHO use to build growth-reference charts.（中文:LMS方法(偏度-中位数-离散度法),CDC和WHO用于构建生长参考曲线图的统计方法。）
  • PEDSnet — a multi-institution U.S. pediatric research network pooling electronic health record data across children's hospitals.（中文:PEDSnet,一个汇集多家儿童医院电子健康记录数据的美国多机构儿科研究网络。）

Methods / tools to lift

  • Extended BMIz / extended BMI percentile — CDC-published formulas that match standard charts below the 95th percentile, diverging only in the extreme tail.
  • Multi-metric longitudinal tracking comparison via ICC as a screening step before committing to a single deviation metric.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

cftr-registry note (method only): ordinary BMIz is a poor choice for any candidate-generation rule scoring deviation in very-high-BMI patients; extended BMIz is a drop-in, CDC-published replacement.

Buildable artifact: bmiz-ceiling-swap-test (S) — recompute standard BMIz and extended BMIz on longitudinal, public/synthetic pediatric BMI records above the 97th percentile, and flag cases where extended BMIz moves substantially while standard BMIz stays flat, as a short reviewable divergence list.

What to verify first

  • Whether extended BMIz's tracking advantage holds up against an actual adiposity ground truth (e.g., DXA fat mass), not just its own longitudinal self-consistency.
  • Exact ICC values and effect sizes (not given in the abstract) — full text needed to judge whether differences are marginal or substantial.
  • How PEDSnet's own pre-cleaning of implausible values may have shaped which extreme-BMI children were available for comparison.
  • That extended BMIz is still CDC-reference-anchored, so it inherits that reference's own cohort-composition and dating assumptions.

Pipeline stage: (a) candidate generation — primary. Changes which deviation metric a flagging rule should be built on for high-BMI patients; does not itself constitute an adjudication, review-budget, or evaluation method.

Error types covered / not covered: Covered — z-score ceiling/compression artifacts specifically at very high BMI. Not covered — unit conversion errors, digit transposition, decimal shift, carry-forward/duplicate values, or real-clinical-event confusion; this paper is about metric choice, not error detection itself.
