Ep4 (Track C, #2) — Does the z-Score Calculation Method Itself Introduce Error?

Martinez-Millana et al. (2018, PLoS ONE) ask a more fundamental question than most Track C papers: is the z-score calculation that every population-cutoff flagging rule depends on actually accurate? They trained a Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) model on CDC's own published z-score reference tables (8,694/7,812 data points) and compared it against WHO and CDC-LMS (Cole's 1990 method) across three stages: standard CDC cutoff points (GPR/CDC-LMS beat WHO, p<0.001, with CDC-LMS residual outliers concentrated under 60 months), a 3,000-child simulation (11.1% of GPR-vs-CDC-LMS comparisons showed >30% absolute error, concentrated in the ordinary z ∈ [-1,1] range), and — notably — 731 real weight/height measurements from 212 children with cystic fibrosis in the European MyCyFAPP observational study (methods diverged by up to 1.42 SD for weight-for-age, statistically independent of the measurement value, p<0.001). The counterintuitive finding: different z-score calculation methods disagree most in the ordinary central range every flagging rule treats as "clearly fine," not at the extreme tails where flagging actually happens. The buildable artifact: implement a GPR z-score calculator on the public CDC reference tables and compare it against CDC-LMS to check where and how much the two diverge.

Plain-English glossary

  • BIV threshold / cutoff — the z-score value beyond which a measurement is flagged as biologically implausible. (中文：不合理值阈值，超过该z分数值的测量结果会被标记为生物学上不合理。)
  • GPR (Gaussian Process Regression) — a regression method that models observed data as coming from a Gaussian joint distribution and produces a full posterior density over the target rather than one fitted curve, defined by a covariance kernel rather than intercepts/coefficients. (中文：高斯过程回归，一种把观测数据视为来自高斯联合分布、并给出目标值完整后验密度而非单一拟合曲线的回归方法，由协方差核函数而非截距/系数定义。)
  • Kernel (in GPR) — the function defining the covariance structure of a Gaussian Process; the paper tested Squared Exponential, Exponential, Matern 5/2, and Rational Quadratic kernels. (中文：核函数，定义高斯过程协方差结构的函数；本文测试了平方指数核、指数核、Matern 5/2核和有理二次核。)
  • LMS method (Cole, 1990) — the statistical method behind CDC growth charts: three age-and-sex-specific parameters (L = skewness, M = median, S = coefficient of variation) that transform a raw measurement into a z-score even for non-normal distributions. (中文：LMS方法，CDC生长图表背后的统计方法：三个按年龄和性别划分的参数（L=偏度，M=中位数，S=变异系数），用于把原始测量值转换为z分数，即使分布并非正态也适用。)
  • z-score (for anthropometrics) — how many standard deviations a child's measurement sits from the expected value for their age and sex, per a given reference system (WHO or CDC). (中文：z分数，指一个孩子的测量值相对于按某个参照体系——WHO或CDC——给出的同龄同性别预期值，偏离了多少个标准差。)

JORDAN Let's start with what's actually being calculated. 我们先从到底在计算什么开始说起。

ALEX A z-score for an anthropometric measurement — weight-for-age, height-for-age, BMI-for-age — expresses how many standard deviations a child's value sits from the expected value for their age and sex. 一个人体测量指标的z分数——年龄别体重（weight-for-age）、年龄别身高（height-for-age）、年龄别BMI（BMI-for-age）——表示一个孩子的数值相对于同龄同性别预期值，偏离了多少个标准差。 The two dominant reference systems are WHO's growth standards and the CDC's growth charts, and the CDC specifically uses something called the LMS method, developed by Cole in 1990: L, M, and S are three age-and-sex-specific parameters — a skewness parameter, the median, and a coefficient of variation — that together let you transform a raw measurement into a z-score even though the underlying distribution of, say, weight at a given age isn't perfectly normal. 两大主流参照体系是WHO的生长标准和CDC的生长图表，CDC具体使用的是Cole在1990年提出的LMS方法：L、M、S是三个按年龄和性别划分的参数——一个偏度参数、中位数，以及一个变异系数——它们共同的作用是把一个原始测量值转换成z分数，即便某个年龄段体重之类指标的实际分布并不完全服从正态分布。 The problem the authors identify is precision at certain percentiles — the paper opens by noting both WHO and CDC-LMS "can be imprecise in some percentiles," which is the gap they're trying to close. 作者们指出的问题在于某些百分位处的精确度——论文开篇就指出WHO和CDC-LMS方法"在某些百分位上可能不够精确"，而这正是他们想要弥合的差距。

JORDAN So what's their alternative? 那他们的替代方案是什么？

ALEX Gaussian Process Regression, GPR. 高斯过程回归（Gaussian Process Regression，GPR）。 Rather than smoothing the data and fitting three age-varying parameters the way LMS does, GPR treats the observed z-score data as coming from a Gaussian joint distribution and produces a full posterior density over the target value given the predictors — gender, age in months, and the raw measurement — rather than one fitted curve. 它不像LMS方法那样对数据做平滑处理、拟合三个随年龄变化的参数，而是把观测到的z分数数据看作来自一个高斯联合分布，并根据预测变量——性别、以月为单位的年龄、以及原始测量值——生成目标值的完整后验密度，而不是一条拟合曲线。 Practically, GPR is defined by a covariance matrix built from a kernel function rather than intercepts and coefficients, and the authors tested several kernel choices — Squared Exponential, Exponential, Matern 5/2, and Rational Quadratic — before settling on the Squared Exponential kernel as their primary model. 实际操作中，GPR由一个基于核函数（kernel function）构建的协方差矩阵定义，而不是截距和系数，作者们测试了几种核函数选择——平方指数核（Squared Exponential）、指数核（Exponential）、Matern 5/2核和有理二次核（Rational Quadratic）——最终选定平方指数核作为主要模型。 Training data was CDC's own published z-score reference tables — 8,694 data points for weight-for-age and height-for-age, 7,812 for BMI-for-age, drawn from 3,686 subjects — so the model is essentially learning to reproduce and smooth the CDC's own ground truth more precisely than the CDC's own three-parameter LMS fit does. 训练数据就是CDC自己发布的z分数参照表——年龄别体重和年龄别身高有8694个数据点，年龄别BMI有7812个数据点，来自3686名受试者——所以这个模型本质上是在学习如何比CDC自己的三参数LMS拟合更精确地复现和平滑CDC自己的基准真值。

JORDAN How did they actually test whether GPR does better? 他们具体是怎么测试GPR效果更好的？

JORDAN Why would GPR do better than a method the CDC itself designed and validated? 为什么GPR会比CDC自己设计和验证过的方法表现更好？

ALEX The core difference is flexibility versus a fixed functional form. 核心区别在于灵活性与固定函数形式之间的差异。 LMS smooths the data first and then commits to exactly three age-varying parameters — skewness, median, spread — as the only levers available to describe the entire distribution at every age; if the true distribution's shape does something those three parameters can't capture well at a given age, the fit degrades locally without a way to adapt beyond that parameterization.
