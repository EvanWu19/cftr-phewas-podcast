Blurb

Lin and colleagues (PLoS Genetics, 2026) run genome-wide association studies of adult height (120,301 Han Taiwanese) and familial short stature (2,050 cases / 27,966 controls), then sweep the resulting genetic signal across the disease catalogs of five East Asian biobanks. The value for our project is not the trait — it's the architecture: build a single genetic anchor (a polygenic risk score), run a phenome-wide association study to find which diseases it predicts, use Mendelian randomization to separate genuine causal links from passengers, and use survival models to time the onset. Their clean hit is that taller stature independently raises atrial fibrillation risk (odds ratio ~1.12–1.15 across cohorts, replicated; earlier age-of-onset by Cox model), while an apparent height–endometriosis link dissolves once menarche timing and body weight are accounted for. The proposed direction: mirror this four-move pipeline using CFTR as the anchor instead of a height score. The open question is whether a ~5,000-patient CF cohort has the power and the replication partner that a five-biobank height study enjoyed.

Plain-English glossary

  • Atrial fibrillation (AF): an irregular, often rapid heartbeat.
  • Biobank: a large research collection of people's DNA linked to their health records.
  • Cox proportional hazards model: a statistic for time-to-event data — asks not just whether an event happens but when.
  • Colocalization: a Bayesian test of whether a signal in one cohort is driven by the same causal variant as a signal in another.
  • Endometriosis: a disorder where uterine-like tissue grows outside the uterus.
  • Familial short stature (FSS): a pediatric diagnosis of genuine shortness (below 3rd percentile) with a short parent but otherwise normal development.
  • GWAS (genome-wide association study): fixes a trait and scans the whole genome for associated variants.
  • Hazard ratio: in survival analysis, the relative rate of an event per unit of the predictor.
  • Locus / loci: a genomic region containing an association signal.
  • MAF (minor allele frequency): how common the rarer version of a variant is.
  • Mendelian randomization (MR): uses genes as natural stand-ins for a trait to test whether the trait causes a disease.
  • Mixed linear model: a statistical method that accounts for relatedness between participants to avoid false signals.
  • Odds ratio: how many times the odds of a disease change per unit of the predictor.
  • PheWAS (phenome-wide association study): fixes the genetics and sweeps across hundreds of traits/diagnoses.
  • Phenome: the whole catalog of a person's traits and diagnoses.
  • Polygenic risk score (PRS): a single number summing the small effects of thousands of variants for one trait.
  • Principal components: mathematical summaries of ancestry, included as covariates so ancestry doesn't masquerade as signal.
  • REGENIE: software for running association scans efficiently at biobank scale.
  • SNP: a single-letter DNA difference — the most common kind of variant.

Methods to lift

  • PRS-CS — polygenic-score construction method used here with a 1000 Genomes East Asian linkage-disequilibrium reference panel.
  • LD score regression — estimates genetic correlation between two traits from summary statistics.
  • coloc (R package) — Bayesian colocalization to test shared vs distinct causal variants across cohorts.
  • GCTA-COJO — conditional/joint analysis to separate independent signals and classify novel vs known loci.
  • Mendelian randomization (univariable + multivariable, MR-weighted-median / MVMR-Lasso) — tests causation and whether an effect is mediated by other traits.
  • Cox proportional hazards + Kaplan–Meier — time-to-event modeling for age-of-onset and cumulative incidence.
