How did this episode land for you?

Blurb

A 2016 Statistical Methods in Medical Research review by Eleanor Pullenayegum and Lily Lim tackles a problem upstream of any outlier-flagging method: in clinic-based longitudinal data, each patient generates their own irregular "visit process," and when the timing of visits is itself statistically linked to the underlying disease course (patients visiting more when unwell), any trajectory fit to that data can be biased — a timing-specific cousin of the missing-data "ignorability" assumptions. The review catalogs existing correction methods (joint models, inverse-intensity weighting) and the specific assumption each depends on, concluding that no single method handles every plausible visit scenario, that some common real-world visit patterns aren't handled well by any of them, and recommending study-design mitigations rather than relying purely on after-the-fact statistical fixes. This is an abstract-only read (no PMCID, closed access). It doesn't change the CFFPR pipeline directly on any of the four sharper fronts, but it supplies a vocabulary and warning worth borrowing: any spline or mixed-effects candidate generator implicitly assumes its visit process is ignorable. The buildable artifact is a lightweight Visit-Process Diagnostic to check that assumption before trusting a trajectory baseline.

Plain-English Glossary

  • Ignorable (visit process) — A visit process is ignorable if, once you condition on what's already in the model, the timing of visits carries no extra information about the outcome; the timing-specific analog of "missing at random."（中文:可忽略(ignorable),指在对模型中已有信息做条件化之后,就诊时间点本身不再携带关于结局的额外信息;是"随机缺失"假设在时间点维度上的对应概念。）
  • Informative visit timing — When the timing of a patient's visits is statistically associated with their underlying disease course (e.g., visiting more often when unwell), biasing any trajectory estimated from the resulting data.（中文:信息性就诊时间,指患者就诊的时间点在统计上与其潜在病程相关联(例如身体不适时更频繁就诊),会使基于这类数据估计出的任何轨迹产生偏差。）
  • Inverse-intensity weighting — A correction method, analogous to inverse-probability weighting, that upweights observations from patients who visit less often than their covariates would predict.（中文:逆强度加权,一种类似于逆概率加权的校正方法,对就诊频率低于其协变量所预测水平的患者的观测值赋予更高权重。）
  • Joint model (of visit intensity and outcome) — A model that assumes the visit-timing process and the outcome process share an underlying random effect, so both are modeled together rather than treating visit timing as a separate nuisance.（中文:联合模型,假设就诊时间过程与结局过程共享某个底层随机效应,因此将二者一起建模,而不是把就诊时间当作单独需要处理的干扰因素。）
  • Kahn framework (temporal dimension) — A registry/EHR data-quality framework; its "temporal" dimension concerns exactly this kind of timing-related data-quality concern.（中文:Kahn数据质量框架,一个注册库/电子健康记录数据质量框架;其"时间性"(temporal)维度关注的正是这类与时间点相关的数据质量问题。）
  • Retrospective chart review — A study design that uses existing clinical records collected during routine care, rather than data collected on a fixed protocol-driven schedule.（中文:回顾性病历审查,一种利用常规诊疗过程中已产生的临床记录(而非按固定方案时间表专门采集的数据)开展的研究设计。）
  • Visit process — The sequence of dates on which a given patient is observed/measured in a clinic-based longitudinal dataset, as opposed to a fixed trial-style visit schedule.（中文:就诊过程,指在基于门诊的纵向数据集中,某一患者被观察/测量的一系列日期,与临床试验那种固定的就诊时间表不同。）

Full Citation

Pullenayegum EM, Lim LS. Longitudinal data subject to irregular observation: A review of methods with a focus on visit processes, assumptions, and study design. Statistical Methods in Medical Research. 2016. PMID: 24855119 | DOI: 10.1177/0962280214536537

Methods / Tools to Lift

  • Visit-process taxonomy — a structured way to categorize how visit timing might depend on past, current-observed, or current-unobserved outcome values, and whether visits are protocol- or patient-driven.
  • Joint modeling of visit intensity + outcome — shared-random-effect approach for when timing and outcome are believed to share an underlying driver.
  • Inverse-intensity weighting — reweighting observations to correct for visit-frequency imbalance, the timing analog of inverse-probability weighting.

Where It Lands + Buildable Artifact

Pipeline stage: primarily (e) nothing directly, but it supplies vocabulary/framework worth borrowing — the warning that any spline/mixed-effects candidate generator assumes its visit process is ignorable; secondary touch on (d) evaluation, since visit timing correlating with error likelihood (not just outcome) could confound a flagged-only gold-standard sample. Buildable artifact: a Visit-Process Diagnostic — before fitting any trajectory candidate generator on registry encounter data, check whether visit-gap length correlates with the extremity of prior recorded residuals, as a rough signal for whether the ignorability assumption looks safe for that dataset. Input: longitudinal encounter table (patient ID, visit date, height/weight). Difficulty: S-M.

What to Verify First

  • Whether this taxonomy, built mostly around general retrospective chart-review settings, transfers to a protocol-influenced registry where visits are already expected at roughly regular intervals.
  • Whether any catalogued correction method (joint models, inverse-intensity weighting) has actually been tested on pediatric growth/anthropometric trajectories specifically, versus the general biomarker outcomes this literature more typically covers.
  • That this is a review, not something validated directly on registry data — none of its recommended design mitigations should be assumed already feasible or in place for how registry visits are scheduled and recorded.
  • That this paper addresses bias in trajectory estimation from correctly recorded values at informatively-timed visits only — it says nothing about value-level data-entry errors, which remain a separate, coexisting hazard.

Pipeline Stage

(e) Vocabulary/framework only, primary — no direct candidate-generation, adjudication, or review-budget mechanism proposed. (d) Evaluation, secondary — a conceptual caution about confounding in flagged-only validation samples when visit timing correlates with error likelihood.

Error Types Covered / Not Covered

Covered: none directly — this paper addresses trajectory-estimation bias from informative visit timing, not value-level errors.

Not covered: unit conversion, digit transposition, decimal shift, carry-forward, and real-clinical-event confusion are all out of scope; the paper explicitly assumes recorded values are accurate and only addresses when they were observed.

JORDAN Let's set up the vocabulary first. What do you mean by a patient's "visit process"? 我们先把术语讲清楚。你说的患者"就诊过程"(visit process)具体是什么意思?

ALEX In a clinic-based registry like a CF patient registry, nobody follows a fixed trial-style schedule where every patient is measured at exactly month 0, 6, 12, and so on. 在像CF患者注册库这样基于门诊的注册系统里,没有人会遵循像临床试验那样固定的时间表,让每个患者都恰好在第0、6、12个月等时间点接受测量。

ALEX Instead, each patient generates their own sequence of visit dates, driven by some mix of clinical protocol, how they're feeling, and scheduling logistics — and that sequence of dates is what this literature calls a visit process. 相反,每个患者都会生成自己独特的一系列就诊日期序列,这背后是临床规程、患者自身状态和排班实际情况等多种因素共同作用的结果——而这一系列就诊日期,这篇文献就称之为就诊过程(visit process)。

JORDAN And "informative" visit timing means what exactly? 那"信息性"(informative)就诊时间具体是什么意思?

ALEX It means the timing of visits is statistically linked to the underlying disease course itself — the classic example is a patient who shows up more often precisely when they're unwell. 它指的是,就诊的时间点本身,在统计上与潜在的病程状态相关联——最典型的例子就是,患者恰恰在身体不适的时候,会更频繁地前来就诊。

ALEX If that's happening and you don't account for it, your dataset ends up overrepresenting "sick" periods relative to "well" periods for that patient, which biases any trajectory you fit, growth curve or otherwise. 如果确实存在这种情况,而你又没有加以考虑,那么你的数据集就会相对于"健康"时期,过度代表该患者的"生病"时期,这会使你拟合出的任何轨迹——无论是生长曲线还是别的——产生偏差。

JORDAN This sounds a lot like a timing-specific cousin of the missing-data assumptions we already know — ignorable versus non-ignorable missingness, just applied to when you observe rather than whether you observe. 这听起来很像我们已经熟悉的缺失数据假设(missing-data assumptions)里,专门针对时间点的一个"堂兄弟"概念——可忽略(ignorable)和非可忽略缺失,只不过这里针对的是"什么时候观察到",而不是"是否观察到"。

ALEX That's exactly the right analogy, and it's useful to think of it that way — a visit process is "ignorable" if, once you condition on what's already in your model, the timing carries no extra information about the outcome, and "informative" or non-ignorable if it does. 这个类比完全正确,而且这样想很有帮助——如果一旦你在模型里对已有的信息做了条件化,就诊时间本身不再携带关于结局的额外信息,那这个就诊过程就是"可忽略"(ignorable)的;如果它确实还携带额外信息,那就是"信息性"或非可忽略的。

ALEX It also connects directly to something we've been building in the QC methods catalog — the temporal dimension in the Kahn data-quality framework is exactly this concern, just framed as a data-quality check rather than a statistical-bias problem. 这也直接关联到我们在QC方法目录里一直在积累的内容——Kahn数据质量框架(Kahn framework)里的"时间性"(temporal)维度,关注的正是这同一个问题,只是它是从数据质量检查的角度来表述,而不是作为一个统计偏差问题。

JORDAN So what was the actual gap this review is filling — presumably statisticians already knew informative visit timing was a thing people should worry about? 那这篇综述实际填补的空白是什么——我猜统计学家早就知道,信息性就诊时间是一个应该担心的问题吧?

ALEX They did, and there were already several analytic approaches on the table by 2016 — but each one bakes in a different, specific assumption about exactly how visit timing depends on the outcome process, and those assumptions hadn't been systematically lined up against the kinds of visit scenarios you actually encounter in real clinic-based data. 确实如此,到2016年的时候,市面上已经存在好几种分析方法——但每一种方法都内置了一个不同的、具体的假设,关于就诊时间究竟如何依赖于结局过程,而这些假设此前并没有被系统地与你在真实门诊数据中实际会遇到的各种就诊场景一一对照检验过。

ALEX For example, one class of methods, joint models, assumes the visit-intensity process and the outcome process share some underlying random effect, so you model them together instead of treating visit timing as a nuisance to correct for separately. 举例来说,一类方法是联合模型(joint model),它假设就诊强度过程和结局过程背后共享某个随机效应,所以你是把它们一起建模,而不是把就诊时间单独当作一个需要校正掉的干扰因素来处理。

ALEX Before fitting any spline or mixed-effects growth-trajectory candidate generator on registry-style encounter data, run a simple check for whether visit timing correlates with the extremity of a patient's prior recorded values — for instance, whether shorter gaps between visits follow more extreme-residual encounters — as a rough signal for whether the ignorability assumption looks safe or risky for that dataset. 在对注册库风格的就诊记录数据拟合任何样条或混合效应生长轨迹候选生成器之前,先做一个简单的检查:看看就诊时间间隔,是否与患者先前记录数值的极端程度相关——比如,在残差(residual)较极端的就诊之后,下一次就诊的间隔是否更短——把这个作为一个粗略的信号,判断对这个数据集而言,可忽略性假设看起来是安全还是存在风险。
