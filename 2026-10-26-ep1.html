Ep1 (Track A — variant interpretation): A field guide to variant-pathogenicity predictors, and why no single score should be trusted alone

Pakpahan, Sihombing and colleagues (2026, GigaScience) publish a narrative, method-focused review tracing the evolution of variant impact predictors (VIPs) through five overlapping phases: rule-based heuristics (SIFT, PolyPhen), statistical/probabilistic models (FATHMM, GERP++), classical machine learning and ensembles (CADD, REVEL), deep learning (SpliceAI, DeepSEA), and transformer architectures split into protein language models (ESM-1b, AlphaMissense) and regulatory genome transformers (Enformer, AlphaGenome). Reported benchmark performance climbs from ROC-AUC ~0.80–0.86 for the earliest tools to ~0.90–0.97 for the newest transformer-based ones, led by AlphaMissense at ~0.94 — but the authors themselves caution that these figures come from heterogeneous, non-comparable evaluation settings. The paper's real contribution for this podcast isn't a new tool; it's the explicit recommendation to compare several structurally distinct predictors rather than trust one score in isolation, and to treat disagreement between them as the signal worth a human reviewer's time. Buildable artifact: a CFTR Multi-VIP Concordance Dashboard.

Plain-English glossary

  • ACMG/AMP guidelines — the joint American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics / Association for Molecular Pathology framework that combines weighted evidence codes into a final pathogenic/benign classification.（中文：美国医学遗传学与基因组学学会与分子病理学协会联合制定的变异分类框架，通过加权证据代码得出最终的致病性判定。）
  • ClinVar — a public archive of reported relationships between genetic variants and phenotypes, submitted directly by clinicians and laboratories.（中文：一个公开的变异-表型关系档案库，由临床医生和实验室直接提交数据。）
  • gnomAD (Genome Aggregation Database) — a large population-scale catalog of allele frequencies used to judge how rare or common a variant is.（中文：一个大规模人群等位基因频率数据库，用于判断某个变异在人群中有多罕见或多常见。）
  • pLM (protein language model) — a transformer model pre-trained on huge numbers of protein sequences with no labels, learning which amino acid substitutions are evolutionarily tolerated.（中文：蛋白质语言模型，一种在海量无标签蛋白质序列上预训练的transformer模型，学习哪些氨基酸替换在进化上是可被容忍的。）
  • ROC-AUC — area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, a threshold-independent measure of how well a predictor separates pathogenic from benign variants.（中文：受试者工作特征曲线下面积，一种不依赖具体阈值、衡量预测工具区分致病与良性变异能力的综合指标。）
  • VUS (variant of uncertain significance) — a variant for which there isn't yet enough evidence to classify as either pathogenic or benign.（中文：意义不明变异，指目前尚无充分证据判定为致病或良性的基因变异。）
  • XAI (explainable AI) — methods that attach a transparent, biologically meaningful rationale to a model's prediction rather than a bare score.（中文：可解释人工智能，指为模型预测结果提供透明、具有生物学意义的依据，而非仅给出一个分数的方法。）

Full citation

Methods / tools to lift

  • AlphaMissense — fuses AlphaFold structural predictions with protein-language-model pattern recognition for missense pathogenicity scoring.
  • ESM-1b — a protein language model usable standalone for variant-effect scoring via masked-residue prediction.
  • InterVar — a rule-based implementation of ACMG/AMP guidelines for reproducible, guideline-concordant variant classification.
  • Ensembl VEP / ANNOVAR — integrative annotation pipelines that bundle multiple predictors and databases into one workflow.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Serves Karen / CFTR variant interpretation and VUS pathogenicity prediction. The transferable idea is the review's closing recommendation: don't trust a single predictor's score in isolation — compare several methodologically distinct predictors and treat disagreement between them as the signal worth investigating. Buildable artifact: a CFTR Multi-VIP Concordance Dashboard. MVP: for every current CFTR VUS, pull scores from several structurally distinct predictors (e.g. AlphaMissense, REVEL, CADD, ESM-1b), normalize them to a comparable scale, and surface the variants with the sharpest cross-predictor disagreement as the priority list for manual review, rather than ranking everything by one tool's score. Difficulty: small-to-medium.

What to verify first

  • Whether the ancestry-bias problem this paper flags for gnomAD/ClinVar specifically distorts any of CFTR's existing VUS calls — a CFTR-specific question this review doesn't answer.
  • Whether any of the named predictors are already part of CFTR2's own curation pipeline — to confirm directly with Karen rather than assume.
  • Whether the headline AUC figures (0.80s–0.97) are comparable across tools at all, given the paper's own admission of heterogeneous evaluation settings — any dashboard should recalibrate or normalize scores on a shared evaluation set rather than use published numbers at face value.
  • Whether this review's optimism about multi-omics fusion and few-shot adaptation for rare variants has been demonstrated for a gene like CFTR specifically, or remains a field-wide aspiration.
