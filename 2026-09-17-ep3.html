Blurb. Carsley et al. used EMRALD, a network of Ontario, Canada family practices, to study how complete and accurate primary-care EMR height/weight data actually are for 54,964 children under 19. Using WHO Growth Standards to compute BMI z-scores, they found 93% of children had at least one complete growth-measurement set somewhere in their record, but only 66.2% of all visits had complete BMI z-score data -- split sharply by visit type: 89.9% of well-child visits vs. 33.9% of sick visits, with missing height (not weight) the main driver of incompleteness. Only 2.7% of BMI z-score data were excluded as implausible. The headline lesson is the size gap between the two defect categories: roughly a third of visits missing usable data, versus under 3% flagged as biologically implausible once data exists. This doesn't propose or test any detection method, so it doesn't change a candidate generator, adjudication step, or review budget directly -- its value is as an external base-rate calibration point (pipeline stage e: vocabulary/framework) for scoping how much effort should go toward missingness versus implausibility. The buildable artifact is a small completeness-rate calculator stratifying visit-level data-availability by visit type or other context variables on a public/synthetic longitudinal anthropometric dataset.
