How did this episode land for you?

Combining MAVEs and computational predictors improves variant classification across ancestries in hereditary cancer genes

Bora, Badonyi, Pathak, and colleagues — including Singapore's SG10K_Health Consortium — benchmark multiplexed assays of variant effect (MAVEs) against three computational variant-effect predictors (GEMME, EVE, and CPT-1) across five hereditary cancer genes with existing functional-assay data: BRCA1, HRAS, MSH2, PTEN, and TP53. Neither evidence source wins outright — MAVEs beat all three predictors in BRCA1 and HRAS, predictors beat the MAVE in PTEN, and it's close in MSH2 and TP53 — but MAVEs are consistently more decisive, leaving only 5.2% of variants indeterminate versus 11–20% for the predictors. The paper's real test is applying a calibrated combination of MAVE and CPT-1 evidence to 483 real ClinVar variants of uncertain significance found in two ancestrally underrepresented cohorts (SG10K_Health in Singapore and the Mexico City Prospective Study), reclassifying 91.5% as benign or pathogenic. For Karen's CFTR variant-interpretation work, the transferable piece isn't a specific score — it's the three-bucket structure (concordant-benign / concordant-pathogenic / flagged-discordant) the paper uses instead of forcing every variant into one merged number. The buildable artifact is a CFTR Dual-Evidence Discordance Flagger that applies this structure to CFTR VUS using whatever computational predictors and, if available, functional-assay data already exist for the gene.

Plain-English glossary

  • MAVE (multiplexed assay of variant effect) — A functional assay that tests nearly every possible amino-acid change in a gene at once, in a dish, using a biological readout (survival, growth, splicing, binding) as a proxy for whether each variant breaks the protein.（中文：一种功能测定方法，能在培养皿里同时检测一个基因几乎所有可能的氨基酸变化，用细胞存活、生长、剪接或结合等读数来判断每个变异是否破坏蛋白质功能。）
  • VEP (variant effect predictor) — A computational model that scores a variant's likely effect from sequence, evolutionary conservation, or structure, without any lab experiment.（中文：一种仅依据序列、进化保守性或结构信息，从计算角度为变异打分的工具，不涉及任何实验室实验。）
  • VUS (variant of uncertain significance) — The ACMG/AMP classification bucket for a variant that is neither clearly pathogenic nor clearly benign given current evidence.（中文：ACMG/AMP分类体系中，用来收纳那些目前证据既不足以判定为致病、也不足以判定为良性的变异的类别。）
  • ACMG/AMP evidence codes — The American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics / Association for Molecular Pathology's shared system of evidence codes (e.g., PS3 for strong functional evidence, PP3 for supporting computational evidence) that combine into a final variant classification.（中文：美国医学遗传学与基因组学学会与分子病理学会联合制定的证据编码体系，例如PS3代表强功能证据、PP3代表支持性计算证据，最终合并得出变异分类结论。）
  • ClinVar — A public database of variant-to-clinical-significance interpretations submitted by laboratories and clinics.（中文：一个公开数据库，收录实验室和临床机构提交的变异与临床意义的对应关系。）
  • gnomAD — A large population database of genomic variant frequencies, used to estimate how common a variant is in different ancestry groups.（中文：一个大规模人群基因组变异频率数据库，用于估计某个变异在不同族裔群体中的常见程度。）
  • GEMME — A computational variant-effect predictor that scores variants from evolutionary sequence patterns.（中文：一种通过进化序列模式为变异打分的计算预测工具。）
  • EVE — A variational autoencoder-based variant-effect predictor trained on multiple-sequence alignments.（中文：一种基于多序列比对训练的变分自编码器变异效应预测工具。）
  • CPT-1 — A newer variant-effect predictor that combines protein-language-model embeddings with structural features; itself partly trained on functional-assay (MAVE) data from genes other than the five studied here.（中文：一种较新的变异效应预测工具，融合蛋白质语言模型的嵌入表示与结构特征，本身也部分使用过其他基因的功能测定（MAVE）数据训练。）
  • Bayesian point system (Tavtigian 2020) — The statistical framework that converts likelihood ratios from each evidence type into ACMG/AMP points, which sum toward a final classification.（中文：一套统计框架，把每种证据类型的似然比换算成ACMG/AMP证据分，加总后得出最终分类结论。）
  • CanVIG-UK — The UK Cancer Variant Interpretation Group's clinical interpretation rules for combining evidence in cancer-predisposition gene variant classification.（中文：英国癌症易感基因变异解读工作组制定的临床判读规则，用于综合各类证据对癌症易感基因变异进行分类。）
  • ΔΔG (protein folding stability change) — The predicted change in a protein's folding stability caused by a mutation, estimated here with the FoldX tool.（中文：某个突变导致蛋白质折叠稳定性发生的预测变化量，本文用FoldX工具估算。）

Methods / tools to lift

  • acmgscaler (R package) — calibrates functional/computational evidence into ACMG/AMP points via kernel density estimation of likelihood ratios.
  • GEMME, EVE, CPT-1 — three complementary computational variant-effect predictors with different architectures (evolutionary-pattern, VAE, protein-language-model+structure).
  • FoldX 5.0 — structural ΔΔG estimation from a repaired PDB model.
  • Gene-specific MAVE thresholding — using each gene's own functional-assay fitness scores rather than pooling across genes on one universal scale.
  • Tavtigian 2020 Bayesian point framework + CanVIG-UK rules — combining independently-calibrated evidence streams into a single clinical call.

Where it lands + buildable artifact

Lands on Karen / CFTR variant interpretation (method only). Buildable artifact: CFTR Dual-Evidence Discordance Flagger — take a CFTR VUS list, pair it with existing calibrated computational scores (REVEL/CADD/AlphaMissense/whatever's in the current in silico panel), and, if a comparable CFTR functional-assay or MAVE dataset exists, calibrate that too using this paper's kernel-density approach. Sort every VUS into three buckets — concordant-benign, concordant-pathogenic, or flagged-discordant — rather than merging into one score. Input: a CFTR VUS list + calibrated functional and computational scores. Difficulty: M if CFTR functional data exists (mostly combination/calibration engineering); drops toward S as a VEP-only multi-predictor discordance flag if no CFTR MAVE-equivalent is on hand yet.

What to verify first

  • Whether a CFTR deep-mutational-scan or MAVE-style functional dataset with decent variant coverage actually exists, comparable to Findlay's BRCA1 assay — without it, the approach starts as VEP-vs-VEP discordance rather than MAVE-vs-VEP.
  • Whether any of the computational predictors Karen's group already uses were themselves trained or benchmarked against functional-assay labels, which would weaken the "independent evidence streams" assumption the Bayesian point system relies on (CPT-1 has this issue in the paper itself).
  • Whether the paper's flat 0.1 pathogenicity prior and ACMG point thresholds — calibrated for autosomal-dominant cancer genes — transfer at all to CFTR's autosomal-recessive inheritance and different baseline pathogenic-variant rate, or whether a recessive-specific recalibration would be needed instead.
